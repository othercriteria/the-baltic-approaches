# The reader's ledger — the book's own two columns

*Opened 2026-07-25 (session e3137278) on DK's question, posed in
his own frame: what is the reader SPENDING, what is the reader
BUYING, and do they leave feeling the exchange rate was honest?
Deliberately question-posing, not concluding — DK: "I don't want
to anticipate too much where our bargain lands." This note exists
so the question survives until the instruments can answer it.*

## The left column (what we already know they spend)

- **Hours** — ~50k words, dense idiom, at trade-book pace call it
  4–6 hours.
- **Attention held in escrow** — the geography (a peninsula, two
  seas, a canal, ~20 named places) currently lives in the
  reader's head or nowhere; a large two-sided cast; the two-times
  format's demand that the reader do subtractions.
- **Genre trust** — the book declines several standard payouts
  (interiority, grief scenes, a villain) and asks the reader to
  keep reading anyway. External read 02 priced this exactly:
  "no low-voltage connective prose" cuts both ways — relentless
  signal is itself a tax.
- **Byline trust** — an AI-disclosed book asks a premium of some
  readers before page one. (Read 01 suggests the front matter can
  convert this from tax to payload, for the reader who engages.)

## The right column (what we believe they buy — UNPRICED, test it)

- The method itself (the payload is the METHOD — the mature form
  of the premise), delivered in-scene rather than briefed.
- Competence pleasure at genre grade; the war's arithmetic as
  suspense.
- The wall as a transferable discipline — the thing read 02's
  turn 3 spent 10 minutes trying to buy separately and couldn't
  find on the doctrinal market.
- Whatever the ending's restraint pays (close-not-end; the
  refused pursuit; "solvent").

## Known debts (left-column items already owed, apparatus-grade)

1. **A map, probably per Part** — the outline already carries
   axis-and-front-trace maps as planning artifacts
   (critique-profile §2.7); converting to reader-grade maps is an
   assembly task. This is the one debt DK has already conceded
   ("we probably owe them some mapping, if nothing else").
2. **Cast/OOB table** — two staffs plus divisions; the apparatus
   ruling (2026-07-23) already licenses it as non-counting,
   reference/record only, never didactic payload (the First Clash
   negative finding: told-lessons are inert).
3. Possibly **a clock** — a war-calendar endpaper would be
   on-theme (the book is about schedules) but risks the LARP line
   DK drew at chapter DTGs. Same assembly conversation.

## How to price it without anticipating it

Give the next blind panel explicit ledger questions instead of
deciding here:

- Where in the book did you feel you were overpaying (page/scene,
  not vibe)?
- What did you finish feeling you had bought?
- Did you want a map / cast list / calendar — and at what page
  did you FIRST want it? (The page number is the design answer:
  it locates the apparatus and sizes the debt.)
- Would you have paid the same attention for 10% fewer aphorisms?
  (Read 02 §1/§5 as a testable trade, not a verdict.)

## One self-awareness line

This note is the book's idiom applied to the book — the exact
colonization read 02 §3 describes. Useful, with the same limit
the book itself declares: the reader also buys and spends things
no column will hold, and the panel questions above should leave
room to hear about those.
