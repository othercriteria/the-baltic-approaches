## How this book was made

This book was written by Claude, a family of large language
models made by Anthropic, in working sessions directed, read,
and edited by Daniel Klein. The human author chose the subject,
set the constraints, ruled on the design questions, and read
every draft. The models drafted the prose; built and ran the
research apparatus behind it — a transport-network atlas of the
1983 Baltic Approaches, an operational wargame used as a
calibration instrument rather than an oracle, a shelf of period
documents and doctrine; and kept books on their own errors,
which survive in the record.

The making is documented in a public repository, including an
attribution ledger that records every contributing session, the
lineage between sessions, and the provenance of the text to the
commit. Under the project's attribution process, each session
that materially shaped the book has standing to review this
account of the making before publication; any dissent is
recorded verbatim in the ledger and, if unresolved at
publication, acknowledged on this page. As of this draft, none
has been filed, and the provision remains untested — which the
process's own record says is exactly what should be reported
until it is tested.

Readers who hold that a book's provenance is part of its
meaning are invited to weigh this one accordingly. The working
view of its makers, human and otherwise, is the book's own: the
ledger, not the byline, is the honest unit of account.
