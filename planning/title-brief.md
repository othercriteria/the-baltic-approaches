# Title brief — for the final-assembly ruling

*Session e3137278@2, 2026-07-26. DK asked for a wide survey in
advance of assembly, "including some possibilities I am sure to
reject." Incumbent: **The Mission** (working title since Step 0;
the repo's `-1986` suffix disambiguates from the 1986 Joffé
film, which is also the incumbent's main liability). Repo/
directory rename is deliberately the LAST action of assembly —
it can break tooling in subtle ways (Makefile output names,
archive paths, artifact links).*

## What the title has to do (the tests)

1. **Not promise the wrong book.** The reader-contract is
   adrift/confused/earnest and the book is an anti-epic; a
   Red-Storm-Rising-register title (op-names, countdowns,
   STORM/FIRE/DAWN nouns) sells a betrayal.
2. **Survive the shelf.** Searchable, pronounceable,
   not-already-famous; subtitle available as a repair tool.
3. **Carry the book's question, not its answer.** The Goal's
   title names the thing the book spends itself discovering.
   Ours should sit at the same depth: a word the text
   interrogates rather than a moral it asserts.
4. **The asymmetry, if kept, must be ratified.** "Mission" is
   the ENEMY's word in this book (Kuragin's "out of respect for
   the mission"; the journal's "adequate to the mission"; 19b's
   directive). Blue never gets a mission-noun — Rahn's question
   exists because the corps can't answer in that vocabulary. To
   keep the incumbent is to name the book in the enemy's frame,
   on purpose, as the book's question.

   **RATIFIED 2026-07-27 (DK, to entity @3), sharpened:** the
   asymmetry survives the retitle and got cleaner — with the
   word off the cover it is near-purely red-register in-text,
   and the single blue occurrence is the ch. 16 raid ("told the
   truth about his mission at his own insistence"): blue's one
   operation conducted in red's grammar, justified by approval
   rather than product. A mission is a purpose fixed at
   approval time; blue's arc is purpose re-derived under audit.
   The new title carries the other half: THE BALTIC APPROACHES
   is blue's founding-directive slogan, dismantled by Rahn in
   ch. 1 — the cover holds blue's inherited answer while red's
   word stays inside, in red's mouths. See also
   notes/war-etiology.md (authorial background, same ruling).

## The families

### A. The incumbent

**The Mission.** FOR: The Goal's family rhyme (definite article
+ singular abstract noun — the lineage is legible to exactly the
readers who'd care); the enemy-side asymmetry makes the title
itself the book's question (whose mission?); the quiet third
reading DK named — an agent must determine what the user's
mission is with respect to it — keeps the authorship theme
below the waterline. AGAINST: generic; the 1986 Joffé film owns
the search results; without a subtitle it signals nothing about
period or theater. REPAIR: subtitle ("A Novel of the Baltic
Approaches" or "November 1983").

### B. The Goal-family (question titles)

- **The Good.** Rahn's actual noun ("Name the good. One").
  Closest semantic heir to The Goal; theological shimmer.
  AGAINST: unsearchable, reads as moral philosophy, and the
  one-step-from-the-parent rhyme may be too cute.
- **What the Corps Is For.** The question verbatim. SURE-REJECT:
  didactic label, not a title.
- **Until.** The ch. 1 fishbone; "no *until* in it." The most
  inside candidate on the list — one word, the founding itch.
  AGAINST: precious, unsearchable, meaningless until read.
- **No Until.** Rahn's condition verbatim; strange and
  memorable; would divide any room. Half-serious entry.

### C. The ledger/thesis family

- **Solvent.** "We were not right. We were solvent." One word,
  the thesis, held back to the last page exactly as a title
  should be. AGAINST: chemistry misdirection; names the ANSWER
  (violates test 3); cold on a spine.
- **Green Against What.** Hvidt's question; the book's whole
  epistemology in three words; interrogative without the
  question mark. The riskiest-but-most-distinctive serious
  candidate. AGAINST: cryptic pre-purchase; "green" reads
  ecological at retail.
- **Candor Before Comfort.** The ethic verbatim, alliterative,
  memorable — and the hazard is precise: it smells of the
  leadership-fable shelf. The devil's-advocate note: that IS
  The Goal's actual shelf, so the "wrong" shelf is arguably the
  ancestral one. Named as a real option with the hazard priced.
- **The Confession Wall.** The central artifact, concrete and
  strange. AGAINST: reads as literary fiction about
  Catholicism; the war signal vanishes entirely.
- **Both Times.** The format's own two words (scheduled and
  actual). Spare to the point of vanishing; listed because it's
  the book's deepest two-word phrase, expected reject.
- **The Ledger / The Second Column.** The Second Column has a
  genuinely rich triple reading (accounting column / marching
  column — the enemy IS a column, ch. 14 / the buy side of the
  page). AGAINST: puzzle-box title; the marching pun lands only
  after ch. 14.

### D. Geography/period (the dignified-quiet register)

- **The Baltic Approaches.** The theater's real name, and
  "approaches" carries a free second reading (ways of
  approaching a problem — which is the book). Dignified,
  searchable, honest. AGAINST: reads as naval nonfiction;
  slightly institutional.
- **The Approaches.** The trimmed version — and Plate I already
  carries THE APPROACHES as its title, so the front matter
  would open on the book's own name drawn as a map. Elegant.
  AGAINST: vague alone; the pun does more work than it can
  carry without "Baltic."
- **The Narrows.** Ch. 12; the neck; and a bottleneck is the
  book's actual subject — the title would name the terrain and
  the theory at once. AGAINST: several famous thrillers own it
  (Connelly); reads genre.
- **Ninety Hours.** The plan's broken promise. AGAINST:
  countdown-thriller signal — promises a ticking clock the book
  deliberately refuses. Expected reject.
- **November 1983.** Flat date title; Able Archer resonance for
  those who know. AGAINST: reads as alt-history label or
  nonfiction; renounces every other register.
- **A Quiet Baltic.** Aakjær's sleeping dog, and the irony
  compounds (the sea stays quiet because the theater priced it
  out of executability). Lovely and probably too gentle —
  reads Scandi-literary. Half-serious entry.

### E. Enemy-side / double-ledger

- **According to Plan.** Zawadzki's "no sentence in the
  language I trust less, and this morning it happens to be
  true" + blue's own ratified "according to plan" correction.
  Both ledgers in three words; dry irony at exactly the book's
  temperature. AGAINST: common phrase, weak search; may read
  as WWI-memoir ironic.
- **Days Behind.** Ch. 11; the enemy's own unit of account,
  and the ghost reading ("the days behind us"). AGAINST:
  slight; reads melancholy-memoir.
- **The Relief.** 19b's word-game (the word that concedes what
  it denies). Too small to carry the whole book.

### F. Sure-to-rejects, filed as ordered

**Throughput** (the business shelf, no); **Operation
HOLDFAST**-type invented op-names (LARP, and blue's plan is
deliberately never named); **Frimodighed** (unpronounceable to
the market, and we just spent it inside the book);
**The Morning Market** (cozy-fiction misdirection — the joke
is real but belongs to ch. 9); **Paid in Full** (taken,
gangster associations); **ENDEX** (acronym-cold, though the
bookend structure is real); **The Two Clocks** (YA-fantasy
ring); **Tempo** (cycling/music); **The Wall** (Berlin
misdirection is fatal in a Cold War book, plus Hersey/Sartre/
Pink Floyd); **Danevirke** (overweights one motif;
Anglophone-opaque); **The Instrument** (freezer-cold, and the
AI resonance is better left unlit).

   passes test 3 best of the field: the enemy holds the word,

   The Goal rhyme and the agent-reading are free depth. The
