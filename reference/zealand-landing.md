research pass: Pałka (on the shelf, Polish General Staff archives),

## 1. Red reality

**The force was brigade-strength wearing division flags.** Polish
7th Landing Division, mid-80s: ~3,200 men, 40 T-55, 92 PT-76, 137
TOPAS APCs [F: Pałka fn.14]. 6th Airborne Division: ~3,500; it
dropped as a complete division exactly once in its history (1965,
on Soviet aircraft) [F: Pałka pp.100-101]. Soviet 336th Guards
Naval Infantry Brigade, Baltiysk: 1,519 peacetime [F: wiki/336th].
GDR: one amphib-trained regiment (MSR-29), moved inland 1980,
training tapering, none after 1987 [F: DIIS KK3 p.447]. The 1977
plan's full package — 6AD + 7LD + 15th Mechanized Division (on
mobilized merchant ships) — totals 20,747 men and 307 tanks
against ~28,000 defenders: **the attacker lands at 1:1.3 AGAINST
in personnel** [F: Pałka Table 4, p.117].

**Lift was the constraint, permanently.** 1964 staff finding:
Polish + promised Soviet ships lift one regiment of 7LD per wave;
merchant augmentation needs ~20 days' preparation, "precluding
surprise or blitzkrieg" [F: p.103]. 1969 landing-rate arithmetic:
~3,000 men ashore in 2 hours if all goes well; +2,000 by H+30;
second group complete at **H+100 hours** [F: p.109]. The 1977 plan
papers over the gap with a 92-ship combined armada (37 PL, 37 SU,
18 DDR) + 26 militarized merchants — Pałka: assumptions "so
inflated" they were unrealistic days into a war [F: p.112]. US IC
corroborates the ceiling from outside: Soviet Navy lift moves
one-half to three-quarters of its naval infantry in a single lift,
remainder in mobilized Ro-Ro [F: NIE 11-14-85 ¶188]. Ivan Rogov
was never Baltic-based (exercise visits only) and left for the
Pacific in Nov 1986 — FE scored the departure as −15% Soviet
Baltic amphib lift [F: DIIS KK3 p.446].

**Sequencing doctrine put the landing late.** Both sides agree:
air superiority → sea control → mine-clearing (days) → landing.
CIA 1989 (now ON THE SHELF, DK browser fetch 2026-07-22 —
cia-1989-wp-planning-operations-against-denmark.pdf): "would not
be planned to begin before late in the first week of the war"
[F: Summary, p. iii]. The held document adds precision the search
index lacked: the assessed scenario runs D3 front offensive →
Kolding-Vamdrup-Bramming line ~day 7 → **Zealand offensive begins
day 8** (6th Airborne drops near Haslev that morning; 7th
Sea-Landing assaults Køge and Fakse bays; 15th Mech on merchant
ships lands by end of day 8, bypasses Copenhagen toward
Helsingør) → complete ~day 13 if resistance is strong [F: pp.
1-2]. The airlift constraint is quantified: delivering the
essential elements of one airborne division to Denmark takes
**80-90 percent of the Soviets' TOTAL military air transport
force**, at substantial risk [F: p. 5] — the "windowless"
airborne leg is gated by a scarce theater asset. The failure
judgment is tri-conditional: absent air superiority OR sea
supremacy OR the successful land offensive northward (early
linkup), "Pact planners would believe that the assault landing
operation probably would fail" [F: p. 5]. And the 1987 force
table names the Soviet Zealand package Warsaw was never told
about: **6th Guards MRD (Northern Group — the division FE's
Saltnicken readout flagged), 7th Guards Airborne Division
(Baltic MD), 94th Guards MRD (GSFG)**, tripling the Soviet share
of the assault force 1978→1987 (14,000 → 38,000 men) while the
Polish share held flat [F: Table 1, p. 3]. Pałka's handover,
FE's exercise inference, and the CIA's OOB now triangulate. The
Polish plan itself: landing on D6-7 of the front offensive ≈ war
day 8-9, having slipped from D+2 (1961) to D+5+ precisely out of
respect for mines and mobilized defenders [F: Pałka p.106; DIIS
KK2 p.628]. Landing sites: only Køge Bugt and Fakse Bugt ever
appear; Kapitanets inspected both in 1978 and found neither well
suited [F: KK2 p.629].

**The conventional variant did not close.** The Polish staff's own
verdicts: 1964 air component "practically unfeasible"; 1969 the
operation "can only be contemplated under conditions of nuclear
war"; 1977 conventional variant leaves "only a slight advantage,"
feasibility "doubtful" [F: pp.102-114]. The 1977 plan allocates
**15 nuclear weapons, 2,095 kt aggregate**, first strike at
G−5.5h, to make the beaches assailable [F: Table 3, p.115]. Red's
own retrospectives are harsher: Kapitanets (Zapad-81) — clearly
insufficient minesweeping; the straits problem meant "an entire
state ... had to be blockaded, destroyed and forced out of the
war." Hoffmann (Volksmarine chief): against laid Danish/German
mine barriers a landing is "extremely complicated, if not
impossible." The GDR refused throughout the 1980s to raise naval
infantry for what it privately called suicide [F: DIIS KK3
pp.545-549].

**The mission migrated, dated precisely.** May 1985 Moscow
consultations → Polish plan of 29 Nov 1986: Zealand passes to the
Soviet 16th Army / United Baltic Fleet; 7LD becomes the 7th
Coastal Defence Brigade (2 Aug 1986) contributing landing teams to
a Soviet battle group; **Warsaw is given "no specific information"
about the Soviet package**. Poland's residual amphib task is
Bornholm (D2-3, ~2,840 defenders). The 1988-89 defense-first
revision was never signed [F: Pałka pp.119-122]. From ~1984 the
Eastern fleets' exercises turn defensive; the last joint landing
exercise (June 1986) was "a final play-through of old tasks that
could no longer be solved" [F: DIIS KK3 pp.543-546]. → This
couples the amphib story to the setting-year choice:
planning/setting-time.md criterion 6.

## 2. Blue perception — the baseline, by period

**FE (Danish military intelligence), via *Truslen mod Danmark*:**
- 1965: landing conditional on Central Front success, planning case
  D+2..D+10, main assault at Stevns; WP one-lift = ~5,000 men + 112
  tanks [F: DIIS KK2 pp.525-528].
- 1970s: the landing is now top-priority, "could not be launched
  early enough" — **exactly wrong**: red had already slipped it to
  D+5 out of respect for the defenses FE knew about. Expected
  shape: Fakse Bugt beachhead, seven battalion groups (3 SU / 2 PL
  / 2 DDR), 6-8,000 men in one lift, follow-on 1-2 Polish
  divisions in 1-4 days. Simultaneously Zealand is deleted from
  the coup/limited-war scenario (1974) and called "well defended"
  [F: KK2 pp.567-571].
- 1980s: softening — landing contingent on the tactical situation,
  explicitly NOT simultaneous with the Schleswig-Holstein attack;
  assault echelon "well under 10,000"; no GDR assault troops
  carried; lift = the Soviet NI regiment + half the Polish force;
  the Polish "divisions" recarried as brigades. From 1985: 11-12
  days' WP preparation judged most likely. Warning doctrine:
  political warning 48h-8 days; tactical, worst case none [F: KK3
  pp.431, 462-465; KK2 p.566]. **CITE PRECISION FLAG (2026-07-22,
  vol-3 mining pass):** the 48h-8d phrasing was NOT found verbatim
  in KK3 ch. 77 (pp. 425-467); what vol 3 quantifies is WP
  *preparation* time — bimodal ~1 week (fast) vs 11-14 days
  (deliberate), with 11-12 days named the WP optimum from 1985.
  The 48h-8d band's operative leg is KK2 p.566 — TO-VERIFY there
  before the W-sweep cites it as FE doctrine. Full treatment:
  reference/diis-findings.md §1.
- 1987 counter-swing: FE paper 526/87 RAISES the Jutland-axis
  threat (WP corridor to the North Sea) under US/Petersen
  influence, "from debatable premises" (DIIS) — worst case kept
  into the 1990s even as the amphib picture softened [F: KK3
  pp.463-464].

**US IC:** 1981 — Denmark is the Polish front's ultimate *ground*
objective, navy in support, conditional on sea/air superiority.
1983 — combined Baltic Fleet "would support amphibious assault
operations ... against Denmark and West Germany." 1985 — fullest
form: air superiority → sea control → "amphibious operations
against the Baltic straits," while flagging the lift ceiling and
NATO's improving denial (Baltic diesel subs, Danish-isles FACs, WP
mine-clearing deficiencies named). Register is
conditional-planning throughout — never a probability that the
landing would actually be attempted [F: NIE extracts].

**Documented perception-reality divergences** (DIIS ch. 80): blue
had the wrong lead nation (assumed Soviet-led/GDR-heavy Jutland;
actually a Polish front task to 1987), the wrong landing count
(one vs at least two), the wrong timing philosophy (1970s), missed
the mid-80s defensive inversion while *seeing every piece of it*
(GDR training stopped, Ivanov's lift admission, the 1988 defensive
turn — all noted, none allowed to restructure the picture, the
merchant-shipping worst case doing the work), and part of the
1980s Baltic anomaly surge FE scored as red was blue (submarine
incursions, NATO's own offensive exercise pattern) [F: KK3
pp.448-461, 537-554].

## 3. Blue's update channels

**Direct observation (the I&W chain, all documented FE practice):**
- Embarkation: assault battalions board at six points on the
  PL/GDR coast; rear echelons at Świnoujście/Rostock/Warnemünde;
  post-1983 nearly all embarkation moves to the GDR coast (shorter
  sea leg) [F: Pałka pp.112-113, 119].
- Escort/minesweeper massing is unconcealable: the Poles computed
  needing ~40 ASW boats, 4-6 destroyers, 8 missile ships, 20 MTBs,
  **50 minesweepers** [F: p.103-104].
- Merchant Ro-Ro mobilization: ~20 days' preparation — the
  long-lead, *costly* indicator [F: p.103].
- Pattern-of-life sensors: the Kriegers Flak/Trelleborg pickets
  (nationality swaps tracked as signal), AGI surges (normalized:
  "ordinary, WINTEX-level" during Able Archer 83), warship
  circumnavigations of Zealand, monthly recon-sortie counts,
  basing changes (Laage's naval Fitter-Ks = "increased possibility
  of air support for amphibious operations"), Bornholm SIGINT
  (Dueodde) [F: KK3 pp.443-454].
- Exercise readouts as calibration: FE reconstructed Saltnicken
  (Aug 1986) wave-by-wave — 7 Aist hovercraft first wave, Ivan
  Rogov fourth, two merchants — and drew OOB inferences (6th Gds
  MRD "had landing operations as an option") [F: KK3 p.446].

**Inference (the macro clock):** no landing before air superiority
+ sea control + days of visible mine-clearing → Zealand gets days
of general war as strategic warning even if beach/hour surprise
holds (Barański 1983: operational surprise "impossible"; only
time-and-place surprise remains) [F: Pałka p.119; CIA 1989]. And
one lever is blue's own: **the mine barriers**. Laid, the landing
is (Hoffmann) near-impossible — "if the operational preconditions
were not created, a landing would be suicide — this, however, was
not written down" [F: KK3 pp.465-466]. Blue's confidence should be
a function of its own barrier state, which is a political-release
decision — an Andon-cord-shaped input.

**The mine-barrier perception triangle (completed 2026-07-22 by
the Nordic Forces paper, cia-1984-nordic-forces-in-the-1980s.pdf):**
three intelligence pictures of one barrier. Red feared it
near-decisive (Hoffmann: "extremely complicated, if not
impossible"; Kapitanets's minesweeping verdict). Danish doctrine
leaned on it as pivotal (FE from 1986; mining "a decisive element
of their national defense" per CIA 1989 p. 5). And the US IC in
Nov 1984 judged the LAYING capacity hollow: "Denmark is
especially weak in an area where it should play a coequal role
with the West German Navy: minelaying. Only one of Denmark's four
minelaying-capable vessels is designed as a minelayer; two other
vessels are out of commission, and the fourth is a training
ship," plus an assessed skilled-personnel shortage [F: p. 13].
Civilian ferries/trawlers would be mobilized as auxiliary
minelayers. For the book: the barrier that deters the enemy may
be the barrier your own allies believe you cannot lay — and
nobody in any of the three headquarters knows which picture is
true.

**Bogason's own perception-vs-reality verdicts** [added
2026-07-22]: "In the 1980s the Danish Intelligence Office
considered the risk of an attack by the WAPA to be relatively
low"; "the Danes did not at any time see any imminent risk"
[Ch 18-19] — consistent with our FE picture. His revising nuance:
~1980 the feared case shifted to LIMITED aggression — a
below-NATO-threshold grab of "Bornholm or Lolland and Falster as
an element of political pressure against the Danish government"
[Ch 18] — a threat mode our model does not represent (a
sub-threshold island grab is neither feint nor full commit;
noted, not queued). Op HURRICANE detail [Ch 18]: time-on-target
coordinated HARPOON then EXOCET then KORMORAN strike to break an
invasion force's momentum; helicopter targeting was planned "but
neither navy had the equipment to do it until after the Cold
War"; the nuclear alternative (SEP BARD BELL (E),
SACEUR-authorized, outside Danish waters) tested Sept 1983.

**The asymmetry that matters most for us:** FE updated UP easily
and DOWN almost never — every downward datum was absorbed by the
worst-case merchant-hull construct. Blue's estimate is not a clean
Bayesian; it is a ratchet with an institutional floor. That is
documented behavior, and it is exactly what a red feint exploits
for free [F: KK3 pp.447-448, 452].

## 4. Consequences for the instrument

**v9 parameter audit against this research:**
- `zealand_landing_fails = true` — supported far beyond
  expectation *for the conventional case with barriers laid*:
  every party's own verdict (Polish staff, Kapitanets, Hoffmann,
  GDR, DIIS, Pałka). The honest refinement: failure is conditional
  on mine-barrier state and NATO air/sea denial surviving — and
  the historical plans closed the gap with ~15 nuclear weapons.
  **The landing-success branch is, in reality, the
  nuclear-release branch** — flag for the book (the amphib threat
  is where theater nuclear pressure enters the story), out of
  scope for the conventional instrument.
- `zealand_battle_days = 4` — reasonable (D-day assault + 100-hour
  second-group window).
- The sea-state window (amphib_close_min/max 10-17) understates
  the real clock: the landing already cannot come "before late in
  the first week" for sequencing reasons, independent of weather.
  Credibility should decay on BOTH clocks.

**v10 graded credibility — now designable from documents.** Blue's
G-2 estimate p(landing) should be driven by an indicator state
red partially controls:
- *Cheap signals* (red can manipulate at ~no cost): exercise
  tempo, picket/AGI posture, embarkation-area activity, amphib
  shipping concentration. These are the feint's instruments.
- *Costly signals* (hard to fake, long lead): merchant Ro-Ro
  mobilization (~20 days), minesweeper massing, the actual
  air-superiority and mine-clearing campaigns.
- *Blue-owned prior*: own mine-barrier state (political release).
  Bogason [Ch 13, 18] now supplies the ladder exactly: nine
  planned fields, 5,755 mines (1965 system); German-laid fields
  east of Grønsund/Fehmarn, Danish the rest incl. the Køge/Faxe
  anti-invasion fields; **release is a government decision**, but
  the navy loads and takes waiting positions at Counter Surprise
  Alert, lays CONTROLLED fields at Simple Alert, uncontrolled
  mines at invasion beaches, full defensive mining at Reinforced
  Alert — a graded, rehearsed sequence (ex. NORTHERN LIGHT 1967).
  And his verdict splits the CIA-1984 hollow-capacity judgment:
  "The navies had the capabilities for laying the minefields;
  however, the main issue... was political: would the governments
  decide to lay mines in time" [Ch 19] — while corroborating 1987
  material decay ("critical deficiencies within 'decisive'
  equipment like... mines"; replacement unaffordable) [Ch 18].
- *The documented ratchet*: blue updates up fast, down slowly
  (institutional floor) — asymmetric update rates are not a
  modeling cynicism, they are the FE record. This gives the feint
  its teeth mechanically and honestly.

**The commensurability decision (DK: keep surfaced, not forced).**
Three shapes, now all priceable from research:

- **(A) Theater-customer only** (v9 as-is + graded credibility):
  no landing units exist; claim/caution values stay dials.
  Cheapest; the dials are now at least research-informed (the
  historical insurance was real: the threat pinned roughly half
  the Danish field army east of the Great Belt for thirty years —
  KK2 pp.527-528).
- **(B) Full second front**: Zealand OOB exists (3 Danish brigades
  + territorials ≈ 28,000; the landing package per §1), landing
  combat resolved on-map. The research removes more arbitrariness
  than expected (correlation, rates, timings all documented), but
  it buys a front the protagonist never sees, plus naval/mine
  mechanics with no calibration bench. Real cost, low narrative
  yield.
- **(C) Hybrid — landing package as real units, battle abstract:**
  red's amphib package exists as OOB units in a theater pool
  (≈ 7LD's 3 regiments + 6AD + 336th NIB + [year-dependent] 15th
  MD or Soviet 16th Army elements — year-parameterized loading
  already supports the 1985/86 fork). Employments: commit (units
  transit; sea-denial attrition en route from barrier/denial
  state; abstract resolution against the documented correlation),
  hold-as-threat (pins blue insurance via credibility), or —
  where historically legitimate — release to the mainland battle.
  Honest caveat the research adds: **the landing specialists had
  little alternative employment** (7LD was light, amphib-specific;
  only 15th MD and Soviet follow-on convert cleanly to mainland
  CV), so the feint is cheap for red in reality too — the
  opportunity-cost side of commensurability is genuinely thin,
  and a model that shows the feint nearly free is being accurate,
  not lazy. What (C) buys over (A) is that *committing* becomes
  chargeable (red loses real units to a failed landing) and the
  follow-on release choice becomes real.

Lean, held loosely: (C) — but the deciding question for DK is
whether the book needs red's commit branch priced at all, given
that every historical actor concluded red would not commit
conventionally. If the answer is "the threat-in-being IS the
story," (A)+credibility suffices and (C) is machinery for a
branch the enemy himself had rejected.

## Addendum (2026-07-23): the 1977 exercise family + 1980 lift appendix (shelf batch 8, distilled)

Three Wilson Center/CWIHP items (IPN-BU originals, Russian, tr.
Gary Goldberg; proper PDFs in reference/pdf/, wc-* prefix; Opus
distillation this date) — two coherent snapshots, not four
documents: the **June–July 1977 Maritime Front family (VAL-77
explanatory note, doc ID 114599 + FALA-77 reconnaissance plan,
approved 28.06.77)** and the **January 1980 exercise set
(assault-landing formations, doc ID 114550 + NATO-OOB appendix
17 → see oob-verification.md addendum)**.

**VAL-77 (the between-SOYUZ-75-and-83 gap, filled):** Marshal
Kulikov controlling; Maritime Front = 14 divisions / 2,965 tanks /
76 launchers; main axis Wittenburg–Neumünster–Flensburg–[Aalborg],
Jutland by day 10–11; **amphibious op D4–D6, Straits islands by
day 6–7** (Fyn taken in a day; Zealand bridgehead 10–15 km);
second landing southern Norway (Tønsberg–Farsund) day 16–17;
front norm ~30 km/day; correlation cast at parity in divisions
with red armor edge 1:1.3 (Jutland sector red-favorable 1:2.8 in
tanks). **Nuclear handling is three-stage and the landing sits in
Stage 2 — conventional**: the exercise rehearses taking the
bridgehead and all of Fyn "using only conventional means," with
NATO cast as the nuclear initiator in Stage 3 (front allocation
278 weapons/8.7 MT + OBF 157, supplied only on D-day eve).
**Flagged tension with the 1977 plan-of-record above** (15 nuclear
weapons underwriting the beaches at G−5.5h): VAL-77 documents red
testing the un-nuked landing branch its own staff privately judged
doubtful — exercise scenario vs plan, not a contradiction, but it
shows the conventional branch being seriously rehearsed in 1977.

**FALA-77 (red's collection plan = an inventory of red's fears):**
main reconnaissance effort "concentrated on Zealand"; priority
targets = the fort belt (Stevns, Mosede, Dragør, [Kongelunds]),
**Fakse and Køge Bays** (confirming those as the only beaches in
the planning corpus), Copenhagen, 1st/2nd Zealand Brigades, blue
nuclear delivery (Nike-Hercules, I-HAWK, "the 650th battalion" =
RakArtBtl 650) and **"new nuclear minefields"** in the bays;
phases pre-op → D–D2 (crossing/bridgehead) → D3–D7, each
repeating the nuclear-reconnaissance sub-task; means = OSNAZ
regiments, the 6th Radio Recon Center's 48-post HF DF baseline
(Braniewo–Ustka–Dziwnów), GRU agent tasking, Il-28R/MiG-21R air
recon, ELINT ships. **Mirror-imaging from the collection side:**
red collects hardest against the threats red itself would pose
(nuclear beach-prep, naval infantry — it imagines a marine
regiment defending Zealand). **Bornholm is absent as a target in
1977** — staging/rear, not objective — cleanly dating the other
end of the documented 1986 migration to Poland's residual
Bornholm task.

**Jan-1980 lift appendix:** three naval assault formations,

ferry augmentation baked in — confirming the merchant-lift
