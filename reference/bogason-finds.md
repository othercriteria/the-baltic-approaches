## 1. The NATO graduated precautionary/alert measures — a mobilisation checklist for the pre-war chapters
*[Annotation 2026-07-26, RESOLVED 2026-07-27: designator
print-confirmed by DK — footnote reads exactly "MCM-102-68
(draft) 18-12-68. HEM." A draft MCM of Dec 1968, one year after
MC 67/2; the scrape did not garble it, the document is simply
undigitized. Verified lineage for the promulgated system
remains the MC 67 series (nato-alert-system.md).]*

**What (Ch15/pp.475–476, "What to do if tension arises?", from MCM-102-68 draft
1968).** Bogason reproduces NATO's graduated "Counter and Precautionary
Measures" list for AFNORTH's Northern Region — the actual menu a headquarters
steps through as a Soviet build-up is detected. Tier one (military vigilance,
still *discreet*): man the war HQ with skeleton staffs; progressively implement
emergency-communications plans; review mobilisation plans and alert procedures
and "make discreet preparations"; intensify intelligence collection; increase
duty personnel to cover essential positions; conduct readiness exercises. Tier
two (selected simple-alert measures): partial continuous manning of NATO HQ;
pre-stock/man/maintain airfields; **prepare host-nation support and logistics
for guest forces**; bring air defence to full strength; prepare defence of ports
and coastal waters; **increase measures to prevent sabotage**; make initial
"denial measures" preparations; establish naval control of shipping; **tighter
control of frontiers and refugee screening; intensify control of known/suspected
enemy agents and of civil airports and ports.**
**Why it matters.** This is a ready-made spine for the novel's tension/handover
chapters (`02-handover`, `03-ninety-hours`, `04-norms`). It gives a LANDJUT staff
concrete, period-authentic verbs — and, crucially, the built-in *tension the book
is about*: "make discreet preparations" vs. actually alerting; the political cost
of visible mobilisation. The refugee-screening / counter-agent / anti-sabotage
lines are a whole texture the drafts don't yet touch. A staff officer reading
down this list, deciding which measures he dares recommend, is a scene.
**Grade: CANON-TOUCHING** (bears directly on the pre-contact chapters' procedure).

## 2. The reinforcement dilemma made brutal — the Alford verdict and the 6th Field Force's hollow logistics
**What (Ch15/pp.490–491, "Reinforcements in the 1980s").** The UK grew openly
distrustful that Denmark was using NATO reinforcement plans as an *excuse to cut
its own defence*; Danish cuts "caused turmoil" in the British MoD. Bogason quotes
IISS deputy director Col. Jonathan Alford (1986 conference) to the effect that
Denmark assumes — "not wholly incorrect" — that the allies cannot afford to let
it be overrun, so it need do little; he closes that Danes have "a remarkable
capacity for self-delusion." Then the mechanism behind the British scruple: the
6th Field Force's Logistic Support Group sat at **cadre strength in peacetime**,
filled out only by reservist call-out and "Shadow Postings." A blunt internal
note: the regular combat brigade could not support itself before the reserves
were called out — deploying it before callout "would involve considerable risk."
Elsewhere the British line is flatter still: reinforcement "presupposes that
there is something to reinforce" (Ch18/p.596). GARBLED-adjacent force figures
appear (UKMF ~29,000 men / 45 tanks to Esbjerg in 11 days, a reduced 10,000-man /
15-tank / 36-aircraft Zealand option) — treat numbers as PURSUIT.
**Why it matters.** This is the historical rhyme for the novel's central engine —
the wall, the metrics that misread self-defence as decline, the theatre begging
up the chain. The reinforcement force the corps is counting on is itself a
fiction that "presupposes something to reinforce" and can't even feed itself
until a political callout no one wants to trigger. Pure staff-dialogue ore, and a
Jonah-grade paradox (the reinforcement that must be earned before it arrives).
**Grade: CANON-TOUCHING** (echoes the wall/reinforcement theme in `16-the-bank`,
`17-the-refusal`).

## 3. WP objective = seize the Kiel Canal by taking the bridges; NATO must demolish the crossings
**What (Ch12/pp.379–380 Polish plan; Ch6/pp.146–151 SH defence; Ch12/p.~378).**
Across WP planning as NATO read it, the operational *object* on the SH axis is the
Kiel Canal as the gate to the North Sea/Atlantic: the attacker "wanted to cross
the Kiel Canal by conquering the bridges," so the defence's job was to demolish
the crossings and hold the enemy at the canal long enough to mobilise Jutland.
The 1950s Danish/British scheme had a strengthened Danish Brigade prepare canal
defence and, with a British armoured regiment, "temporarily stop the enemy by
demolishing the Kiel Canal." A DA/GE friction seam: an early plan to put a third
of the corps *south* of the canal "did not please the German military top."
**Why it matters.** The drafts already live on a canal–Schlei position
(`07-the-roads` is "north of the canal"; `12-the-narrows` is headed CANAL–SCHLEI
POSITION). This supplies the *why* the enemy wants the canal (not terrain for its
own sake but the Atlantic exit) and a concrete bridge-demolition decision —
crossings blown, on a clock, as a load-bearing act. Confirms the front geometry;
could sharpen a demolition scene. The south-of-canal DA/GE dispute is a staff
argument in miniature.
**Grade: CANON-TOUCHING** (confirms/sharpens the canal-line drafts).

## 4. WINTEX-81 home-front injects — the friction of a mobilising society
**What (Ch18/pp.593–595, "Exercises," from the German WINTEX-CIMEX 81 injects).**
Bogason walks two days of the biennial NATO command-post exercise, quoting the
scripted incidents NATO chose to rehearse — i.e. what it *feared*. Selected:
civilian ships' crews (40% on the supply flotillas) refuse to muster and go
work-to-rule, crippling oil replenishment; many West German reservists ignore the
call-up, so units can't form; a captured spy passes cryptic radio-amateur traffic
("Everybody should come to KORINTH; ARES will bring ice cream, but where is
IKARUS?"); LINK-11 and all HF ship-shore radio jammed for eight hours; arson
aboard a frigate (petrol cans, soaked rags); civilians "increasingly tried to
block their own forces"; soldiers demoralised by the unknown fate of families in
overrun districts; enemy chemical use; air strikes crater airfields "not usable
for two to three days"; WAPA paratroopers at Flensburg force a naval-HQ
displacement to Karup.
**Why it matters.** This is a home-front-friction palette the drafts barely have.
The evacuation returns in `07-the-roads` already carry the families-in-the-path
theme; WINTEX-81 extends it to sabotage, refusal-to-muster, work-to-rule on the
logistics the throughput arithmetic depends on, and the reservist who doesn't
come. Each is a small, deniable, period-true way for the war to *cost* before the
enemy arrives — texture a thickening pass could plant.
**Grade: ENRICHMENT** (revision texture for the civil-military chapters).

## 5. NATO storage-in-Denmark logistics: storage-day norms, German depot demands, and the treasury-dodge clearing office
**What (Ch6/pp.152–154, "Logistics: NATO storage in Denmark").** SACEUR's 1950s
logistics analysis fixed how deep the theatre stocked: Danish army supplies held
**60 days on Zealand, 90 days (+30 for Zealand) in mid/north Jutland**; navy
stores decentralised out of Copenhagen toward Korsør and Frederikshavn; air
stores at airfields plus large north-Jutland depots — the German LOC running
through Jutland with Frederikshavn as main harbour. German pre-stock demands grew
over the negotiation (winter 1957–58: 20,000 t ammunition, 1,600 t equipment,
10,000 m³ fuel; later two 5,000-t ammo depots and equipment depots of 10k/25k/25k
tons). The political fix: rather than "German depots" on Danish soil, grant
SACEUR access and let him sub-grant the Germans; Danish personnel maintain them,
**reimbursed via a SACEUR "clearing office" so the money never touches the Danish
treasury.** (Same section confirms NEPS POL pipeline for airfields, 1955 — already
held.)
**Why it matters.** Gives the didactic-logistics payload real numbers to anchor
inventory arithmetic (days-of-supply by region), and a lovely bit of alliance
face-saving: an accounting mechanism invented so a politically impossible fact
(German stocks in Denmark) could be true without being visible. That is a
Goldratt-flavoured "the constraint is the policy, not the physics" beat.
**Grade: ENRICHMENT** (logistics arithmetic + one usable alliance-friction device).

## 6. The 926th TFG A-10s at Vandel — a concrete close-support asset for SH and Zealand
**What (Ch15/pp.486–487).** From 1982, Vandel airfield hosted the USAF Reserve's
926th Tactical Fighter Group (A-10 Thunderbolt II) as a deployment station — ~75%
reservists, flown in from Louisiana (10 h to the Azores, then 6.5 h, air-refuelled
by KC-130), providing "40 sorties per day" to the BALTAP defence during
exercises. Its A-10s were meant to kill armour on the Zealand beaches "and to
support NATO forces in Schleswig-Holstein." Earlier the Harrier of No.1(F) Sqn
(UKMF) deployed to Vandel from 1973, chosen for hide-sites and a 1,000-ft rough
strip usable after runway cratering.
**Why it matters.** The air-apportionment file governs the deep/close split;
this gives a specific *close-support* platform, its sortie rate, and its
reservist provenance — the kind of concrete asset the "market" bargaining
(`12-the-narrows`) trades in. That the CAS is a fly-in Reserve unit adds a
fragility beat: it isn't there until the political-logistical machine delivers it.
**Grade: ENRICHMENT** (concrete asset for the close-air arithmetic).

## 7. WP amphibious geometry driven by nuclear-avoidance — dispersion as doctrine
**What (Ch17/pp.517–519, German 1972 analysis of seven observed exercises).**
Soviet amphibious doctrine capped each landing task group at brigade size and
spaced groups so one nuclear strike could not catch two, yet close enough to keep
comms — so a division's landing needed a 15–20 km frontage, each battalion a
"box" up to ~6 km. Embarkation preferred *beaches*, widely spread, to conceal the
build-up and deny a mass nuclear target. The Germans presumed a large amphibious
assault would only come *after* a surprise attack had already gutted NATO's
forces. Detailed landing sequence given: beach-master + demolition teams first,
shore bombardment, a 12-mile "point of no return" line, mine-sweepers cutting
channels, amphibious tanks/IFVs as first wave ~1 mile out, second echelon ~4 h
later.
**Why it matters.** The Zealand-landing file exists; this adds the *reasoning* —
dispersion is not timidity but nuclear hygiene — which lets a red-side or
intelligence scene explain a "why so spread out?" observation correctly. The
"only after NATO is already gutted" premise dovetails with the novel's
failing-defence frame.
**Grade: ENRICHMENT** (sharpens the Zealand/amphibious material's logic).

## 8. The WP's own fiction: every exercise opens with NATO attacking first
**What (Ch10/pp.341–347; Ch18/pp.596–597).** In exercise after exercise NATO
watched (Saaremaa 1957; Swinoujscie 1958; the Polish plans) the WP script the
same opening: a NATO/"WHITE" strike east, halted, then a "BLACK" counter-offensive
that overruns westward — the political self-justification baked into the drill.
Danish intelligence read it straight across to the real world. WP planning also
assumed *NATO* would start any war (incl. nuclear) — the WP "no first use" meant
they reserved the right to fire the moment NATO looked to be preparing to. And
NATO noticed the WP rehearsed the Zealand landing on Saaremaa's Keskrana beach
*because it geographically resembles Køge Bay.*
**Why it matters.** For a novel committed to playing it straight and to the
"perception was reality" idea, this is the red side's interior honesty: they too
believed they were the defender. A red-POV or captured-document beat that shows
the enemy sincerely framing his westward attack as a counter-stroke would
complicate the antagonist without winking. The rehearse-on-a-lookalike-beach
detail is a gift for an intelligence scene.
**Grade: ENRICHMENT** (red-side complication; intelligence-texture).

## 9. Alliance friction as doctrine and hardware: the two navies couldn't share a picture
**What (Ch18/pp.569–571).** Denmark declined NATO's LINK-11 as too expensive and
built its own CEPLO/data-link (by 1986 tracking every WP unit in the Baltic via a
15-year "electronic footprint" database from seven shore stations); the Germans
used LINK-11, but the shared fallback was LINK-14 — teletype raw data only. Result
Bogason names plainly: technologies that served each navy but "did not promote
tactical cooperation." Layer on the CONMAROPS story (Ch18/pp.558–560): the Germans
pushed the 1981 maritime concept partly to win *budget* fights and expand their
operating area north — doctrine as a funding lever.
**Why it matters.** Concrete, unglamorous coalition friction: two allies who
can't merge their tactical pictures because of a 1970s procurement choice, and a
service using strategy documents to win money. Both are staff-room truths a
LANDJUT-level ensemble would carry, and both resist genre gloss.
**Grade: ENRICHMENT** (alliance-friction texture; light INSTRUMENT value on comms).

## 10. WP nuclear-exercise arithmetic and the desant substitution
**What (Ch18/pp.596–598).** From East-German archive studies Bogason cites:
in exercises the whole WP front held ~840 warheads, firing ~250 in a first strike;
a typical EG army segment used ~30–35. Any WP first strike came as two consecutive
salvos aimed first at NATO nuclear C2 and firing stations, the second hours later
against what recce showed had survived. Critically, the Soviets *also* exercised
hitting the same target set — command centres, firing stations, airfields, key
logistic points — with **purely conventional desant** (special forces airlifted
deep) as a *substitute* for nuclear strikes. Post-1985/Chernobyl the plans drift
defensive and shed the nuclear phase.
**Why it matters.** The nuclear-release file exists; this adds the counterforce
targeting logic and, more usefully for a conventional-war novel, the doctrine of
achieving nuclear-grade effects on rear C2/logistics *conventionally* — which is
precisely the deep-battle / follow-on-forces threat the didactic payload treats,
and a reason the corps rear is never safe even absent release.
**Grade: INSTRUMENT** (calibration for deep-strike/nuclear-threshold modelling).

## 11. Poland 1981 as a live intelligence-surge template
**What (Ch18/pp.560–561).** When Solidarity peaked and the December 1981 coup
came, the Danish navy surged Baltic surveillance on two fears the government
named: armed resistance to the junta, and a *refugee stream across the Baltic* if
it were crushed. Ships rotated patrols off the Polish coast by day and inside
Bornholm radar cover by night; the navy used the crisis to argue (in its report)
for keeping large ships.
**Why it matters.** A near-setting (1981) worked example of a headquarters
reading a *political* convulsion next door as an operational tasking — and of a
service quietly using a real crisis to win an internal budget argument. Both are
transposable to the novel's opening tension and to its bureaucratic-honesty theme.
**Grade: ENRICHMENT** (period texture for the pre-war intelligence picture).
