## 0. Corpus caveat (mine, not theirs)

The review corpus was built with a locale-collated shell glob and
had 19a before 19. **The Makefile is correct** (GNU make `$(sort)`
is C-locale); DK's PDF and all builds are fine. Discount the three
reviewers' ordering complaints as artifact — but note the editor,
believing the swap authorial, argued on merits that chronology is

## 2. THE CONVERGENCE — voice homogenization (all five)

Same finding in five vocabularies: student (the colonels blur),
genre ("Sorkin syndrome... a war between two faculty senates.
Real staffs contain at least one fool, and this book has none on
either side"), editor (the walk-on epigram contest; Lammers/
Kjeldsen converge; Hvidt drifts into Rahn's register by ch. 16),
hostile ("one dialect, the author's... intentions where its
people should be"), veteran (dissents in part — recognizes the
men as true; only Rahn "partly assembled"). **This is the #1
revision-campaign target**, subsuming the tic scrub. Editor's
prescription adopted as working plan: ration walk-on epigrams
(let two or three be inarticulate), re-differentiate Lammers
(engineer's bluntness) from Kjeldsen, one oracle per book.

## 5. DESIGN — for DK, not for unilateral action

1. **The HQ never displaces** (veteran's "one operational
   objection of weight"): a static corps main HQ politely spared
   for a month is "the book's greatest kindness to itself"; the
   wall surviving three displacements "is a better story, not a
   worse one." Largest open design question the panel produced.
2. **The Kiel Canal forcing happens between chapters** (veteran:
   "I wanted that night, and you owed it to me").
3. **The Danish-German fault line never speaks** (veteran): one
   ugly corridor moment; related, the Hvidt-as-COMLANDJUT licence
   should be felt aloud once by a German officer.
4. **The emotional ledger** (hostile's central indictment: "one
   calibrated pang per chapter, each promptly filed... a theodicy
   of process. Nothing in this war is lost that cannot be
   converted into tuition"; student's naive version: "sometimes I
   wished someone WOULD give a speech"). The book's designed
   answer is the Rylski strand — which the hostile conceded is
   where "the book's own faith is tested." DK question: does the
   blue side need one uncashable loss — a grief the wall cannot
   file — or is the discipline the point?
5. CENTAG plant (genre + editor): one more half-beat or cut.
   (Design intent was deliberate background; two independent
   flags say it reads as a dropped stitch.)
6. Rahn: one early human wrongness (editor), "partly assembled"
   (veteran) — both note ch. 17's refusal redeems him.
7. Bjelke/Kuragin symmetry "one notch too neat; roughen one"
   (editor).
8. Belt ferry lift under-molested by Baltic Fleet air (genre) —
   VERIFY tier; possibly one acknowledging line.

## 6. What survived (validations worth recording)

- The thesis is reconstructible by a distracted teenager: the
  student essay independently stated "every number green and
  still losing" and applied the two-times format to homework.
- The arithmetic survives adversarial checking: the genre
  reviewer ran mobilization hours, tonnage, throughput, the
  sea's schedule, twilight — one ding (the moon, now fixed).
- Geography/calendar "almost embarrassingly good" (veteran
  checked weekdays against a 1983 pocket diary). Atlas
  discipline paid in full.
- The Polish Front decision is the thing "no novel ever gets
  right" (veteran; genre: matches post-1991 documents).
- Ch. 1: "do not touch it" (editor). Ch. 12: best sustained
  chapter (editor). Ch. 17 folder scene: finest scene in the
  book (veteran), moral center (editor), "the one scene in which
  a human being confronts something no ledger can price"
  (hostile).
- The genre refusals work: combat-as-formats "hits harder than
  any autoloader porn" (genre); no-hardware-nouns priced as
  costing half the natural audience and worth it.
- The Goal ancestry was independently identified by genre and
  hostile reviewers — unprimed. Genre verdict: the trick works
  because "he charges the protagonist for the theory." Hostile
  verdict: "world war as a bake-off between management
  philosophies." Both true; the distance between them is the
  book's actual bet, and DK should read both paragraphs whole.
- New-this-session material passed the panel blind: the
  countermarch apology (hostile: "the format used, for once, as
  an apology"; veteran: "made an old man put the manuscript
  down") and the Feldjäger on the furniture (veteran: "books
  never know it") were both written in this session's expansion
  passes.
