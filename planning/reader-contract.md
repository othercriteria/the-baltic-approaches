# The deal with the reader

*Instituted 2026-07-20 from DK's framing, before Phase 1. This is
upstream of every structural choice, including protagonist echelon —
test candidate structures against it.*

## The contract

The didactic novel in The Goal's line is about — and also **for** —
someone who is:

1. **Adrift.** If someone were coming to save them, they wouldn't
   need it. No relief column, no reinforcing corps released from
   theater reserve, no staff visit that fixes things. Institutional
   attention is elsewhere or is itself the problem.
2. **Confused.** If they knew what to do, they'd do it. The failure
   is not of will or diligence — the protagonist's map of how their
   world works is wrong, and the instruments they've been issued
   (metrics, doctrine, reporting formats) actively feed the wrong
   map.
3. **Present, earnest, showing up.** If they were checked out, this
   would not be the right book for them. The contract makes no such
   assumption about their *comrades* — a believable staff includes
   the cynical, the burned-out, the careerist — but the protagonist
   keeps arriving, keeps trying the wrong thing sincerely before
   learning to try the right one.

The Goal's Alex Rogo is the type specimen: plant failing and marriage
failing (adrift — division HQ is measuring him, not helping him);
efficiencies improving while the plant dies (confused — the metrics
are the confusion); and he drives to the plant every day anyway
(earnest). The reader Goldratt wrote for was a Rogo, not a Goldratt.

## Consequences for this book

- **The mentor teaches thinking, not answers.** Jonah never tells
  Rogo what to do; he asks questions and leaves. A Jonah who solves
  the front violates clause 1 — he would be someone coming to save
  them. The protagonist must apply, misapply, and re-derive.
- **No anointed protagonist.** Not the general's brilliant protégé
  whose ideas the institution was waiting for. The insight must win
  on results inside the protagonist's own span of control, against
  indifference or friction from above.
- **The metrics must genuinely mislead on-page.** Body counts, loss
  ratios, terrain held, sortie counts — the period's attrition
  instruments are the "efficiencies" of this book. The reader should
  be able to feel why a competent, earnest officer believes them.
- **Victory is scoped to the protagonist's reach.** Rogo saves one
  plant, not the corporation. The counterstroke that closes our arc
  should be the protagonist's echelon succeeding — not the war
  turning. The war turning would be rescue.
- **Comrades get the full range.** The contract's generosity about
  comrades is a voice resource: the ensemble can hold the checked-out
  and the hostile without the book condescending to them.

## Where the contract points: theater selection

DK's observation, recorded as the working hypothesis: clauses 1 and 2
are easier to achieve honestly in **peripheral theaters**, where the
mismatch between legacy doctrine (Active Defense and its cousins) and
present conditions is either

- (a) not the object of focused top-down attention — the reforming
  energy of TRADOC/the central front debate simply hasn't arrived;
  nobody is coming (clause 1); or
- (b) unfixable *given* top-down oversight — the theater is
  micromanaged from a distant headquarters whose picture is wrong,
  so knowing better locally doesn't help (a crueler form of
  clause 2).

The central front (CENTAG/V Corps country) is where the doctrinal
renaissance was *happening* by 1986 — AirLand Battle was published,
resourced, and rehearsed there. A protagonist there who discovers
tempo-as-throughput is re-deriving what his institution already
printed. In a peripheral theater, the 1986 setting can honestly
contain a 1976-vintage doctrinal world — the mismatch is the terrain
the mentor arc needs. (Candidate theaters and the echelon question:
planning/protagonist-echelon.md.)

## A note on register

The contract also constrains tone: a book *for* the adrift-earnest
reader cannot sneer at earnestness (no Catch-22 irony as the base
register) and cannot promise rescue (no competence-porn where the
system works once the right man is heard). Restraint rules from
CLAUDE.md apply; the didactic register decision (seminar-overt vs
submerged) is still open in planning/premise.md and should be settled
against this contract.
