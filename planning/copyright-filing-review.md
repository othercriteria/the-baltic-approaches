### STRATEGY

**1. [KNOW-WHY] File. The trade is $65 + two paperbacks + some
hours against an outcome distribution where every branch is
acceptable.** Grant: a federal record of a compilation-layer
claim plus §412 remedies for that layer — thin but real, and a
genuinely novel record artifact. Narrowed grant (examiner
annotation under 37 CFR 201.7(c)(4)): same, smaller. Refusal: a
reasoned letter answering the exact question the project exists
to pose, at marginal cost of the fee. Correspondence purgatory:
hours, not dollars. There is no branch where filing leaves you
worse off than not filing, because of finding 2.

**2. [KNOW-WHY] The public ledger creates approximately zero
*marginal* adverse exposure, because LICENSE.md already made
every damaging admission the application could make.** §411(b)
(per *Unicolors*) requires knowing inaccuracy; this application
is the most-disclosed in the history of the register — the Note
hands the examiner the repo. Estoppel-wise: a future defendant
quoting the application ("text generated by AI, claim limited to
arrangement") gains nothing they couldn't quote from LICENSE.md's
"Copyright status, stated plainly" section, which you ratified
and published first. The disclosure ship sailed 2026-07-31,
deliberately. The application is downstream of it.

**3. [KNOW-WHY] A refusal is not a published, citable adverse
determination — unless you make it one.** Initial refusals and
examiner letters are correspondence, not published decisions;
only Review Board opinions (second reconsideration, $700 tier)
are published on copyright.gov. You control the escalation
ladder ($350 → $700 → APA suit) and can stop at any rung. The
project will presumably publish the letter itself, but that's
your record discipline, not a precedent machine.

**4. [KNOW-WHY] Claim scope is right, including the plates, and
the excluded/included pairing is coherent.** Excluding "map
plates" as material while claiming their
selection/coordination/arrangement is not a contradiction — it
is exactly the *Zarya* structure (images excluded, arrangement
claimed) and the 88 FR 16192 n.33 compilation logic: the AI
material is unprotected outside the compilation; the compilation
authorship is the claim. Your Author Created string tracks the
guidance's own template phrasing (88 FR 16193) with the
human-authored-content slot honestly empty — the *American
Cheese* pattern, which has been granted.

**5. [FIX-IF-CHEAP] The honest complication — arrangement itself
substantially AI-proposed, human-ratified — is disclosed by
reference (the repo link) but not by statement. Consider one
clause in the Note.** Something like: "Portions of the selection
and arrangement were themselves AI-proposed and ratified by the
applicant, as the repository documents." Tradeoff, priced: it
raises the probability of narrowing/refusal, but this project
has priced refusal as acceptable, and a grant obtained on facts
the examiner didn't engage is a *weaker* record artifact than
either a grant or a refusal on the true facts. It is also the
terminal inoculation against any future "fraud on the Office"
theory — though finding 2 says that theory was already
near-dead. If the experiment's integrity is the point, add it;
if maximizing grant probability is the point, the repo link
already discharges the disclosure duty. Know which one you're
optimizing.

**6. [NOISE] The eBook's "All rights reserved" line, KDP's
regime, the license posture.** A © notice is not a
representation of registration scope; §506(c) requires known
falsity and some rights do exist. Already correctly parked at
errata tier in copyright-ai.md. One adjacent check (costs five
minutes): confirm the KDP AI-content disclosure answers given at
publish align with the public record — that's an Amazon-account
consistency exposure, not a USCO one, and the record is
eminently discoverable.

### MECHANICS

**7. [FIX-IF-CHEAP] The Note's claim sentence contradicts the
claim fields.** The brief's Note text says the claim is limited
to "selection, coordination, arrangement, **and editorial
direction**." You deliberately dropped "editorial direction"
from Author Created and New Material Included because directing
is not authoring (the brief's own §2 reasoning, and Part 2's
commissioned-artist analysis agrees). The three claim fields
tell one story; the Note tells a slightly wider one. An examiner
reconciles field-vs-note discrepancies by correspondence — you'd
be buying a letter about your own wording. Trim the Note to
"…limited to the applicant's selection, coordination, and
arrangement" (mention direction as *description* of the process
if you like, just not as claim scope).

**8. [FIX-IF-CHEAP] Verify the Note field actually stored in
full.** The review screen shows it clipped in a scroll box. You
verified the two Limitation "Other" fields store full strings
behind their 40-char displays; apply the same verification to
the Note before submitting, since it's your §411(b) inoculation
and your pre-answer to the examiner. Scroll it, screenshot it.

**9. [KNOW-WHY] 07/30/2026 is a defensible publication date, but
know the two soft spots.** (a) KDP "Submitted on July 30" is the
submission event; the live-on-sale date (the §101 publication
event — copies offered to the public) may be 07/30 or a day
later. (b) More interestingly: the repo went public 2026-07-26
with the near-final text, LICENSE-authorized for cloning and
building — under Compendium online-publication practice,
authorized public download is publication. Both cut in different
directions by a day or four; neither approaches §411(b)
materiality (good-faith date judgments on murky facts are the
norm, and *Unicolors* forgives good-faith legal mistakes). The
one operational consequence: if first publication was arguably
07-26/27, the §412 window arguably closes **~2026-10-26, not
10-30**. Your plan targets mid-August receipt; treat
end-of-September as the real drop-dead and the ambiguity costs
nothing.

**10. [KNOW-WHY] Byline vs. author field: the deposit's title
page says "Daniel Klein with Claude"; the application names one
human author.** This is per the guidance (do not list AI as
author, 88 FR 16193), and the Note explains it — but expect the
examiner to notice the title page and possibly ask. The answer
is already in the Note. This is the correspondence you should
*want*.

**11. [KNOW-WHY] Deposit edition: corrected-barcode second
printing against a 07/30 first publication.** §408(b)(2) wants
two complete copies of the best edition; the LoC best-edition
hierarchy prefers print over electronic (paperback choice
correct, ISBN choice correct — matches the deposit in hand).
Text identical, cover barcode corrected: no examiner compares
printings. Nobody will ever care.

**12. [KNOW-WHY] Certification is clean under §506(e).** Every
statement on the screen is true, and the false-statement clause
covers the Note too — which, as the brief notes, is a feature
for this filing. The Form TX don't-sign-before-publication rule
is satisfied (07/30 < 08/02). Year Created 2026: correct
(draft-final 07-27). No-checkbox included side: validator
accepted it, and it's the correct entry — checking "Text" would
assert the one claim this application exists not to make.

**13. [FIX-IF-CHEAP → KNOW-WHY] PII in the public catalog:
claimant address is mandatory and will publish; birth year and
the Rights & Permissions block are optional and will also
publish.** The R&P section exists specifically to be a *public
licensing contact* — it currently mirrors home address +
personal cell + gmail. Birth year is an optional disambiguator.
Decide on the phone/R&P block with the same deliberateness. Two
minutes now versus a permanent catalog record (extraneous-PII
removal procedures exist but are narrow).

### PROCESS

**14. [KNOW-WHY] The effective-date plumbing is correctly
understood; execute it with receipts.** Effective date = Office
receipt of the last element (the mailed deposit), DC offsite
mail screening adds days-to-weeks, §412 window per finding 9. Do
now / at submission: (a) save the full application PDF including
the *scrolled* Note, the payment confirmation, and the case
number in the filing log; (b) print the shipping slip twice, one
**inside** the package — a deposit that arrives divorced from
its slip is an orphan in a warehouse; (c) photograph the two
copies + slip in the box; (d) track with signature; (e)
calendar: mail date, T+3 weeks "verify eCO shows deposit
received, else call (202) 707-3000," 2026-09-30 re-mail
fallback, 2026-10-26 conservative window close; (f) whitelist
@copyright.gov / @loc.gov — examiner letters carry ~45-day
response deadlines and a missed one closes the case with the fee
spent.

**15. [KNOW-WHY] When the examiner letter comes — and for an AI
claim, plan on it — answer narrowly from the Note and the
ledger.** Confirm the extent of AI generation, describe the
human contribution, cite the repo; do not attach the ledger
wholesale, do not advocate doctrine at the examiner, and
remember every response is a written statement in connection
with the application (§506(e) again). The correspondence *is*
the experiment's yield; keep it as clean as the filing.

### UNKNOWN UNKNOWNS

**16. [KNOW-WHY] The fee NPRM is a real clock.** March 2026 NPRM
proposes ~43% average increases; the $65/$350/$700 ladder is
locked per-action at filing time, not per-project — a
reconsideration filed after a final rule pays the new fee. One
more reason not to drift.

**17. [NOISE] Country fields blank on all three address blocks;
"Literary Work" class covering the pictorial plates.** eCO
treats blank country as US-default with a US state present;
TX-class registrations routinely embrace incidental pictorial
matter. Neither will draw a glance.

**18. [KNOW-WHY] The §407 mandatory-deposit obligation is
discharged by this same package** — which means *not* filing
would leave a live (if rarely enforced) two-copy LoC deposit
obligation for a published US work anyway. The registration
makes the deposit do double duty. The true marginal cost of
filing over not-filing is roughly $65.
