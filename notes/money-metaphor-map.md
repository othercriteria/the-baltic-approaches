## Headline findings (for the surgeon in a hurry)

1. **No INCORRECT economic uses.** Zero emergencies. The system is
   scrupulous end to end: cost = opportunity cost (what you give up), price
   = the act of quantifying cost, stock vs flow held correctly (ch 14),
   compounding correct (ch 2/7/11), credit/interest/solvency all deployed
   in true sense. The book even **polices its own economics on-page**
   (ch 5, Aakjær strikes "terrain" from the buy-column: "terrain is not a
   good, it is a place where goods happen").
2. **The reviewer's charge is half right.** "After chapter ten the metaphor
   stops doing work" — but the post-10 density is ~90% **load-bearing**:
   payoffs of early plants, character idiom, and wall/document register. The
   genuinely mechanical residue (author's-hand narration reaching for a
   money-word where a plain word serves) is **thin** — a handful of reflexive
   verbs and two or three decorative similes, plus one didactic-seam line.
   The felt "tic" is a **cadence** effect (every decision reflexively paired
   with a money-verb), not a mass of wrong or empty uses. The correct
   remedy is a light thinning to restore freshness, **not** a scrub.
3. **Effectively taught: core wedge by ch 5–6; full system by ch 10.** The
   reviewer's ch-10 line is right as the *completion* point (the bank +
   his-ledger + interest install there). Trimming pressure legitimately
   begins **ch 11**.

## 3. Load-bearing vs mechanical — by category, with locators

### (a) TEACHING — first / deepening statement of a concept (protect)
- Ch 1: Brauer "what it bought / what it cost"; Rahn "Buys… a commercial word".
- Ch 4: Kjeldsen "worth a third of its face"; Hvidt "rate of exchange… what an hour is worth".
- Ch 5: the whole ledger build — "buy column of a corps war ledger", "spent numbers and bought addresses"; Rahn "consumption is countable, production requires you to have decided what you are for".
- Ch 10: "credit is trust with a date on it"; HIS NORM/ACTUAL/PATIENCE.
- Ch 14: Lammers stock/flow ("cannot attack out of the flow… must save up").

### (b) PAYOFF — a taught concept detonating at a hinge (protect absolutely)
- **The ferry-minute arc** — the single most important plant→payoff in the book:
  ch 2 "correctly priced, and quietly compounding… at rates the file could not
  print" → **ch 15 "The dividend crossed the Great Belt"** + "his price made
  flesh" + "the two days had cost what Lammers' price had said".
- **The bank arc** — ch 10 "batteries would bank one-third" / "spending my
  soldiers on your credit" → **ch 12 "The bank went first"**, "watching his own
  signature convert", "a man attends the spending of his own estate" →
  ch 16 (title) → ch 18 "read it like a bank statement".
- **"Bought at full price and never audited"** — ch 1 Kjeldsen → ch 9 payoff (same phrase returns as the deep-program lag bites).
- **Compounding** — ch 2/7/11 → **ch 18 wall-text "COMPOUND INTEREST, EXACTLY WHAT EVERYTHING COSTS. IN THE MORNING WE WILL FIND OUT WHAT IT BUYS."**
- **Sleep = ammunition** — ch 14 "sleep had entered the corps' books as ammunition" → ch 16/18 payoffs.
- Ch 19: "the refused dates went downrange all morning… paid with interest";
  "the argument paid its own bill first, and the bill arrived on the western
  shoulder"; "the quiet was the kind that holds receipts".
- **Solvency** — ch 20 "We were not right. We were solvent" / "Solvent is a
  military category" / "Paid in full."

### (c) CHARACTER — a speaker's own idiom (diegetic; protect)
Each principal has a *distinct* economic register — this is characterization,
not authorial tic:
- **Lammers prices and invoices** — "priced its absence" (ch 2), "reading a competitor's invoice" (ch 14), runs the mirror-ledger.
- **Kjeldsen converts to his air trade and counts change** — "worth a third of its face" (ch 4), "no one has ever audited it" (ch 9), "customers who count their change" (ch 9), "days added to his preparation" (ch 15).
- **Aakjær reads *his* ledger** — "I am describing his ledger, not his soul" (ch 6), "spending it well… the largest number on no board we own" (ch 12).
- **Hvidt buys the criticism and asks for the invoice** — literally: ch 9 "bought the criticism whole and asked for the invoice"; "Note the rate of interest. Execute." (ch 12).
- **Rahn teaches** (consumption/production, creditors/foreclosure); **the Kolding banker** line (ch 10, returns ch 12); **Kuragin**: "Confidence is a currency too" (ch 17).
- **The captain's log / the elderly Oberstleutnant** invent currency-conversion at the bridge (ch 7) — diegetic, the format then "spreads through the headquarters on its own authority".

### (d) MECHANICAL — narration reaching for the money-word out of habit (the trim candidates)
See §4. Short list; each individually defensible, targeted for **cadence**.

### (e) INCORRECT — wrong economics
**None found.** See §1 headline. Checked specifically for cost/price
conflation, stock/flow confusion, and sunk-cost misuse — all clean. Ch 6's
"The care is capital… slow to abandon capital" is *not* a sunk-cost fallacy: it
is framed forward-looking ("restraint is rented… holds as long as his ledger
says the shop can still be had whole") = option value of the intact port.
Correct.

## 4. THE TRIM LIST (ranked; narration only; plain-word alternative given)

Concentrated on chs 11–16 per the charge, swept elsewhere. **This list is
short by design** — the text is disciplined; over-cutting will damage payoffs
(see §DO-NOT-TOUCH). Rank 1 = cut/replace with confidence; Rank 3 = only if
deliberately thinning cadence.

**RANK 1 — pure decoration, plain word loses nothing**

1. **Ch 14 (14-the-cathedral-rebuilt), "the corps declined to remain on
   deposit."** The his-economy point is already carried by the prior clause
   ("the enemy held the address and was saving it"); "on deposit" re-says it in
   money. *Alt:* "and the corps declined to be there when he spent it." (or cut
   the clause).
2. **Ch 11 (11-days-behind, l.134), "a supply sergeant who used it the way men
   use payday."** A money-textured simile attached to no concept. *Alt:* "the
   way men speak of a promised holiday", or cut the simile (the lieutenant's
   "bitterly" and the driver's ignorance already triangulate the register).
3. **Ch 15 (15-sequencing, l.89), "kept himself for the rates of exchange."**
   Third money-noun in two sentences ("market" / "currency" / "rates of
   exchange") — the cluster is what reads as tic. *Alt:* "kept himself for the
   hard conversions" / "for the trades that mattered."

**RANK 2 — weakest of the defensible uses; thin to let neighbors ring**

4. **Ch 16 (16-the-bank, l.26), "in the wall's currency, patiently, like a
   catechism."** The quoted catechism line that follows *is* the currency;
   "in the wall's currency" pre-labels it. *Alt:* cut "in the wall's currency,".
5. **Ch 12 (12-the-narrows), "each one was a spending decision, and the
   currencies were the ones the wall had taught them all to see."** The trailing
   clause is a **didactic seam** — it tells the reader a mapping they already
   own (the "PowerPoint-in-prose" flag). *Alt:* stop at "each one was a spending
   decision." The concrete list that follows (reserve / bank / line) does the
   teaching by showing.
6. **Ch 9 (09-the-morning-market, l.~197), "A man whose theory has printed
   money can be forgiven the face Holt wore."** *Counter-brief:* "printed money"
   carries a **counterfeit** note the chapter's reversal needs (the money turns
   out fake when the front doesn't notice). Editor's call — if kept, keep it
   *because* of the counterfeit reading, not the money color. *Alt (if cut):*
   "A man whose theory has just been proved…".

**RANK 3 — individually fine; touch only in an aggressive cadence pass**

7. Ch 15 (l.118) "the currency did the arguing" — sets up days-as-currency; keep unless thinning.
8. Ch 12 (l.251) "paying its dividend nine hours late" — earned (the 0430 re-arm gamble); low.
9. Ch 14 (l.216) "the enemy acted, the corps priced the act, the corps answered" — this is a *summary of the taught loop*; borderline didactic but it also marks the pivot from response to intent. Keep unless the §5 didactic concern wins.

## 5. Baroque-early cases (elaboration before the system is taught)

The brief anticipated pure decoration in the early chapters. **On the page
this problem is essentially absent** — the early elaboration is almost entirely
high value-of-information (it *is* the teaching), which is the license the
governing constraint grants.

- **Ch 2, the ferry minute** — "a small gray decision about ferries, correctly
  made, correctly priced, and quietly compounding from that day at rates the
  file could not print." This is the most baroque early passage. **Verdict:
  earned.** It installs *compounding* and plants the ch-15 dividend; the
  elaboration focuses attention on the exact mechanism (deferred requisition →
  compounding hours) the reader will need. A model of baroque-but-justified.
- **Ch 2, "priced twice: once in readiness bought, once in political noise
  made… run strictly in order of the second price"** — installs the two-price
  idea and the whole "irresponsible about nothing except headlines" ethic.
  Earned.
- **Ch 1, Kjeldsen "bought it at full price"** — could read as early flourish,
  but it plants the phrase that pays off in ch 9. Keep.

**Correction to the brief's expectation:** the mechanical-drift risk is **not**
early over-decoration; it is a **mid-book cadence** — the reflexive pairing of
every decision with a money-verb from ~ch 11 on. That is where §4 applies, and
it wants a scalpel (≈3 sure cuts, ≈3 more if thinning), not a scrub.
