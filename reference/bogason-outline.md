## 1. Front matter — thesis, method, sources, periodization

**Thesis.** The book reconstructs how NATO planned to defend the Baltic
Approaches — the narrow straits (Øresund, Storebælt, Lillebælt; with Kattegat/
Skagerrak to the north) linking the Baltic to the North Sea — across the whole
Cold War. Its subtitle, *When Perception was Reality*, signals the argument:
planning was driven by how commanders **perceived** the Soviet threat and each
other's intentions, and those perceptions became the operative reality that
shaped organisation, tactics and hardware. The recurring strategic axiom is
Denmark as "the cork in the bottle" — whoever wants to move submarines and
surface forces between the Baltic and the Atlantic must control Danish territory,
airfields and the straits.

**Scope / focus.** Bogason states plainly in the Foreword that the two navies
(Danish and West German) had the core roles and are his main focus; army and air
force are included "mainly in a skeleton version." Littoral warfare — as opposed
to blue-water — is the organising concept. Forces are overwhelmingly Danish and
West German, with declining British and (Southern) Norwegian participation.

**Method / source base.** Narrative built from **declassified national-archive
documents**, explicitly *not* the still-classified BALTAP/NATO archives (Bogason
warns declassification would take years). Principal cited repositories:
- **SHAPE** documents (by far the most-cited series; declassified NATO/SHAPE papers).
- **Rigsarkivet** (Danish National Archives) — Danish navy/C-in-C papers, intel reports.
- **Bundeswehr-Archiv, Freiburg** and **ZMSBW** — West German navy/army material.
- **The National Archives, Kew** (British: JCS/COS critiques, Live Oak).
- **Forsvarsgalleriet** and **Weyers Flottentaschenbuch** — ship photos/data.

**Periodization.** Chronological, hung on NATO strategic doctrine and decade
crises: MC 14 → MC 14/1 (1950s) → MC 14/2 massive retaliation → MC 14/3 flexible
response (mid-1960s on); reinforced by the 1961 Berlin crisis, 1962 Cuba, 1968
Czechoslovakia, 1970s missile modernisation, and 1980s CONMAROPS. Three declared
levels of analysis: grand strategy, operational art, tactics.

## 2. Chapter map (19 chapters; running total ~602 pp of narrative + Sources)

**Ch 1 — Introduction (pp. 1–19).** Frames the study, the "narrow sea exits"
geography, littoral vs blue-water warfare, military sufficiency and
reinforcement, and the three levels of analysis. Walks the MC 14 / 14/1 / 14/2 /
14/3 strategy sequence as background. *Figs:* 1.1 Baltic Approaches base map;
1.2 the three NATO defence lines (Rhine/Ijssel → Weser/Lech → forward).

**Ch 2 — Initial ideas for defending the Baltic Approaches (pp. 20–40).**
1948–49 Scandinavian Defence Alliance and its collapse; early Danish strategic
thinking borrowed from German (Admiral Heye) ideas; 1949–50 NATO organisation
and first strategy; Sweden's defence and possible covert cooperation.
*Named:* Heye memoranda; DC-13. *Figs:* 2.1 possible minefields; 2.2 Heye
threat/counter-move map; 2.3 NATO org June 1950; 2.4 Danish–Swedish Sound note.

**Ch 3 — NATO's military organisation and initial plans (pp. 41–75).** Build-out
of AFNORTH/AFCENT/Atlantic commands; making DC-13 operational; Standing Group's
Soviet-threat picture (SG 176); first AFNORTH defence plan (land/sea/air); a
sharp British JCS critique and a British plan for **nuclear** attack on Baltic
and Northern targets; exercise **MAINBRACE (1952)** to test flank strategy.
*Figs:* 3.1–3.9 (command org charts, SG 176 Soviet-attack maps, Baltic target
map). *Table 3.1:* Mainbrace naval forces (**GARBLED numbers**).

**Ch 4 — NATO in control: Annual Review and intelligence reporting (pp. 76–96).**
The Annual Review and Combat Effectiveness Reports as NATO's instruments of
information/control; SG 161 intelligence updates. Denmark and West Germany
assessed. *Tables 4.1–4.3:* force build-up 1952–54 / 1956–57 and Danish military
spending (**all GARBLED — numeric cells empty**).

**Ch 5 — Denmark almost alone (pp. 97–138).** The weak state of Danish defence
1949–50 and rearmament plans for army/navy/air; 1955 shift to "attack at source"
against Baltic bases/airfields; navy–air-force and Danish–Norwegian–British naval
cooperation; **Exercise BROWN JUG (1957)**; integrating Danish defence into NATO;
the 1955–60 budget squeeze. *Figs:* 5.1–5.8 (Danish navy 1952; planned
minefields 1950; D-1 / D-day dispositions and mine-laying; Orange/Red landing
exercises on Zealand). *Tables 5.1–5.2* (ship requirements — **GARBLED**).

**Ch 6 — NATO Policy and Danish military issues in the 1950s (pp. 139–182).**
*(High novel value.)* Soviet exploitation of the Bornholm agreement; **the
territorial defence of Schleswig-Holstein 1949–62** and the emerging
LANDJUT/COMLANDENMARK command relationship (Rendsburg appears here); the **NEPS
pipeline** for Danish airfields (from 1955, later extended toward Germany); radar
coverage and fighter-interception geometry (Karup and Aalborg aircraft);
pre-positioned supply/storage negotiations with West Germany; offensive
operations into the Baltic. **Exercise HOLD FAST**. *Figs:* 6.1 Bornholm exercise
area; 6.2 HOLD FAST area (German 6th Div SE of Kiel Canal, Danish 3rd Div NW);
6.3 NEPS pipeline map; 6.4–6.6 radar/interception lines 1954/1957/1959.

**Ch 7 — NATO's New Approach and the Danish "No" to nuclear weapons: A Janus
Head? (pp. 183–226).** Tactical-nuclear primer; **MC 48** "New Approach" and its
objectives/campaigns; Danish general strategy under it; control of warheads;
Danish political "no" hardening then softening to "maybe"; **MC 70** and the
economics; consequences for the Approaches. **Exercise IRON OUT** (a nuclear-
release exercise order). *Figs:* 7.1 attack/defence schematic (Gen. Kragh
lecture); 7.2 possible nuclear target areas in Denmark 1955; 7.3 NIKE batteries
ringing Copenhagen; 7.4 IRON OUT draft order (50-kt bomb near Rødvig).
*Tables 7.1–7.3:* army/navy/air force (**GARBLED**).

**Ch 8 — West Germany's rearmament (pp. 227–280).** *(High hardware value.)*
Bundesheer, Luftwaffe and Bundesmarine stand-up; **Admiral Ruge's forward-defence
concept** in the Approaches; destroyer/frigate/FPB, submarine, mine and
amphibious force design; emerging ship-to-ship missile threat; German-controlled
nuclear platforms; exercises. Densest hardware chapter — 21 figures.
*Figs:* 8.1 army divisions N. Germany 1959; 8.2–8.3 infrastructure/airfield
target maps E. Europe; 8.4–8.14 ship classes (Fletcher, Hamburg, Zobel FPB,
Type 205 sub, Lindau MCM, Eidechse amphibious); 8.5/8.11/8.18 navy-station and

convoy-attack geometry; **8.21 "Schleswig-Holstein as an aircraft carrier"**.
*Tables 8.1–8.5:* army/air OOB, navy targets, German mine-laying plan (**GARBLED**).

**Ch 9 — The Creation of Baltap (pp. 281–327).** Eight years of "bureaucratic and
symbolic politics" producing BALTAP: border discussions; naval-integration
debate; integrated naval command; the **AFNORTH-vs-AFCENT fight over control of
naval aircraft**; SACEUR's intervention; the public/political solution and final
negotiations (~1961–62); Soviet reaction. *Figs:* 9.1 COMNAVNORCENT org; 9.2
Ruge's all-waters-to-Norway proposal; **9.3 BALTAP organisation 1962**.

**Ch 10 — The Warsaw Pact and its Tactics, as seen by NATO (pp. 328–351).**

**Ch 14 — Towards flexible response and new NATO bodies (pp. ~440–473).** Shift
