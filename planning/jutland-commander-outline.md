# The Mission — division version: one-page outline
*Bake-off challenger. Protagonist: General Officer Commanding, Jutland Division. Filed 2026-07-22 to sit beside the Rendsburg G-3 conception; DK rules.*

**Protagonist.** Major General Erik Vestergaard, GOC Jutland Division — three mechanized brigades, the Danish field army's mainland half. A tank officer of the mobilization army: earnest, competent, loved by his staff, and issued a wrong map. His instruments — readiness percentages, meters of obstacle dug, kilometers held, days of delay purchased — are the peacetime definition of a good division, and he believes them the way Rogo believed efficiencies. Unlike the G-3, he is Rogo *with hands*: march tables, engineer priorities, demolition triggers, and brigade reserves move on his word, nobody's permission required; the corps above exists to threaten, not to approve. His arc is The Goal's: he learns his division is a flow system whose goal is not ground denied but an *hour created* — the hour when the enemy stands spent and unsupported — and that every local virtue not serving that hour is waste wearing a medal.

**Spine.** (1) *M-hour.* The division marches SOUTH, away from a homeland under amphibious threat, and for forty-eight hours exists only as throughput across the Little Belt bridges — against a refugee counterflow. The constraint is walkable: an hour lost at the bridge is an hour lost to every battalion behind it. Herbie is a bridge. (2) *Digging in.* Holstein; the covering-force clock; his pride is the obstacle belt his engineers cut deep and forward. His own readiness reports mislead him on ground he then drives past — the metric caught lying, same page. (3) *The hold-line dawn.* Corps forbids the withdrawal his map demands; loss projections past twenty percent, the order signed sixty kilometers back. Attrition arrives with names. Behind him, Zealand — half the army pinned, his family behind the beaches he is not defending; ahead, retreat and homecoming fused: every kilometer ceded runs up the E45 toward Padborg. (4) *The confusion peak.* His belt is *working* — red slowed, ground held, his numbers superb — yet the promised counterstroke never comes. What no echelon sees whole: his belt keeps red fat on its railheads and keeps the first echelon coherent when the second arrives; the window his virtue was meant to serve, his virtue is closing. (5) *Insight.* A mobilized reserve captain — a bridge and traffic engineer in civilian life, his Jonah, asking questions only — walks him to it: what is the division *for?* He re-derives tempo as throughput inside his own boundary: thins the belt, re-times demolitions to desynchronize red's echelons rather than delay them, lets red overextend, husbands one brigade for the gap. Experiments changed at dusk, read at dawn. (6) *The hour.* The window opens once; his counterattack cuts a spent lead regiment between echelons — victory scoped to his reach, invisible in the corps' reporting format. He holds, and knows why.

**Didactic payload.** This altitude teaches natively what corps must brief: constraint identification (a bridge you can stand on), local optima versus system goal (the belt-vs-window conflict as load-bearing plot), batching and flow (march tables), metrics misleading with ground truth in frame, iteration at learnable tempo. Honest loss: FOFA and deep battle become inference — he reads the second echelon only by its effects. The Goal's rebuttal stands: Goldratt taught a systems theory without leaving the plant.

**The corps from below.** Rendsburg is weather: orders in English whose reasons don't travel, a new commander's unfamiliar signature on the hold order, sortie allocations from Karup that arrive or don't — UniCo with a liaison line. The binational conference material survives as pressure felt, never as scenes attended.

**Risk.** A monolingual Danish ensemble forfeits the integrated English-language Rendsburg staff, the theater's best native gift, and no cheap fix exists. The premise contracted for AirLand Battle's operational argument, and a division book delivers it only by inference — if the inference reads thin, this is the brigade book wearing a general's stars. And a general competent enough to command yet confused enough for the contract is a needle's eye: miss it and he reads as a fool, which kills both the contract and the authenticity bar.
