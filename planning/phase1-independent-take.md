## 1. Positions on the open rulings, with grounds

**1.1 Shape: A′ (compressed 2-chapter overture), and it follows
from the base without the specimen.** The structural counter-brief's
§1 attack is simply correct: The Goal has no pre-crisis chapters —
its ch. 1 IS the crisis — so option A's 13k peacetime part has no
precedent in the form, and the reader contract's own clauses cannot
fire in peacetime (a corps under exercise evaluation is neither
adrift nor confused-with-consequences). But the laboratory and the
baseline are real needs, and A′ buys both at 5k: open on ENDEX day
with the exercise's last hours going wrong *in a way the metrics
score as success* — the misleading-instruments engine starts on page
one, under exercise micro-clocks, without war. The change of command
at the endex dinner; the warning arriving under exercise cover at
the end of ch. 2. One addition to A′ as specced: the ENDEX scoring
scene should plant the SPECIFIC numbers (movement-table hours,
loss-exchange assumptions) that the war then falsifies one at a
time — the baseline is only worth its 5k if every later deviation
can cite it in one sentence.

**1.2 Ladder depth: SIX stations deep, not eight thin.** The
counter-brief's multiplication is arithmetically decisive (two
honest cycles ≈ 5–6k/station; ~30–35k variable ⇒ 5–6 stations),
and the §4a/§7 contradiction was conceded by the incumbent. My
composition — where I differ slightly from the counter-brief's
five — keeps exploit/subordinate as its own station because the
daily air conference is the iteration engine's main bed and
collapses without a station of its own:

| # | Station | Core transposition |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Metrics believed-and-broken | Exercise scoring + attrition/terrain-held reporting; believed in A′, broken by ~day 3 |
| S2 | What is the goal of this defense | Not terrain, not loss ratios: buying the corps' own build-up — throughput of combat power into position (the 90-hour clock's meaning) |
| S3 | Dependent events + fluctuations + the constraint (folded) | March tables are queues; the Danish division's move IS the hike; the constraint on corps tempo found where the columns stack |
| S4 | Exploit / subordinate | The daily air conference + reserve release + the screen; subordinating every echelon to the constraint |
| S5 | The enemy's constraint (the G-2 turn) | Red's echelon architecture and consumption read by inference; the v17 finding in-book: the G-2's architecture picture, not doctrine, decides the G-3's air question |
| S6 | The window | The counterstroke as throughput event, scoped to the corps' reach |

The meta-beat (whose consumption model is the argument standing on)
is NOT a station: it is the closing scene of the final chapter,
riding on the mentor-withdrawal affect — the staff stops asking the
instrument for answers and asks it for the shape of its own
ignorance. The counter-brief's writability test stands: if that
scene can't be sketched in one page that a blind reader can
paraphrase, it demotes further, to a line.

**1.3 Register: per-station, not global.** The counter-brief's
attack on the global conditional holds: nobody has embodied
intuitions about apportionment, and the project's own four-swing
whipsaw history is the proof that even instrumented experts could
not run ahead. Assignments: S1–S3 seminar-overt (genuinely
embodied — queues, hikes, scoring); S4–S5 adversarially staged by
default (the conference IS an adversarial forum; the G-2 turn is
staged as argument-with-evidence, closer to the source's tribunal
scenes); S6 set-piece (derivation compressed, consequence carries
it). The wargame instrument's role is staging aid and consistency
guarantor, not legibility oracle; the reader-ahead audit table gets
built at outline time and rules per station.

**1.4 B-plot: friction-dominant blend, (a) + a thin ration of (c)'s
interiority.** Mechanism (a) — civilian movement control as an
operational problem, the roads the corps needs full of what the
corps defends — is the only mechanism that is simultaneously
period-true, unsentimental, and A-plot-load-bearing (it prices S3:
the constraint competes with the defended for road-hours). The
protagonist's own family exists on-page in small fixed doses
(calls that don't connect, one letter, the map's Kreis names read
differently when one is his) but the CARRIAGE is friction. This is
close to what DK's rejection of pure (c) leaves standing, reached
here from the sentimentality argument, not the ruling.

**1.5 Second mentor visit: yes, at the envelope act-break.** The
counter-brief's strongest page: the Jonah find (the Americans send
a car; his own country's corps does not return his calls) is a
physical-presence irony, and a phone-only mid-book mentor is the
quest-giver failure mode arrived at by economy. Paid for by the S3
fold (three source-stations became one). Visit 2 belongs exactly at
day 13–14: the envelope break is the one moment the book's external
pressure (the audible knocking) and its didactic pressure (the
constraint has MOVED — is it still ours?) coincide.

**1.6 Red: R3 as designed, under the reasoning-not-reconnaissance
rule.** Independently of the prosecution's filing I find its two
load-bearing arguments survive scrutiny from the base alone: (i)
the Smyth slot is genuinely unfillable from blue — the ensemble
converges on the method, so week-3's only sincere instrument-

own reporting culture (norm-compliance fiction, plan_lag against a
declined timeline) means red's room contains no answer key — the
reader watches a second staff misled by its own instruments and
still has to do the G-2's work. Beats 1/2/3 at days ~3/9–10/17+,
one room, two named officers, placed after blue's commitments. The
beat-2 A/B artifact (interior vs G-2-mediated, blind-tested) stays
the gate before canon. Red beats outrank the division/Karup
interleaves in the overflow queue: they buy the didactic
hierarchy's #2 engine; interleaves buy visibility that the ruled
liaison windows already partially deliver.

**1.7 Voice: close-third limited, hard focalization discipline.**
The base pulls both ways (goal-read-notes §8: first-person-present
load-bearing in the source; the ensemble and any non-protagonist
scene pull third). The decider is structural, not tonal: R3 red
beats and the liaison windows are incompatible with first person,
and I independently want R3 (1.6). So: close-third on the
protagonist for the spine, with the confusion clause enforced as
information discipline (the narration knows nothing he doesn't);
red beats and windows as clearly-marked excursions. First-person
would be the better book only if red-zero were ruled — the voice
and red rulings are one ruling, and should be made together.

**1.8 Protagonist nationality: GERMAN — the strongest of the
genuinely open calls, argued here at full weight.**

- *The doctrine-returns-home thread only pays in a German frame.*
  The book's freshest historical irony — AirLand Battle's German
  intellectual debts arriving back in American packaging, in
  English, at a headquarters whose German half descends from the
  tradition TRADOC consulted — is scenery to a Danish protagonist
  and PATRIMONY to a German one. The Jonah hazard (clean-Wehrmacht
  nostalgia) is also best policed from inside: a German G-3 of the
  1983 generation has his own vexed relation to the Balck
  inheritance — he can want the operational insight and distrust
  its provenance IN THE SAME SCENE, which is the honest form of
  the thread and unavailable to an outsider-observer.
- *The change-of-command asymmetry maximizes.* Incoming commander
  Danish (ruled). A German G-3, one year in post, becomes the
  corps' institutional memory overnight ACROSS the national
  boundary — every persuasion scene is also an alliance scene,
  in English, the third language of neither man's childhood.
  Danish protagonist + Danish commander halves this.
- *The B-plot mechanism (1.4) is structurally German.* The hold
  line runs through German Kreise; the E45 fusion of retreat and
  homecoming; friction-as-carriage needs the defended ground to be
  the protagonist's OWN. The Danish variant's family-behind-the-
  Belts is real but its carriage is phone-and-letter — mechanism
  (c), the rejected one.
- *What the Danish choice buys, honestly:* the mobilization clock
  is Denmark's (the 90-hour hike is Danish roads); outsider
  vantage strengthens adrift/confused; fresher for Anglophone
  readers; and the commander-protagonist SAME-nation pairing has
  its own quieter drama (the new Danish commander seen by a Danish
  G-3 who knows what Copenhagen expects of him). I weigh this
  real but lighter: the Danish perspective enters the book anyway
  through the commander, the Jutland Division, and the theater
  command at Karup — the German interior does not enter except
  through the protagonist.
- Test before canon: the matrix re-render (same chapter, both
  nationalities × both voices) remains the right instrument; this
  section is the brief for one cell of it.
