## IGB / GDR TRANSPORT VERIFICATION — LÜBECK/MECKLENBURG SECTOR, NOV 1983

Convention below: **PHYSICAL** = roadbed/rail continuity a wartime advance could exploit; **PEACETIME** = what was actually open/sealed in 1983.

---

**1. Lübeck-Schlutup / Selmsdorf road crossing — CONFIRMED (open)**
- **PEACETIME:** OPEN in 1983. Grenzübergangsstelle (GÜST) Lübeck-Schlutup / Selmsdorf, the **northernmost** IGB crossing and the only one inside a major city. Reopened 1 March 1960 (after 1945–1960 closure); from 1973 also handled *Kleiner Grenzverkehr* (local border traffic). Grade: A.
- **Road numbers:** West side B104 (Mecklenburgische Straße out of Lübeck-Schlutup). East side the transit route ran on the former **F104** from the crossing, then **F105** from Selmsdorf (today's B104/B105). Grade: B (road-number-on-each-side is well attested but "F" vs "B" nomenclature: GDR used *Fernverkehrsstraße* F-numbers in 1983; renamed B after 1990).
- **Traffic permitted:** International transit, heavily truck traffic bound for **Scandinavia** — via Warnemünde (Gedser/Denmark ferry) and via Sassnitz/Rügen (Trelleborg/Sweden ferry); plus local border traffic. Grade: A.
- **PHYSICAL:** Continuous paved axis — a usable road corridor.

**2. Herrnburg rail crossing (Lübeck–Bad Kleinen line) — CONFIRMED (open)**
- **PEACETIME:** OPEN in 1983. Herrnburg (GDR side, opposite Lübeck) was the control station; closed 1952, reopened 1960 as one of the last interzonal passenger reconnections. Grade: A.
- **Services (1980s):** An interzonal passenger pair **Hamburg–Lübeck–Rostock** (the long-standing D 439/438 Köln–Rostock ran this route), plus weekend Lübeck–Schwerin service; **freight also crossed here.** Controls: ~40 min at Herrnburg platform + a further ~40 min of on-train inspection Herrnburg→Bad Kleinen. Grade: A. (Note: the named "D 1439/1438 Hamburg–Güstrow" and "E 2039/2038 Hamburg–Schwerin" pairs are documented for the *1989* timetable — mark not-1983-specific; but a Hamburg–Rostock interzonal pair via Herrnburg is valid for 1983.)
- **PHYSICAL:** Continuous rail — but **SINGLE-TRACK** (see #6).

**3. Schwerin–Gadebusch–Ratzeburg axis (B104 east / B208 west) — CONFIRMED (SEALED)**

Reopened only **12 November 1989.** Grade: A.
- **PHYSICAL:** INTERRUPTED at the border. The two national road segments did not connect roadbed-to-roadbed; the border ran through fortified terrain (fence, ploughed control strip, lakes — Ratzeburger See/Mechower See/Goldensee chain). **Wartime through-use would require engineering work** (breaching the barrier belt and bridging/grading the gap). - **Lübeck–Bad Kleinen line (the Herrnburg line):** **SINGLE-TRACK** (2nd track removed post-1945 as Soviet reparations) and **never electrified.** Grade: A.

**7. BONUS — Sperrgebiet depth &amp; fortification, northern stretch 1983**
By 1983 the GDR border regime on this Mecklenburg stretch had its mature layered form (fixed by the 1952 ordinance, hardened through the 1970s–80s): an outer **5 km Sperrzone** (restricted zone, permit/residence-controlled), an inner **500 m Schutzstreifen** (protective strip, entry only by special permit for residents/workers), and a **10 m control/barrier strip** at the line itself. Villages in the strip had been subject to forced clearances (*Zwangsaussiedlungen*). This depth is why the sealed interior axes (#3) were not merely "closed gates" but kilometres of engineered ground. Grade: B (general-regime sourcing, not a Mecklenburg-segment-specific 1983 survey).

## CONVERSION NOTES (native units → military planning)
- **Car-unit / PBE (personbilenhed):** Danish ferry throughput is quoted in "biler" or "car-units." 1 PBE ≈ one passenger car ≈ **~5 lane-metres** of deck. A lorry/truck ≈ **~3 PBE**; a semi-trailer rig ≈ 4–5 PBE. So the 1985 figure of 7,007 car-units/day ≈ **~35,000 lane-metres/day** of vehicle deck moved by 4 ferries.
- **Lane-metres from car capacity:** a ~200-car road ferry ≈ **~1,000 lane-metres**; the 390-car *Arveprins Knud* ≈ ~1,950 lane-m.
- **Rail side:** IC ferry = **~494 m of track across 4 lanes**. For military rail moves, tanks travel on flat wagons ~13–20 m each → order **~25–35 armoured-vehicle wagons per crossing**, minus locomotive/spacing.
- **Tonnage rough-out:** For heavy AFVs by road deck, deck strength (not just length) governs. A Leopard 1 (~42 t, ~9.5 m) occupies ~2 PBE of length but far more of the weight budget; realistically a road ferry rated ~200 cars carries only a **modest number of MBTs** before hitting axle-load/deck limits — treat "car capacity" as an upper bound on *length*, not tonnage. For planning use lane-metres × ~2 t/lane-m as a light-vehicle heuristic, and derate hard for tracked armour.
- **Crossing cadence for movement-rate math:** both belt routes ~**1-hour crossing**; Rødby–Puttgarden ~**1 hour in 1983** (NOT 45 min). With turnaround, one vessel yields ~**8–10 round trips/operating-day**. Fleet of 6 on the road route → the multi-thousand-vehicle/day throughput above.

# LAUTSCH ZEITZEUGENBERICHT — EXTRACTION &amp; VERIFICATION

**Document:** Siegfried Lautsch (Oberst a.D. NVA / Oberstleutnant a.D. Bundeswehr), *„Kämpfen können, um nicht kämpfen zu müssen." Operatives Denken im Selbstverständnis der NVA der DDR in den 1980er-Jahren am Beispiel der Operativen Planungen des Militärbezirks V.* BMVg Politik I 5, „Gespräche am Ehrenmal", 1 July 2021. 22 pp. Author = Leiter der Operativen Abteilung, Kommando Militärbezirk V (Neubrandenburg / 5. Armee); took part personally in the 1983 and 1985 operational planning; Frunze-Academy graduate. All page numbers are the printed page numbers (= PDF page numbers).

A recurring, load-bearing caveat: the maps of the 5th Army's operations (figs. on pp. 15/16/17) are labelled **"rekonstruiert durch den Autor"** — Lautsch reconstructed them from memory (Gehmert's 1983/85 decisions; Sylla's 1988 decision). The originals are destroyed or in Moscow (p. 15). They are informed reconstructions, not scans of the archival Entschlusskarten.

## NUMBERED VERDICTS

### TARGET 1 — Küstenfront / Polish Front, Jutland axis
**Verdict: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED (with an explicit author caveat).**

Confirmed: a **1. Polnische Küstenfront of three armies**, echeloned in depth **behind** the 5th Army, following **after two days**, attacking **toward Jutland** *and* along the North Sea coastal strip.

&gt; p. 8: *"Um 1980 war vorgesehen, dass hinter der neu geschaffenen 5. Armee nach zwei Tagen die 1. Polnische Küstenfront mit drei Armeen tiefengestaffelt folgen sollte, um sowohl nach Jütland anzugreifen als auch entlang des Nordsee-Küstenstreifens, dem Angriff der 5. Armee folgend, anzugreifen."*
&gt; ("Around 1980 it was foreseen that behind the newly created 5th Army, after two days, the 1st Polish Coastal Front with three armies would follow in depth, in order both to attack toward Jutland and to attack along the North Sea coastal strip, following the 5th Army's attack.")

Reinforced at the decision level:
&gt; p. 13: *"Er musste zügig erfolgen, denn nach zwei Tagen sollte die 1. Armee der polnischen Front folgen und nach Jütland angreifen."* ("It [the 5th Army attack] had to proceed swiftly, because after two days the 1st Army of the Polish Front was to follow and attack toward Jutland.")

**"Bypass Hamburg":** supported indirectly — the 5th Army's own axis runs *south* of Hamburg (p. 13, *"im Raum südlich Hamburg in Richtung auf die niederländische Grenze"*), and on the maps the attack corridor and the Polish follow-on both bypass Hamburg to the south.

**NOT confirmed / NOT in this document:** the **"one air army"** (no air army attributed to the Polish front here); and "advance toward the German-Dutch border" is the **5th Army's** objective, not stated as the Polish front's (the Polish front's stated axis is Jutland + the coastal strip).

**Critical caveat — Lautsch flags this as his own estimate:**
&gt; p. 8: *"Hierbei handelt es sich um meine persönliche Einschätzung vom strategischen Ansatz von 1. und 2. Front, da die Rolle der polnischen Front grundsätzlich und nach den polnischen Unruhen nach 1980 noch nicht erschöpfend erforscht ist, da polnische Kriegspläne erst im Januar 2021 in Warschau für die Forschung offengelegt wurden."*
&gt; ("This is my personal assessment of the strategic approach of 1st and 2nd Front, since the role of the Polish Front, fundamentally and after the Polish unrest post-1980, is not yet exhaustively researched, as Polish war plans were only opened for research in Warsaw in January 2021.")

Map corroboration: fig. p. 15 marks **"1. Poln. Armee EAs (x+2)"** near Szczecin (Polish 1st Army introduced from ~X+2).

### TARGET 2 — Covering phase (two divisions holding the SH border sector ~2 days; 8. MSD reverts to 2nd echelon)
**Verdict: SPLIT — the covering role of the Soviet 94th and the ~2-day/X+2 window are CONFIRMED; the specific "8. MSD + 94th hold Schleswig-Holstein sector, then 8. MSD reverts to 5th Army second echelon" is NOT stated as phrased.**

What the document actually says (footnote 18, p. 8) — the **94. Garde-MSD (Soviet)** was NOT part of the 5th Army's *attack*; it was in a special grouping to **cover the Polish formations' introduction into the Jutland operation**, deployed *outside* the 5th Army's attack lane:

&gt; p. 8, fn 18: *"Bei den Planungen von 1983, die auch eine Angriffsoperation vorsah, stand die sowjetische 94. Garde-Mot.-Schützendivision nicht zur Verfügung, da sie einer besonderen Gruppierung zugeordnet war, deren Ziel es gewesen ist, die Deckung der polnischen Verbände zur Ei[n]führung in die Jütländische Operation sicherzustellen. Infolgedessen wäre sie außerhalb des Angriffsstreifens der 5. Armee eingesetzt worden."*
&gt; ("In the 1983 plans, which also envisaged an attack operation, the Soviet 94th Guards MSD was not available [to 5th Army], as it was assigned to a special grouping whose aim was to secure the covering of the Polish formations for their introduction into the Jutland operation. Map corroboration: on the 1983 attack map (p. 15) the **94. GdMSD (GSSD) is drawn at Dassow** (Lübeck Bay / the Schleswig-Holstein approaches) with arrows toward Lübeck — i.e. covering the coastal/SH shoulder rather than in the main attack corridor.

Crucially, the two OOB call-out boxes on the combined map (p. 16) make the distinction explicit:

- **"5. Armee (Angriff): 8., 19., 20. MSD, 9. PD (NVA), 138., 221. s. PR (GSSD), 5. RBr (NVA)"** — in *attack* the 94th is DROPPED (it goes to the Jutland-covering grouping).

The **~2-day window (to X+2)** is confirmed by the Polish-front timing (Target 1 quotes; map label "EAs (x+2)").

**NOT COVERED:** the claim that **8. MSD** specifically holds a covering sector and then **reverts to 5th Army second echelon** — no such statement. 8. MSD appears throughout as a main combat division in the attack corridor (fig. p. 15). The word "Deckungstruppen (Vorausabteilungen im Angriff)" appears only as a generic planning category (p. 12).

### TARGET 3 — "5. Armeekorps" before 1983, redesignated "5. Armee" in 1983
**Verdict: NOT CONFIRMED / CONTRADICTED-BY-SILENCE.** The term **"5. Armeekorps" never appears** (grep-verified: no "Armeekorps"/"5. AK"/"Korps" other than opposing NATO corps). The document's account is different:

- Peacetime the entity is **Militärbezirk V (Neubrandenburg)**, subordinated to Kommando Landstreitkräfte, Potsdam. On transition to war it splits into an army staff + a territorial-MB command, and the field army is designated **5. Armee** (p. 6):
&gt; p. 6: *"Die Feldarmee mit der Bezeichnung 5. Armee wäre führungsmäßig dem Oberbefehlshaber der aus der GSSD zu bildenden 1. Front unterstellt worden."*
- The grant of independent operational tasks is dated to **~1980**, not 1983:
&gt; p. 6: *"…wurden dem Militärbezirk V erst im Laufe seiner Entwicklung etwa um 1980 als operative Vereinigung selbstständige operative Aufgaben zugebilligt."* (linked to the 1980 exercise "Waffenbrüderschaft-80").
- What *did* change ~1983 was the **mission**, not the name: before 1983 the 5th Army had a **~200 km coastal defensive sector** down to Wittenberge and defended the whole DDR coast; the 1983 revision gave it the concentrated offensive lane south of Hamburg:
&gt; p. 7: *"Für den Kriegsfall war vor 1983 vorgesehen, die 5. Armee in einem rund 200 km breiten Abschnitt von der Küste in südlicher Richtung bis Wittenberge zum Einsatz zu bringen. Im Zusammenwirken mit der Volksmarine hatte sie die gesamte DDR-Küste einschließlich der Inseln (u.a. Rügen) zu verteidigen."*

So the "corps→army in 1983" framing is not supported; the document already uses "5. Armee" as the wartime designation and attributes the 1983 change to mission, not nomenclature.

&gt; p. 17: *"Darüber hinaus hatte der SED-Generalsekretär Erich Honecker seinen Einfluss geltend gemacht und von den sowjetischen Generälen gefordert, die DDR unmittelbar an der Grenze zur Bundesrepublik zu verteidigen, um das eigene Territorium in einem Krieg nicht preiszugeben."*

&gt; *"…weil der sowjetische Generalstabchef Marschall Nikolai W. Ogarkow das Konzept [ALB] mehrfach sogar öffentlich als äußerst gefährlich für die bisherigen Planungen bewertete."*

**1988 strictly defensive** CONFIRMED (p. 18–19) — the 1. Front plan changed for the last time in 1988; strictly defensive, aim only *status quo ante*; driven by the **INF Treaty (18 Dec 1987)**, Gorbachev's **7 Dec 1988 UN unilateral cut (−500,000 men)**, the **new WP military doctrine adopted May 1987 in Berlin**, and the Soviet drawdown in the DDR (4 tank divisions, ~5,000 tanks, ~50,000 men; DDR unilaterally −600 tanks/−50 aircraft, early 1989).

## TEXTURE NOTES (staff culture — for the "red-room" scenes)

**The title/ethos.** The whole piece hangs on the motto **"Kämpfen können, um nicht kämpfen zu müssen"** (be able to fight so as not to have to fight). The planners *"waren überzeugt davon, das militärisch Richtige zur Abschreckung des Gegners und letztlich zum Erhalt des Friedens zu tun"* (p. 4).

**Extreme compartmentation.** Only **three Germans** were allowed to know the plan in full: *"der Minister für Nationale Verteidigung, der Chef des Hauptstabes und der Chef der Verwaltung Operativ"* (p. 12). At army level: the Befehlshaber, Chef des Stabes, and Leiter der Operativen Abteilung (= Lautsch himself). Problems of the operational plan were **never** discussed in the Nationaler Verteidigungsrat; *"Nur der Vorsitzende des NVR [Honecker] hat den Plan gesehen"* — and only after all Moscow/Wünsdorf coordination rounds, for confirmation (p. 12). Planners owed *"gegenüber jedermann absolute Verschwiegenheit"* and had to disguise training/exercises so the war-plan combat actions never became known (p. 5).

**Where the work was done.** Basic planning for the two NVA armies took place inside the **Soviet OK 1. Front / GSSD staff at Wünsdorf** (Zossen); the follow-on detail work in the **DDR MfNV at Strausberg** (p. 12). The army-level written/graphic documents were in **Russian**; division and brigade documents in **German** (pp. 13–14).

**Physical scale of the product.** *"Es entstanden mehr als 150 Karten im Maßstab 1:200.000 bzw. 1:100.000 und ca. 3.000 Blatt, die handschriftlich angefertigt wurden."* The 5th Army's actual decision was rendered on one **1:200,000 "Entschlusskarte,"** briefed to the OB 1. Front, signed by the Befehlshaber and Chef des Stabes and confirmed by the OB 1. Front (p. 14). Documents were kept as **"Geheime Kommandosache" in four copies**, in specially secured separate rooms at Front / Ministry / Military District / division level, and remained there until the NVA dissolved; some were destroyed, selected ones handed to GSSD Wünsdorf and *"heute noch vermutlich in Moskau"* — **none ever reached NVA archives** (pp. 14–15).

**Firewall between peers.** Between the generals/officers of the different armies **no arrangements at all were permitted** on secrecy grounds — *"sogar Konsultationen zu Sachfragen waren deshalb untersagt"* (p. 12). Coordination flowed only vertically, via the GSSD's deputy chief of operations, who *"übernahm persönlich die Koordinierung des Zusammenwirkens der gemeinsamen Handlungen der NVA-Armeen mit den sowjetischen Armeen"* (p. 12).

**Relations with the Soviets — collegial but Soviet-primed.** NVA officers recalled tasks being worked out *"im gegenseitigen Einvernehmen,"* drafts discussed jointly, and on disagreements (e.g. covering the state border, coastal defence) *"setzte sich nach eingehender Beweisführung … der überzeugendere Entschluss durch"* (pp. 12–13). BUT the enemy picture came from **Soviet 1. Front reconnaissance**, used even when it contradicted NVA intelligence: *"Auffallend war, dass … das Kräfteverhältnis im Streifen der 5. Armee mit einer Kampfkraft der Truppen von 100 Prozent angenommen wurden"* — and Lautsch's own retrospective judgment is pointed: *"Offensichtlich wurden aus politischen und militärischen Erwägungen, möglicherweise auch im Bestreben, die Operativen Planungen zu begründen, dem Gegner Aggressionsabsichten und Angriffspotenziale unterstellt"* (p. 13).

**The Entschluss as a genre.** *"Der Entschluss war das Produkt gemeinsamer Überlegungen, Vermutungen, Voraussichten, Erfahrungen und Intuition der Beteiligten und reflektierte das Wollen (die Absicht) des Befehlshabers"* (p. 14). Terminological bridge for a novelist: the NVA's *"Klarmachen der Aufgabe"* = the Bundeswehr's *"Lagefeststellung"* (p. 13); WP *"frühzeitige Operative Planung"* = NATO *"Eventualfallplanung"* (p. 12, fn 23).

**A striking secrecy detail worth a scene.** Regiment commanders were briefed on the *defensive* operation only; briefing them on the *attack* operation was **deliberately deferred to mobilisation** — justified by the assumed long warning time: *"Bemerkenswert ist, dass für diesen Personenkreis eine Einweisung in die Gefechtsaufgaben für die Angriffsoperation ausblieb. Diese Einweisung sollte später, im Rahmen der Mobilmachung erfolgen. Die angenommene lange Warn- und Vorbereitungszeit bot dazu gute Gelegenheit"* (pp. 14–15).

**Nuclear culture (the author's disquiet).** *"Eine große Beunruhigung ging für den Autor damals vor allem von dem Wissen um die von der Sowjetarmee vorgegebene Zielplanung des Einsatzes von Kernwaffen aus, wobei bekannt war, dass die NATO den Einsatz von Nuklearwaffen in vergleichbaren Dimensionen plante"* (p. 5). Nuclear target planning was **dictated by the Soviet army** ("vorgegeben"). He cites SIOP-5 (1982) as &gt;40,000 targets, one warhead per city &gt;50,000 pop., "für Moskau gleich mehrere" (p. 5). Note the reversal in the 1987 doctrine: WP pledged **never to use nuclear weapons first** — *"unter keinen Umständen … auch niemals als erste Kernwaffen einzusetzen"* (p. 20) — while retaining nuclear defence if attacked (p. 20, fn 28). No warhead-count, storage site, or release-authority mechanics are given (Himmelpfort/32-strike-salvo not present).

**Retrospective judgment (pp. 20–21).** Lautsch published *"teilweise im Widerspruch zur Generalität der NVA"* to reveal the 5th Army's planning and dismantle *"jahrzehntelange Spekulationen und irritierende Unterstellungen."* Serving later in both German armies and in the OSCE, he found that even senior NATO experts *"der in den 1980er-Jahren erfolgte strikte Wandel im Operativen und Strategischen Denken der Generalstäbe der WVO nicht bekannt war, unterbewertet oder verdrängt worden ist."* He stresses the 1983 plan was **not** meant to be executed absent reliable intelligence of an enemy attack — it was about **options to forestall a surprise NATO attack**, under *"Vorrangstellung des Primats der Politik"* (p. 20); yet the planners of 1. Front *did* consider **preemptive offensive action** possible on reliable warning: *"hielten die Planer der 1. Front präventive Offensivhandlungen für möglich; d.h. es sollte in die laufenden Angriffsvorbereitungen der NATO hineingewirkt werden"* (p. 16).

## TARGET 8 — NATO/Danish behaviour, warning time, Baltic/amphibious cooperation

**Warning time — 30+ days:** *"Die VSK rechneten damit, dass einem NATO-Angriff eine eigene Vorwarnzeit von 30 Tagen oder mehr vorausgehen könnte. Dies würde es erlauben, in einer Krise Alarmierung, Mobilmachung und Aufmarsch in die Ausgangstellungen rechtzeitig vor einem NATO-Angriff durchzuführen"* (p. 13). NATO expected to raise readiness under cover of exercises, with overseas air/sea reinforcement from the US (p. 13).

**Assumed NATO attack scheme (p. 13):** after massed air strikes, the mixed German-Danish Jutland Corps and Dutch I Corps attack, with two encircling thrusts on the 5th Army main body — **two divisions toward Schwarzenbek, Crivitz, Demmin and Lübeck, Wismar**, and **three divisions toward Lüneburg, Grabow, Freyenstein** — to break the defence, encircle and destroy the main grouping piecemeal.

**Amphibious/coastal:** *"…dass der Gegner nach Einführung operativer Reserven, im Zusammenwirken mit Luftlande- und Seelandetruppen, die amerikanische 2. Marineinfanteriedivision im Küstenstreifen mit Schwerpunkt Insel Rügen und Graal-Müritz, Kühlungsborn zum Einsatz bringen würde"* (pp. 13–14). Luftarmee** (p. 7); maps show territorial MSR elements defending Rügen (Putbus) and the Rostock/Graal-Müritz coast.

**Danish / SH order of battle as assessed (p. 9):** opposing = parts of **NORTHAG** and **BALTAP (Kdo Ostseeausgänge)** with the mixed FRG-Danish **Jütländisches Korps** and Dutch **I.
