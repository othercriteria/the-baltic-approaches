# The echelon question

*The primary question at the back of our minds (DK, 2026-07-20) until
Phase 1 settles it: at what echelon does the protagonist sit, in what
rank/position within it, and on which front? Alternatives are forced
here per process rules — no option is selected yet. Test everything
against planning/reader-contract.md.*

## What the choice must deliver

- **Doctrinal payloads experienced, not briefed.** Echelonment as
  constraint, deep battle / FOFA, tempo-as-throughput, recon-strike,
  sortie generation, misleading attrition metrics — each needs to
  arrive as lived consequence at the protagonist's desk/hatch.
- **A Rogo-shaped span of control.** Rogo owns the plant: authority
  enough to run experiments once persuaded, small enough that results
  are visible in days. The military analog must let the protagonist
  *change something* and *read the result* within the book's clock.
- **The contract's three clauses** (adrift, confused, earnest) —
  which, per the reader-contract doc, pull toward peripheral
  theaters and away from the anointed.

## Echelon options

**A. Division G-3 / chief of staff.** Sees corps intent, owns the
division's tempo, close enough to battalions that attrition has
faces. The classic Rogo fit: the division is a plant (throughput =
combat power delivered forward; inventory = columns stacked on
roads; the constraint = whatever is metering tempo — a bridge, an
artillery ammo flow, a decision cycle). Risk: division staffs are
large; the ensemble must be pruned to stay a staff-section shape.

**B. Corps G-3 plans (major/LTC).** The doctrinal altitude is
native — FOFA and echelon math live here. Risk: exactly the
PowerPoint-in-prose failure mode; war as map symbols; the earnest
clause starves because corps plans officers brief rather than do.
Would need aggressive scene discipline (liaison trips forward,
a corps that is itself small and improvised).

**C. Brigade command / S-3.** Maximal experience, minimal doctrine
legibility — deep battle is invisible from a brigade TOC except as
unexplained absences (why did the second echelon never arrive?).
Could work only with a structural device that shows the reader what
the brigade can't see; that device risks breaking the contract's
confusion clause (the reader should share the confusion).

**D. Chief of staff / G-3 of an improvised, composite formation in a
peripheral theater.** A scratch corps or ad hoc division-equivalent:
mixed nationalities or mobilized reservists, thin staff, no fixed
doctrine loyalty. Rogo-like authority by default (nobody senior is
watching closely — clause 1 by construction), doctrinal confusion by
construction (whose doctrine does a composite formation even run?).
Risk: research burden — improvised formations have thin published
OOBs, so the wargaming/ledger machinery carries more invention.

Rank note, common to all: the protagonist needs a boss to persuade
(the Rogo–Peach axis: pressure from above measured in the wrong
units) and a Jonah outside the chain of command. Command options
(the protagonist *is* the commander) trade the persuasion engine for
direct authority; The Goal's structure suggests keeping the boss.

## Front options (screened against the reader contract)

- **LANDJUT / Baltic Approaches.** Danish-German composite corps in
  Schleswig-Holstein/Jutland against GDR/Polish/Soviet axis with
  amphibious and airborne threats. Genuinely peripheral (NATO's
  attention is on the Fulda/North German Plain), genuinely composite
  (allied friction on-page for free), small enough for a corps to be
  the whole theater — the counterstroke can be *the* counterstroke
  of its theater without turning the war (contract: victory scoped
  to reach). Strong genre freshness; decent English-language
  research base (BALTAP structure is documented).
- **I Netherlands Corps sector, NORTHAG's northern flank.** The
  famous soft spot of the central front: peripheral-in-attention
  inside the main theater — legacy doctrine, mobilization-dependent,
  criticized throughout the early 80s. Clause 2b (unfixable under
  oversight) is strong here; NORTHAG micromanagement + national
  caveats supply it. Research base is excellent. Less fresh than
  LANDJUT (it's the genre's designated weak flank — Hackett used it).
- **Thrace / the Bosporus approaches.** Greek and Turkish formations,
  Bulgarian/Soviet axis; deeply peripheral, politically tangled,
  almost untouched in the genre. Freshness maximal. Risks: research
  base thinner in English; alliance politics could swamp the
  doctrinal payload; "1976-vintage doctrinal world" is *too* true —
  AirLand Battle concepts may be illegible from there.
- **Norway (North Norway / Finnmark).** Peripheral, vivid terrain,
  AMF and allied reinforcement machinery. But the scale is brigade
  war — the echelonment/FOFA payloads have no room to breathe.
- **Eastern Turkey / Transcaucasus.** Maximal periphery, mountain
  warfare, secondary Soviet TVD. Same legibility problem as Thrace,
  plus the thinnest research shelf. Likely a bridge too far.
- **(Baseline to beat) CENTAG / V or VII Corps.** The genre default.
  Doctrinally the *wrong* place per the reader contract: AirLand
  Battle was being institutionalized exactly there by 1986. Keep on
  the list only as the null hypothesis.

## Current lean (recorded, not ruled)

The reader contract + payload-legibility screen points at **A or D on
the LANDJUT or I NL Corps fronts**: an echelon that owns tempo, in a
place the reforming institution isn't looking. LANDJUT offers the
cleaner "whole theater in one corps" scope; I NL Corps offers the
sharper doctrine-mismatch-under-oversight. Phase 1 should wargame a
sketch of each (planning/wargaming.md) before ruling; the OOB ledger
prototype can double as the test rig.

Open sub-questions: Jonah's identity per front (a retired officer of
*which* army? an enemy-doctrine analyst reads differently in a
composite HQ); how the protagonist's nationality interacts with the
composite-formation option; whether the naval/air dimensions of
LANDJUT (Baltic amphib, sortie generation) are payload or scope creep.
