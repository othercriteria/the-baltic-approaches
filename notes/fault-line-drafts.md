# The Danish–German fault line — drafts package (Q3a)

*DK ruled 2026-07-24: draft broadly; the package is deliberately
overkill; adopt a subset. Nothing here is landed. Each variant
gives host, anchor, exact insertion text, cost, and risk. My
ranking at the bottom. The two sub-asks from the panel: (i) one
ugly corridor moment where the fault line speaks; (ii) the
Hvidt-as-COMLANDJUT licence felt aloud once by a German officer.*

Existing load-bearing texture (do not duplicate): ch. 1's
bilingual courtesies (Endstand/frokost, the two liaison officers'
acetates); ch. 2's "He had not seen the corps fight" tension —
professional, not national; ch. 13's vouching scene (the Danish
side of the memory, occupation-shaped); Reinfeld's "which of you
are we dying for" (command fault line, not national).

## V1 — The breath gets a name and a nation (ch. 5, the standstill)

**Anchor:** ch. 5, "Somebody at the fire-support desk let out a
breath through his teeth." → Hvidt's rebuke.

**Replace:**
> Somebody at the fire-support desk let out a breath through his
> teeth. It was not loud. The room was quiet enough to make it a
> statement, and Hvidt found its author without appearing to look.

**With:**
> At the fire-support desk a German Oberstleutnant let out a
> breath through his teeth, and then, because the room was quiet
> enough to make the breath a statement, finished it aloud. "With
> respect, Herr General. It is Holstein that is burning while the
> capitals define substantially. I would like to know if
> Copenhagen would be as patient about Jutland." It was said to
> the desk, not to the commander, which made it worse, and every
> German officer in the room did not look up in the same way.

**And extend Hvidt's rebuke** (after "...has forgotten what the
soldiers are for."):
> "And the other question you asked, Herr Oberstleutnant, I will
> answer once, standing, so it is on the record of this room: you
> would like to know whether I would trade Danish ground at Danish
> prices. You will not learn it from Holstein, because the prices
> here are set in three capitals, not in mine. You may learn it
> from my conduct, at the rate you learn everything else about a
> commander. Until then the corps defends this province as if it
> were the whole alliance, because this week it is."

Cost: ~+140. Risk: the highest-voltage version; puts the ugly
question directly in the operations room, mid-crisis, and makes
Hvidt answer the nationality of his command on the record. The
rebuke paragraph must not read as a set speech — it is licensed
by the convened-hour structure only weakly here (this is a
battle-day room, not a convened hour). If adopted, consider
trimming the rebuke's last sentence.

## V2 — Witzel answers the corridor (ch. 8, after the third order)

**Anchor:** ch. 8, after Witzel's "…if it cannot survive being
said plainly it is not worth doing." (his battle-rhythm
countersignature scene), before "It survived being said plainly."

**Insert:**
> One thing more went through the chief of staff's office that
> evening, and did not go further. A route-improvement major,
> German, collecting his failed inspection, was heard to say in
> the corridor that it had taken a Danish commander three weeks to
> find a use for German engineers that Germans had not already
> priced. Witzel had the door open before the sentence ended.
> "Herr Major. The commander's nationality is a fact of the
> alliance. Your sentence is a fact of your file. One of the two
> is going to outlast this war." He said it in German, which he
> otherwise declined to speak on duty, and closed the door, and
> the corridor took the exchange where corridors take everything,
> everywhere at once and nowhere on paper.

Cost: ~+120. Risk: low-medium. Keeps the ugliness at the working
level, gives the licence question a German answerer in procedure
idiom (his mother tongue, per ch. 6). "which he otherwise declined
to speak on duty" is new canon about Witzel — small, consistent
with ch. 1's English-language corps. The last clause flirts with
epigram; can end at "closed the door."

## V3 — The ugly working-level moment (ch. 7, the route order)

**Anchor:** ch. 7, the 1700 route-order scene, after the elderly
Oberstleutnant's "contested" costing, before "Alternatives."

**Insert:**
> "And I am required to say one thing not arithmetical, Herr
> Oberstløjtnant." The old administrator said it to the map, in
> the voice he kept for figures. "The columns that stand down
> tomorrow are German columns, deferred so that Danish battalions
> may pass. That is militarily correct and I have endorsed it. But
> it will be remembered in the district in exactly those words,
> and the district's memory of such words is longer than this
> plan. Somebody at this table should have to hear that once,
> before the order signs it into the files as routing."

**And Holt's line after the deferral decision** (extend "the
signature page will say so where the signature can see it"):
> "…where the signature can see it. And the district's sentence
> goes in the minute too, Herr Oberstleutnant. In your words. If
> the corps is going to spend that kind of memory, it will not
> pretend it spent something smaller."

Cost: ~+130. Risk: low. The most in-idiom variant — the fault
line priced like everything else in the book, by the man who owns
the column. Deepens the ch. 19a Territorial-redemption thread
(the same officer). Does NOT deliver sub-ask (ii) (no licence
line); pairs well with V2 or V5.

## V4 — Loheide's staff, overheard (ch. 9, the visit)

**Anchor:** ch. 9, the Aukrug visit, after "nobody offered
anybody coffee," before Loheide's brief.

**Insert:**
> In the byre's cable bay a divisional staff captain, not
> knowing or not caring that the corps party could hear, was
> finishing a sentence to a colleague: "—dritte Woche, und die
> Front hält, weil ein dänischer General deutsches Land mit
> deutschen Bataillonen bezahlt." Third week, and the front
> holds because a Danish general is paying for German ground
> with German battalions. Nobody translated it for Hvidt, and
> Hvidt, who had commanded at Rendsburg for a month, did not
> need it translated, and neither man in the byre door said
> anything at all.

Cost: ~+95. Risk: medium. Delivers sub-ask (ii) obliquely and
ugly — the licence resented in German, in earshot, unanswered.
The untranslated-German device is new for the book (which
otherwise renders everything in English); the in-text gloss keeps
faith with the reader. Loheide himself stays clean (his dignity
is load-bearing); his staff carries the resentment, which is how
staffs work.

## V5 — The smallest possible insubordination (ch. 5 or ch. 12)

**Anchor:** ch. 5, the 1100 announcement scene (alternative
placement: ch. 12's capitals sequence), immediately after Hvidt's
"…and this staff will not sneer at it."

**Insert:**
> "Zu Befehl, Herr General," said the fire-support desk, into its
> handsets — German, in an English-language headquarters, the
> smallest available insubordination, and the only one offered
> all day. Hvidt let it stand. Some purchases are cheapest made
> at once.

Cost: ~+45. Risk: low. The fault line at its most compressed:
one phrase in the wrong language, let stand. Can accompany any
other variant; can also stand alone as the entire answer to
sub-ask (i) if DK wants the minimum. "Some purchases are
cheapest made at once" is narrator-epigram — cuttable.

## V6 — The Territorial memory, closed out (ch. 19a)

**Anchor:** ch. 19a, the Territorial companies' second war
("they were the corps' now, by an order Witzel had drafted…and
the answer showed."), extend:

> …and the answer showed. What the order had not changed, one of
> the district companies' after-action supplements stated anyway,
> in the space for remarks: THE COMPANY NOTES THAT IT WAS ASKED
> IN OCTOBER FOR WHOM IT WAS DYING. IT RECORDS THAT IN NOVEMBER
> THE QUESTION NO LONGER AROSE. NOT BECAUSE IT WAS ANSWERED.
> BECAUSE BY NOW THE DEAD ARE FROM EVERYWHERE.

Cost: ~+55. Risk: medium-low. Closes the Reinfeld arc on the
national axis (the dead as the only integration mechanism —
ugly in the earned way). CAPS-format supplement is in the
book's grammar. The last two fragments are strong medicine;
DK should taste before adopting. Note: "asked in October" must
read "asked in the war's first hours" if adopted (the Reinfeld
question was 8 Nov — fix at adoption).

## Ranking and packaging advice

Sub-ask (i), the ugly moment: **V3 first** (most in-idiom, prices
the grievance in the book's own currency), V1 if DK wants it at
command level instead (higher voltage, higher risk).
Sub-ask (ii), the licence aloud: **V2 first** (answered, in
procedure), **V4** if it should go unanswered (uglier, truer;
costs the untranslated-German precedent).
Minimum viable package: V3 + V2 (~+250 words).
Maximum sane package: V3 + V2 + V5 (~+295); V1 replaces V3/V2
only if the command-level version is wanted instead — adopting
V1 alongside them would overload the axis.
V6 only if DK wants the thread closed at the far end too;
V4 and V2 together would double-serve sub-ask (ii) — pick one.
