## Orientation (uncontested)

East–west rampart system across the neck of the peninsula, ~19 km
core (~30 km with outworks), Schlei/Hedeby in the east to the
Treene marshes in the west. Built by the Danes to block northward
advance from the south: **it faces south**, against Franks,
Saxons, and later Germans. The manuscript's "facing south" and
"the narrows between the Schlei's head and the Treene marshes"
are correct.

| Phase | Date | Builder |

## Every documented military event, with direction

| Event | Date | Attacker | Direction | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Henry the Fowler | 934 | East Frankish king vs. Gnupa | **south** | penetrated; Hedeby taken, tribute |
| Otto II | 974 | Ottonian emperor | **south** | stormed; borderland + Hedeby occupied 974–81 |
| Battle of Schleswig (First Schleswig War) | 23 Apr 1848 | Prussia/German Confederation (Wrangel) | **south**, frontal | stormed under artillery; Danish retreat (Danes cut Schlei ice at Mysunde vs. an eastern outflank) |
| Evacuation of Danevirke (Second Schleswig War) | 5–6 Feb 1864 | Prussia + Austria | Austrians frontal **south**; Prussian turning move **east** across frozen Schlei | abandoned by night without a stand (de Meza), withdrawal to Dybbøl |
| Wehrmacht anti-tank-ditch scheme | 1944–45 | (own use; no battle) | n/a | conversion prevented by archaeologist Søren Telling |

**No invasion ever came from the north.** The only non-frontal
threats (1848 Mysunde, 1864 crossing) came from the **east** over
the Schlei — the exact flank the manuscript's fictional geometry
also worries (Sehestedt, the Schlei arm), a happy convergence.

## Verdict on line-edit 12.5 (confidence: very high)

Ch. 12's "dug against invasions that had come, every one of them,
from the other direction" is **flatly wrong** — the record is the
exact inverse. No rescuing reading exists (Godfred's southward
raids are Danes going out, not invasions coming in; 1864's turn
was from the east, not the north).

The historically true — and stronger — irony available to the
passage: the wall always faced the right way, the invaders always
obliged from the south, and it was stormed (934, 974, 1848) or
outflanked and abandoned (1864) every time. It never got to fight
and WIN the south-facing battle it was built for until this
(fictional) war. In-story the enemy axis is from the southeast
and the corps holds the crest, so the corrected irony is both
watertight and earned.
