# Copyright and AI authorship — the law under this book

*Session 94f69afb, 2026-08-01, DK-directed. Sources held in
`reference/pdf/` (shelf batch 11, all US-gov public domain);
application mechanics in `planning/copyright-brief.md`. Verified
current to this date; the framework is case-by-case and moving —
re-verify at any filing.*

## 1. The doctrine, compressed

1. **Human authorship is required.** *Thaler v. Perlmutter* (D.C.
   Cir. 2025-03-18) affirmed refusal of a work claimed as
   authored *by* a machine: "author" in the Copyright Act means a
   human. The court expressly did NOT decide how much human
   contribution suffices when a human uses AI — that line is
   drawn by Office practice, not yet by appellate law.
2. **Prompts alone are insufficient.** The Office's Part 2
   *Copyrightability* report (Jan 2025) concludes that "given
   current generally available technology, prompts alone" do not
   provide sufficient control to make the user the author of the
   output.
3. **Three registrable routes in AI-assisted works** (Part 2 +
   the March 2023 registration guidance, 88 FR 16190):
   (a) human-authored expressive elements; (b) creative
   **selection, coordination, and arrangement** of AI-generated
   material (compilation-type authorship); (c) creative
   **modifications** to AI output. Registration then covers only
   that layer; more-than-de-minimis AI content must be disclosed
   and excluded from the claim.
4. **The precedent line.** *Zarya of the Dawn* (2023): human text
   and the selection/arrangement of a graphic novel registered,
   Midjourney images excluded. *A Single Piece of American
   Cheese* (registered Jan 2025): a claim granted on
   selection/arrangement/modification of AI-generated material
   ALONE. Against these, the Review Board refusals — *Théâtre
   D'opéra Spatial* (624 prompts + Photoshop, refused; applicant
   would not limit the claim), *SURYAST* (style transfer,
   refused), *A Recent Entrance to Paradise* (the Thaler work) —
   mark the other edge. The operative difference: applicants who
   **limited the claim honestly** to the human layer have been
   granted; applicants claiming the AI output itself have been
   refused.

## 2. Applied to this book

- The narrative text was drafted, every sentence, by Claude
  entities; the ledger says so plainly and forever. Under current
  US practice the **prose as such is not copyrightable** and
  would be excluded from any registration. This is true whether
  or not anyone files anything.
- The claimable layer is DK's: premise and constraint system,
  hundreds of documented rulings, line-grain editorial direction,
  selection among generated alternatives, structural
  ratifications. It maps onto route (b) — selection/coordination/
  arrangement — with the honest complication that much
  arrangement was itself AI-proposed and human-ratified. The
  Office has never examined a claim documented at this grain;
  the project sits on the unresolved edge of "sufficient
  creative control."
- The ledger cuts both ways, by design: it is simultaneously the
  best-documented demonstration of human creative control ever
  available to an applicant and a precise public record of how
  little of the fixed expression is human. An application must be
  (and here, can only be) accurate — §411(b) makes knowing
  misstatements a registration-killer, and the record is public.
- **Ruled (DK, 2026-08-01): the text will not be salted with
  human-authored touches to manufacture a stronger claim.**
  Un-completing the book to dissolve the legal situation would be
  antithetical to the project; the position is held as-is.
- Consequence for the license: over the narrative text, the
  repo's license terms operate as the project's stated
  expectations — requests in place of locks — because the lock
  may not legally exist in the US. LICENSE.md now says so
  (amendment of 2026-08-01). Jurisdictions differ: UK CDPA
  s.9(3) protects computer-generated works for 50 years, author
  = "the person by whom the arrangements necessary for the
  creation of the work are undertaken" (DK, straightforwardly);
  the EU and most Berne states track the US's human-authorship
  side. Nothing here was chosen for jurisdictional arbitrage.
- Flag, DK's call (errata tier): the eBook imprint carries
  "© 2026 Daniel Klein. All rights reserved." (print edition
  carries no © line). The line is conventional and not false —
  some rights exist and are reserved — but it is broader in
  tone than the project's stated position; alignment is a
  question for any future eBook rebuild, not an errand.

## 3. What the documents are (reading order)

1. Part 2 report — the policy frame; read §§ on prompts,
   expressive inputs, and selection/arrangement.
2. 88 FR 16190 — the practice rules any application follows.
3. Zarya letter — the grant-side template, closest analogue in
   form (mixed human/AI work, claim limited, registered).
4. Thaler (D.C. Cir.) — the ceiling case; useful for what it
   explicitly declines to decide (n.: AI-assisted works left
   open).
5. The two Review Board refusals — the refusal register and its
   reasoning style; what examiners cite when they say no.
