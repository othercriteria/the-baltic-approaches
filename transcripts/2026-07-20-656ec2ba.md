I've read the whole book (extracted the PDF text via the repo's nix shell — the harness box lacks poppler, so I read the plates' placement from the text layer and layout) plus CLAUDE.md, process.md, versions.md, and the revision-stack, which is really the method's crown jewel. Here's my assessment.

## What the method is actually good at (and what that implies for the next pick)

White Buffalo's certified strengths — procedural concreteness, material culture, lean dialogue, earned restraint, and above all the *instrumented revision program* (fresh-context blind readers, adversarial brief pairs, period audit, regression/hostile certification rounds, the geo-ledger and continuity machinery) — are all strengths that reward **research-dense, systems-heavy, restrained** material. The known weaknesses are length undershoot, groomed-cadence tics, and voice convergence. So the ideal next premise is one whose *natural* size is 50–60k (note the repo's own lesson: the 60k target was abandoned because the story was a 31k story — don't pick a novella and inflate it), and which stresses a new craft muscle (ensemble cast, sustained plot logistics) rather than re-running dual-voice pursuit.

## The four itches

**The Goal for AirLand Battle — my recommendation.** This is the best fit on almost every axis. The didactic-novel form is exactly the right scaffold for the length problem: every chapter carries a doctrinal payload (attrition metrics that mislead, echelonment as the constraint, tempo as throughput, the Jonah-figure Socratic beats), so the outline has natural load-bearing units and length is carried honestly rather than padded. It plays directly to the certified strengths — a division staff at war is *all* material culture and procedure. The research burden is heavy but beautifully documented (FM 100-5 '82/'86, TRADOC pubs, the Soviet OMG literature), and your period-auditor instrument transfers as a doctrine auditor + hardware auditor almost unchanged. Copyright friction is low: didactic form isn't protectable, and the generic NATO/WP-1980s setting is a shared genre (Hackett, Team Yankee) as long as characters and events are original — "Red Storm Rising-like" as vibe, not as borrowed furniture. Zero cultural-sensitivity minefield. The cringe risk (didactic fiction is inherently a little earnest) is genre-honest — The Goal is beloved *because* it commits. The one hostile instrument you'd want to invent: a "Clancy-pastiche detector" and a "PowerPoint-in-prose" reader who flags every place the pedagogy shows its seams.

**Chinese New Orleans / Taiping — the best *book* of the four, but not this one.** The premise is genuinely rich (Taiping's dates sit perfectly inside the antebellum window; the syncretic-Christian theology colliding with New Orleans Catholicism, the free-people-of-color caste system, and slavery is a real novel). But it's oversized at full scope — alt-history means inventing a whole counterfactual society — and it walks you straight back into a sensitivity load (Chinese diaspora + enslaved people, written from outside) right after you asked for a break from that class of challenge. Your own instinct is the right one: **a study within the premise**. The version I'd bank: one narrator, one bounded event — the 1853 yellow fever epidemic summer, say, or a single murder inquiry in the quarter — where the settlement is the *ground*, not the subject. That's a 50–60k shape. I'd save it for the attempt after next, once the method has been proven at this scale on safer ground.

**Star Trek ASW — the same itch as #2 in a different uniform, plus real friction.** Notice that ideas 2 and 3 are the same story at the doctrinal level: a force under a threat its metrics can't see, developing detection, escort, and convoy doctrine. The Trek version has a lovely model (The Cruel Sea compressed to an escort crew) and the size is right. But it's fanfic, and your work-product convention is a *released, attributed, ISBN'd artifact with an open repo* — that pipeline is simply unavailable for a Paramount-universe work. Noncommercial AO3-style release is fine as a practice, but then it can't be the milestone artifact the way White Buffalo was. If the Trek skin specifically is the itch, do it as a side-serial someday; if the *doctrine-development* story is the itch, #2 scratches it releasably. (Filing the serial numbers off into an original space-navy setting is possible but usually loses exactly the flavor you want.)

**Rat-ical Honesty — wrong scale for this goal, great side quest.** A feature screenplay is ~110 pages ≈ 20–25k words; it structurally cannot be the 50–60k milestone, and novelizing it kills the itch. It's also maximally far from the demonstrated strengths — the Opus-profile red flags (cleverness, cuteness) are the genre's *requirements* here, which makes it a fascinating stress test but a bad vehicle. Where it shines: as a separate small project exercising a different pipeline — screenplay format, table-read instruments, and your image-gen machinery doing storyboards instead of plates. Park it, don't drop it.

## Ideas the book itself suggested to me

Reading it, the transferable DNA is: document-driven narration, a rumor network the protagonist moves through, elision of the central violence, and the pursuit shape. Two non-Indigenous premises that rhyme without repeating:

- **A Great Lakes wreck novel** — Aldridge's Oswego world. A November gale, 1860s–1900s; keeper's log + board-of-inquiry testimony as the dual documents (an evolution of journal + chapters into genuinely *adversarial* documents that disagree). Lighthouse and lifesaving-service material culture is as rich as the plains furniture, and the period-audit instrument feasts on it.
- **A telegraph novel** — the 1859 Carrington event as seen from operators' logs across a network: the rumor-network device made literal infrastructure. Probably a 30k book, though, so second-tier for this goal.

But honestly, the AirLand Battle book *is* the post-White-Buffalo idea: it's the "procedural concreteness as the whole aesthetic" bet, scaled up.

## Method notes for 50–60k, whatever you pick

- Build the ledgers (geo/timeline/continuity, plus an order-of-battle ledger for #2) in Phase 1, not as a v0.6 retrofit — that audit was expensive because it was archaeology.
- The undershoot problem compounds at this scale: ~25–30 units each quietly 15% thin is a missing 8k words. Per-unit word floors, human-diagnosed thinness, as CLAUDE.md already warns.
- The new craft increment should be named explicitly in the plan: WB proved dual-voice; 55k wants either an ensemble (the escort-crew/staff-section shape handles this naturally) or a longer single-POV arc with subplot discipline. Pick one on purpose.
- The instrument suite transfers wholesale; only the auditor personas need re-casting (doctrine, hardware, veteran-reader, pastiche-detector).

**Bottom line:** I'd run the Goldratt/AirLand Battle book as the 50–60k attempt, bank the New Orleans study (scoped to one season/one inquiry) as the one after, treat the Trek piece as a possible noncommercial side-serial, and keep Rat-ical Honesty as a short-form pipeline experiment. If that ranking matches your instinct, the natural next step when you're ready is a fresh repo skeleton with the WB process docs ported and re-cast — happy to set that up.
