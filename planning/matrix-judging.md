scratch/matrix-test/README.md. Six blind fresh-context judges
(Opus), three personas × two chapters, run against a

## 2. What the judges converged on (independently)

1. **The Danish position is the richer novelistic engine in
   these two chapters** — every judge, every persona. The
   specific assets, named repeatedly: (a) the buy-column
   inversion — a Dane's family is not a cost the war might reach
   but the GOOD the costing buys ("a stronger thematic rhyme with
   'name the good' than the original owns" — craft ch5); (b) the
   occupation-memory charge under the Rahn mentorship — "for a
   German pupil that is reverence; for a Danish pupil it is
   reverence braided with occupation memory... the German listener
   is a near-total loss for this particular scene" (craft ch13),
   "Danish, decisively" (veteran ch13); (c) minority-partner
   texture — trilingual triangulation (thinks Danish, drilled
   German, works English), "never once the Danish rank," "a war
   administered in two languages and his own not among them";
   (d) costing an ally's soil "more cleanly than a man with a
   house in the border Kreise — and did not trust the cleanness."
2. **The specimen cell's last place is about STAKE, not grammar.**
   DA-close-third beat both first-person German cells for the two
   trust-weighting judges: close third carries warmth fine when
   the protagonist has something at risk in the room
   (free-indirect unsaids: "*He was hardest on us,* Holt did not
   say. *It was the only courtesy he had.*"). The original reads
   coldest because chs. 5/13 as drafted give Vossen no on-page
   personal stake (his Kreis beat sits in ch. 7), so the chapter
   becomes "an intellectual puzzle about accounting."
3. **Voice verdict, portable rather than binding:** first-person
   present wins the naive reader everywhere (live comprehension,
   shared confusion) and both craft judges rate its
   live-lateness beat ("I have it now — a half-second late... heat
   in the face") the best single craft move in the set; but the
   veteran trust vote goes to close-third both times, all three
   personas flag present-tense fatigue risk at book length, and
   the foreshadowing tell ("I let myself have the feeling. I am
   wrong to") plus self-narrated epiphany are recurring
   first-person costs. Since close-third is structurally required
   anyway (red rooms, windows — known to us, not to the judges),
   the actionable finding is: KEEP close-third past and IMPORT the
   first-person virtues as technique — the felt-lateness beat, the
   confusion clause enforced at sentence grain ("he did not
   understand that yet at 0100" is renderable in third), and the
   free-indirect unsaid.
4. **The uniform-voice failure is confirmed at full strength.**
   Both craft judges, unprompted: the four versions are "one
   writer in four costumes"; "the prose has one beautiful voice
   and it belongs to the author, not to any of these officers";
   Roloff, Aakjær, Rahn "all speak in that same polished-epigram
   key." Matches specimen failure §4.2. The renders did NOT fix
   it; nationality/voice swaps don't touch it.
5. **Rahn's monologues are the shared seam in every cell** — "the
   author's essay wearing an 82-year-old general as a glove";
   survives every costume change because it sits in the shared
   skeleton. Confirms allocation row 13's planned conversion
   (monologue mass → the staff's wrong answers) and extends it to
   the ch. 5 midnight call.

## 3. The confound, stated honestly

The test was asymmetric. The Danish renders IMPORTED discrete
interiority blocks (family-stakes paragraphs, language-texture
beats) that the German original chapters do not contain — the
craft judge documented the insertion-block structure without
knowing which cell was original. The German cells never received
their own planned upgrade (allocation-sheet row 5 already
schedules "Vossen sees his own Kreis in the hold order" as an E4
addition; the specimen's German stake lives in chs. 7/16/19a,
outside the tested pair). So part of the 20-vs-7 spread measures
"chapter with a stake beats chapter without one" — which is
finding §2.2, already banked — not "Danish beats German."

What survives the confound: the Rahn occupation-memory charge,
the buy-column inversion, and the minority-partner texture are
STRUCTURAL to the Danish cell — no German-cell upgrade can import
them. What the test never saw: §1.8's German case lives mostly in
untested chapters — doctrine-returns-home patrimony (no ch. 5/13
content), the cross-national institutional-memory asymmetry with
the incoming Danish commander (Hvidt scenes), and the B-plot
geometry (chs. 7, 16, and 19a run through the GERMAN Kreise; a
Danish protagonist makes Column 217 someone else's street and
19a's school someone else's school — the friction variant's
carriage would need redesign, which is exactly the "phone-and-
letter mechanism (c)" hazard DK already rejected). Also note the
change-of-command device: Danish protagonist + Danish commander
halves the cross-national asymmetry §1.8 prized, but the veteran
judge found real charge in the same-nation triangle (a Danish
G-3 vouching to a Danish general for a German mentor).

## 4. Options for the DK ruling (voice+nationality are one ruling)

- **(a) Rule Danish now.** Grounds: six-of-six blind convergence
  on the Danish engine; the structural (unimportable) assets;
  the specimen's German cell was the coin-flip, not an argued
  incumbent. Cost: accepts redesign of the B-plot friction
  carriage (home behind the Belts; the Kreis geometry becomes a
  colleague's grief — Lammers or Roloff could carry the German
  house), and accepts losing §1.8's patrimony thread as
  interiority (it survives as ensemble material: Rahn, Witzel,
  Loheide own it).
- **(b) Symmetric re-test first (one cheap round).** Render ch. 5
  GE-third WITH its planned row-5 stake upgrade + one currently
  untested German-advantage chapter (7 or 16) in the Danish cell;
  re-judge blind. Decides the confound at ~2 renders + 2-3
  judges' cost. Choose this only if the unimportable Danish
  assets (Rahn charge, buy-column) don't already settle it —
  they are the strongest evidence and a fair German ch. 5 cannot
  rebut them.
- **(c) Rule German per §1.8.** Grounds: the brief's three
  structural arguments, mostly untested by this instrument.
  Cost: the ruling would stand against the only blind-reader
  evidence in the record, and the method-not-doctrine reframing
  (draft-zero-findings §2) has already weakened §1.8's lead
  argument — if the payload is the method, the doctrinal
  patrimony is texture, not spine.
- **Voice, all options:** close-third past on the spine stands
  (structural necessity + veteran trust + fatigue risk), with the
  three imported techniques from §2.3 written into the outline's
  craft notes as REQUIREMENTS per spine chapter: a stake in the
  room, a felt-lateness beat where comprehension is the drama,
  and the unsaid rendered free-indirect rather than voiced.

Session recommendation (a brief's recommendation, counter-brief
always available): **(a)**, with (b) as the fallback if DK wants
the confound formally closed before canon. The deciding weight is
the unimportable pair — the Rahn relationship carries five
