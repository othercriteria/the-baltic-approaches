## 0. What the book is, in its own words (the interpretive key)

The Preface (p.iii) is the single most useful page for us. Macksey/DND state the book's identity with unusual precision, and every apparatus decision follows from it:

- It opens by **negation**: it "is not a manual of army doctrine." It is "a training aid," designed to "create a mental image" of combined-arms combat for officers who have not lived it.
- Technique named outright: "an illustrated narrative of battle scenarios," chronological, following a Battle Group's mobile defence against a Soviet Tank Division, "the summer circa 1984."
- Crucially, it **licenses deviation from doctrine**: incidents "may appear to contradict our doctrine"; doctrine is "the background against which he weighs the other factors." The book giving itself permission to dramatize error.
- It **disclaims its own authority**: "may not be quoted as an authoritative source." The fiction is deliberately walled off from the doctrine corpus — the opposite of a manual.
- Author bio is load-bearing to the institution: Major (Retd) **Kenneth Macksey, MC**, ex-Royal Tank Regiment, Normandy tank troop commander, military historian (Guderian, Rommel, etc.). Authority is imported via the author, not the text.

**Takeaway:** First Clash solved the "is this doctrine or a story?" tension by *declaring itself a training aid and disclaiming authority up front* — a cleaner contract than The Goal's (which never admits it's a seminar), bought at the cost of the discovery engine (§4).

## 1. THE APPARATUS INVENTORY

The book's most transferable feature. First Clash carries an enormous amount of doctrine, data, and orders **outside the prose stream**, in tinted boxes, facsimile forms, silhouette org-charts, colored maps, and chapter-terminal "Lessons Learned" pages. The narrative runs on cream stock; nearly every apparatus element is set on a **colored or gray tint block** or a separate leaf, so the eye sorts "story" from "reference" instantly.

### 1a. Front matter (institutional framing)
- **Cover / half-title:** Bilingual DND, pub number **B-GL-309-006/FT-001**, "Canada" wordmark — unmistakably an official publication. Library plate: "Force Mobile Command HQ."
- **Verso credit:** "Prepared by FMC Headquarters," restyled/published by CF Training Materiel Production Centre, CFB Winnipeg; ©1984 DND Canada; "15 January 1984"; "Stock Reprint September 1987" stamp.
- **Foreword (p.i):** Three short tinted bullets. Signed on the authority of **the Chief of Defence Staff**; "effective upon receipt"; solicits reader comments to FMC HQ. The institutional imprimatur — issued like a manual even though it disclaims being one.
- **Preface (p.iii):** ~1 full page, nine tinted bullets (§0 above).
- **Table of Contents (pp.v–vi):** 15 chapters with sub-sections; the cast-foldout note. ~2 pages.
- **Frontispiece map (facing p.1):** Full-page line map, **"The Bühl Plateau and its Approaches"** — the real Rastatt/Bühl area rendered as the fictional battlefield, compass rose, autobahn E4(E11), every named village. Geography established before a shot is fired.

### 1b. Order-of-battle material (silhouette org-charts + holdings tables)
The book's signature reference form:
- **"4 Canadian Mechanized Brigade Group"** org-chart (p.6): NATO symbols, full brigade tree, sub-annotations ("19 × Leopard C1 per Sqn," "18 × TOW," "15 × Blowpipe"). Full page, landscape.
- **"Provisional ORBAT in Assembly Area"** (p.8): simplified task-org box tree for 3 RCR Battle Group.
- **"Initial Deployment ORBAT"** (p.33): a *text* task-org on a tint block — each company's "Under Command / With / In Location" attachment lists.
- **Soviet org-charts, one per formation as they enter**: "1st Guards Tank Division Organization Chart" + "Major Equipment Holdings" silhouette table (p.16); "290th Motorized Rifle Regiment" + holdings (p.60); "301st Tank Regiment" + holdings (p.84); "Motorized Rifle Battalion" + holdings (p.86). Holdings tables are grids of vehicle/weapon silhouettes with counts.

**Interaction with narrative:** *optional but reinforcing* — the prose is followable without the charts, but the charts let a reader audit force ratios and attachments; placed at the moment the formation first acts ("here is who just walked on stage").

### 1c. Facsimile forms and formatted orders (the highest-fidelity apparatus)
Where First Clash goes furthest; the most novel resource:
- **Warning-order apparatus (p.23):** a tinted box **"Contents of a Warning Order"** (doctrinal checklist, "Extract from B-OL-303-002/FP-001") *plus* the **actual warning order text** the fictional Commander 4 CMBG sent. Doctrine template and its in-story instantiation, side by side.
- **"Battle Procedure — Infantry Battle Gp"** (pp.24–25): two-page **timeline matrix** (Bn RGP / Coy RGPs / Pl RGPs / Main Body) tracking the orders-and-recce drill down the echelons.
- **Target List (SECRET) facsimile (p.42):** full reproduction of form **CF 642**, filled in with grid refs, target descriptions, FPF annotations — stamped "SAMPLE."
- **"November Company Oral Orders"** (pp.44–45): the complete **five-paragraph field order** in full formal format. Two pages. The orders the prose has just described being given — shown as a document.
- **"3 RCR Battle Group — Basic Command Radio Net"** (p.70): node-and-spoke **radio net diagram**.

**Interaction:** the apparatus that most *substitutes for* dramatization — the prose narrates a commander thinking; the facsimile hands you the finished staff product. Authoritative, lets a student see the real paperwork — but the *lesson of how to write an order* is delivered by exhibit, not by watching someone struggle to write one.

### 1d. Data / comparison tables
Gray/tan tint blocks, embedded where the weapon matters: **"Minefield Stores" + "Laying Times"** (p.35); **"Armoured Defence Weapons Comparison Data"** (p.36: TOW / Carl Gustav / M72 / Leopard / T-72 / Sagger / RPG-7 by arming range, effective range, RPM); **"Blowpipe"** (p.72); **"Night Fighting Aids and STANO Equipment"** (pp.104–105).

### 1e. Definition boxes + line-art teaching illustrations
**"Defilade"/"Enfilade Fire"** box (p.38) with pen-and-ink tank drawing; hull-down/turret-down schematic panels (p.36); **"Order of March for the Soviet Advanced Guard"** (p.82) — a large labeled bird's-eye line illustration of the Soviet column, a picture doing a paragraph's work.

### 1f. Maps (two conventions)
1. **Terrain maps** (line, monochrome): the frontispiece.
2. **Operational overlay maps** (spot color), one per major phase: "Comd's Plan 1 GTD" (p.12, red on white); "4 CMBG's Deployment Shortly After First Light 25 June" (p.46, blue/green); "The Advanced Guard... Probes Förch at 1600 Hours" (p.78); "The Assault Against Favorite and Kuppenheim" (p.92); "The Attack by 290th MRR at 2000 Hours" (p.98). Consistent NATO blue-friendly/red-enemy over the same base terrain — the reader watches the battle move. **Near-essential**: the one apparatus class the prose genuinely leans on.

### 1g. Photographs
Real B&W photos ~every 2–4 pages with italic captions frequently carrying a **micro-lesson** ("The too-well-exposed Blowpipe waiting for the target," p.72). Ground the fiction in real hardware/terrain; the book's main texture engine.

### 1h. Chapter-terminal "Lessons Learned" pages
Every chapter ends with one or more **full pale-blue leaves**, black **"LESSONS LEARNED"** bar, **bulleted takeaways** keyed to the chapter's sub-sections. Length scales with chapter (Ch.2: two bullets; the Deployment chapter: **three full blue pages**, ~20 bullets). Ch.4's includes an enemy-side list ("The enemy will also have his problems such as: traffic delays; ...fears of nuclear retribution...").

**Page cost, rough (pp.1–105):** front matter ~8 leaves; org-charts/ORBATs ~6 pp; facsimiles/orders/nets ~7 pp; data/definition/illustration boxes ~8 pp; color maps ~5 pp; Lessons Learned ~8–10 pp; photo plates on ~25–30% of leaves. **Ballpark: 40–50% of the physical page count is narrative-external apparatus.** First Clash effectively runs a ~50k-word-equivalent book on maybe half that in continuous narrative, because the apparatus carries the rest — the number most relevant to the narrative-only ruling.

## 2. NARRATIVE CRAFT

### POV structure — the strongest craft asset
**Dual-sided, roaming close-third omniscient, dipping into a very large cast.** Alternates whole chapters between the armies: Canadian chapters move from Lt-Col Tinker (CO 3 RCR) and Maj Linkman (OC B Sqn RCD) down to named privates in slit trenches; **Chapters 2 and 4 are entirely inside the Soviet camp**, and Soviet POV re-enters combat chapters. The Soviets are individuated with real interiority: Maj-Gen Samsonov's dread-driven ambition, Lt Tursov thinking of "his newly-wed wife Nina," Pte Rudi Leshko's bewilderment that his country claims to be *invaded*. Not a cardboard enemy.

The camera **descends the rank ladder within a single scene** — company commander's plan, platoon commander's worry, named private's grievance. It gives the doctrine a human carrier at every echelon.

### Chapter structure & clock
15 titled chapters, most subdivided by named sub-sections; ~8–14 pp each including apparatus. Scanned portion (Ch.1–8) ≈ ~40k narrative words. **Tight calendar-and-H-hour clock**: political build-up "15th of June"; frontier crossed night 22/23; deployment "no move before 2000 hrs"; defend "from 0300 hours 25 June"; first contact "1515 hours"; deliberate attack "2000 hours." Time-stamps are constant tension ratchets. The action is the **first ~72 hours**.

### Hardware & procedure rendering
Fidelity delivered three ways, descending narrative integration: (1) **in-prose nomenclature saturation** with *tactical consequence* (ranges drive who-shoots-whom); (2) the data-box apparatus backing the prose numbers; (3) **procedure rendered as lived friction**: the approach march collapses into a traffic jam (Ch.3), a Leopard throws a transmission, ammunition dumping "slipped behind schedule." Doctrine shown *failing to survive contact with reality* — the book's best trick.

### Prose register
Measured, formal, British-inflected; long periodic sentences; free indirect thought; occasional dry wit in dialogue. **Deliberately dry and unhurried** — the training-aid identity licenses a register a thriller couldn't sustain.

### Do characters LEARN on-page?
**Overwhelmingly they execute correctly.** Learning is displaced onto the reader (Lessons Learned) rather than dramatized as an arc. Exceptions are narrated errors by minor figures as negative object lessons (§3). No protagonist discovery arc; nobody's worldview is overturned. **The central craft gap versus The Goal.**

## 3. THE DIDACTIC MECHANICS (mapped to The Goal's hierarchy)

1. **Narrated correct execution + authorial framing (dominant).** A commander weighs factors, chooses well, the prose quietly explains why it's doctrine. → **narrated assertion**, the weakest tier.
2. **Chapter-end summary restating the scene (Lessons Learned).** Pure narrated assertion / didactic seam; the most PowerPoint-in-prose feature; exactly the move The Goal refuses.
3. **Narrated error with consequences (the good stuff, rare).** Best: **WO George Crane** (pp.85–86) disobeys fire discipline, fires early at a lone BMP, misses, is silently reproved — then the text explains why. Also MCpl Benson's map-reading failure (p.51); Soviet Shulubin into the minefield (p.85). These *enact* lessons through failure and cost → the top tier, but small payloads, professional consequences only.
4. **Adversarial articulation via the enemy chapters.** Samsonov and Oblensky given genuine reasoning teach Soviet doctrine from inside, and the two doctrines critique each other. **Oblensky arguing Samsonov out of a daylight attack (pp.99–100) is the closest thing to a Socratic exchange in the book** — two professionals forcing an alternative. Most worth stealing.
5. **Doctrine-by-exhibit (facsimiles).** Efficient, authoritative, inert.

**Essentially absent:** the naïf's dumb-smart question; the Socratic mentor; the ensemble whiteboard; any reframing physical-model demonstration. Only technique 4 reaches the middle of The Goal's hierarchy.

## 4. WHAT THE TRAINING-PUB FRAME BUYS AND COSTS

**Buys:** authority without ingratiation (CDS signature + Macksey's MC — establish authority once, spend it on restraint); permission to be didactic in the open (the frame legitimizes the apparatus); fidelity reads as institutional seriousness; the apparatus/narrative separation is clean and load-bearing (tint blocks and separate leaves let the reader run story and reference at different altitudes).

**Costs (the negative-case lessons):**
- **No discovery arc.** Nobody learns; the reader is told. No protagonist whose understanding transforms — the engine The Goal runs on.
- **Stakes are purely professional** — missed shots, lost minutes, a reproving radio call. No personal jeopardy making a lesson matter emotionally.
- **Interiority broad but shallow.** Dozens of heads, none inhabited long enough to bond with.
- **The Lessons Learned pages are an admission of failed dramatization.** Every bullet is a lesson the scene should have made unmissable. **Diagnostic marker: anywhere a draft wants a "lessons" box, the preceding scene under-delivered.**
- **Doctrine-by-exhibit is inert.** The facsimile order teaches format; watching Alex fail to prioritize teaches more.

## 5. OTHER CRAFT-RELEVANT NOTES

- **Altitude handling.** A battlegroup book that gestures up (VII US Corps) and down (a single Blowpipe gunner) but lives at company/squadron level. Transferable: the **"thinking two levels down"** motif. A corps-HQ Goal inverts it — the staff officer reasons up to the operational problem and down to what a division can do, and the discovery is about **which level the real constraint lives at. First Clash never finds its constraint; The Goal is about finding the constraint. That gap is the whole opportunity.**
- **The enemy side is the best model in the book.** The "enemy" is the operational problem; giving it agency and internal logic creates the adversarial pressure the protagonist's discoveries must answer. Steal the Oblensky-argues-Samsonov structure as the template for forcing alternatives on the page.
- **Nuclear/chemical as ambient dread, never used.** "Nuclear weapons were at the alert but... no immediate prospect" (p.31); chemical-cloud behaviour as a terrain-siting factor (p.30); Soviet fear of nuclear retribution as a reason to hug the enemy (p.59). Constant pressure on decisions, never discharged — directly aligned with our conceit.
- **Friction is the book's real subject.** Its liveliest writing is the traffic jam, the ammo slippage, the refugee columns, the breakdowns. When plans meet mess it stops being a manual and becomes a story. **The constraint is always revealed by friction, not by correct execution.**
- **The apparatus IS the continuity system.** Recurring silhouette ORBAT + holdings tables, color-consistent maps, and the timestamped clock keep a huge cast legible — a working demonstration that our ledgers (OOB, geography, timeline) can double as the book's apparatus.

**One-line synthesis:** First Clash is the definitive proof that you can carry ~half a book's payload in narrative-external apparatus and still be taken seriously — and the definitive proof that apparatus plus correct-execution prose, without a discovery arc or personal stakes, produces a respected training aid that no one would read for pleasure. Take its carriage architecture and its living enemy; reject its told-lessons and its arc-less competence.

One per chapter/section, on a pale-blue tinted leaf under a black **LESSONS LEARNED** banner, a bold section subhead, then filled-square (■) bullets. **Structure is a bulleted noun-phrase list, never numbered, never argued** — each bullet labels a topic, e.g. (Ch.9) "The value of the tank TV set." / "The importance of smoke at night."; (Ch.10) "The value of the M72 in a melee between infantry and AFVs." Some chapters carry two subheads' worth of bullets on facing leaves (Ch.11–12 sprawl to two leaves). Effectively a **syllabus checklist / discussion index appended after the drama** — a topic pointer, not analysis. Cost: **~1–2 pages per chapter (8–12 pp across this half).**

### E. Cast (back matter, ~1 page of content)
Two blocks — **FRIENDLY FORCES** and **RED FORCES** — each row: *rank · first name · surname · appointment* (e.g. "LCol Brian Cowdray — CO, RCD"; "MGen Gregor Samsonov — Commander, 1st Guards Tank Division"; "Cpl Igor Lyubshin — Tk Comd, 290th MRR"). ~40 friendly, ~11 Red. ### F. Photographs
Heavy — roughly a dozen+ captioned B&W photos in this half (many full-page): T72 w/ KMT5 mine-plough, dismounted TOW, Leopards night-firing, "Red Army infantry charging," A-10, the gas-alarm respirator shot, Hind w/ Sagger, BRDM, ZSU-23-4/SA-7 air defenders, maintenance/casualty-recovery vignettes. Mix of Canadian exercise photography and stock Soviet-kit images. They carry *texture*, not data.

**Apparatus takeaway:** Macksey offloads an enormous doctrinal/data load to narrative-external carriage — hardware specs → tables, tempo & geometry → timestamped maps, drill abstractions → schematics, personae/OOB → back Cast. That validates the ruling that such carriage is "free." **But** the one thing he tries to carry externally that *fails* is the teaching itself: the "Lessons Learned" bullets are inert.

## 2. How the battle resolves

**Shape.** Despite the jacket's "72 hours," this half dramatizes roughly **one compressed night-into-morning** (~1900hrs to ~1100hrs next day) — the titular *first* clash. The Soviet 1st Guards Tank Division (via 290th MRR, then 301st/302nd/303rd Tank Regiments) attacks 4 CMBG's forward battlegroup (3 RCR + B Sqn RCD) on the Blickheim feature. Sequence: preparatory bombardment → minefield-breaching assault on Blickheim (held) → break-in at Favorite/Förch (crest lost) → 301st's attempted break-out toward Iffezheim (blocked/blunted in a killing zone) → RCD counter-attack that stabilizes → **daylight withdrawal under Corps orders.**

**Attrition arithmetic is explicitly on-page.** Losses tallied in prose and, decisively, in the **Sitrep chapter (Ch.12)** which stops the action to state each sub-unit's strength as a percentage: Mike Coy ~85% ("Comfortable"), Oscar ~70%, November ~40%, B Sqn reduced to ~6 tanks, 1 RCHA ~90%. Force ratios named as doctrine ("magic figure of three to one"); B Sqn's exchange rate cited (≈20 enemy vehicles for 5 own). The "Blocking" chapter frames the odds numerically: five Leopards + eight TOWs vs. ~95 tanks + ≥10 BMPs.

**The ending teaches culmination and economy of force, not victory.** No decisive result: 4 CMBG inflicts heavy, favourable attrition yet is ordered to give up ground it bled to hold, because VII (US) Corps is being enveloped and 3rd Guards Tank Division is breaking through on the flank. The withdrawal is a *successful* covering-force delay, not a rout — register deliberately sober/anti-triumphalist. Final paragraph (p.211) turns explicitly grim: each backward step makes nuclear resort "closer, if not obligatory." So victory/defeat at battlegroup scale resolves as: tactical proficiency + heavy enemy cost, strategic retreat, escalation shadow lengthening.

**In-story clock handling — a model.** Constant explicit timestamps (1915, 2025, 2100, 2130, 2200, 0030, 0100, 0200, 0230; **First Light 0315**; **H-Hour 0345** for counter-attack; withdrawal **H-Hour 1000**, with **H-10** notation; 1050, 1115, 2000). Maps are timestamped to match. Tempo is made legible; "First Light" is a recurring hinge the plan is built around.

## 3. Didactic mechanics (late)

**Chapter-end summaries** — verbatim structure: black **LESSONS LEARNED** banner → bold section subhead → **filled-square bullets of topic-labelling noun phrases** (never numbered, never sentences of reasoning). Strictly **episodic**: each keyed to its own chapter, none building on the last. **No accumulation into a method** — no restatement, no compounding theory, no "and therefore the principle is…" spine. By the withdrawal chapters the bullets simply lengthen (Ch.15's list runs a full extra leaf) without deepening.

**Authorial voice** is omniscient and *interventionist* — repeatedly steps out of scene to assert doctrine directly: "military pundits commend," "as history told him," "for generations… gunners and mortarmen." Occasional aphorism is the nearest thing to dialogue-teaching (a character recalls "a British General called Slim" on not trusting the sole survivor's panic, p.117). No naïf-questioner, no mentor figure, no whiteboard scene.

**Against The Goal's hierarchy**, ranked by what First Clash actually uses:
1. **Physical model enacted** (strong): the battle *is* the model — every tactic dramatized in consequence (smoke blinds both sides; white light forfeits hull-down cover; single unsupported tanks die; dug-in infantry beats armour in the open). The book's real teaching, often good.
2. **Adversarial articulation** (strong, the book's best engine): the **Soviet planning POV** — Oblensky, Podduyev, Samsonov reasoning through echelon, momentum, bypass-vs-reduce, concentration risk — teaches doctrine by watching commanders decide *why*. The closest thing to a Socratic dialectic in the book.
3. **Narrated assertion** (heavy): the omniscient explanations and the "Lessons Learned" bullets — the weakest tier, and structurally the apparatus.
4. **Socratic dialogue / naïf questions / ensemble whiteboard**: essentially **absent**. No character discovers anything; command radio exchanges convey orders, not inquiry.

**The negative-case lesson is sharp:** First Clash proves that *enacting* doctrine dramatically is necessary but not sufficient, and that *pushing the lesson into a narrative-external bullet list is the lowest-value move available.* Nobody in the book has a mental model that visibly changes; there is no Jonah, no chain of because-questions, no protagonist arc of realization. It teaches by illustration-plus-annotation, never by guided discovery.

## 4. Enemy-side handling

**Competent and genuinely interior.** Named Soviet POV runs deep: MGen Samsonov (div cmdr), Col Oblensky (290 MRR), Col Podduyev (301 Tk Regt), Col Romanov (div arty), Capt Rusalski (FOO), Lts Shulubin & Tursov, Sgt Nikolai, Cpl Lyubshin, Pte Leshko — all in the Cast. Their doctrine is exposed *from inside their reasoning*: massed narrow-frontage thrust at top speed for momentum (Podduyev: "Tanks will lead… there must be no pause"); artillery-first then armour; willingness to bypass strongpoints and shift the schwerpunkt to the weaker-resisted axis; friction shown honestly (ammunition shortage, minefield-breach delay, jamming-coordination, command indecision after a commander is killed). They visibly *learn* — by the withdrawal, Shulubin and Nikolai are "more circumspect… than at the outset," pacing their pursuit to trade time for lives. The least caricatured element and the most transferable: **the antagonist as an unwitting articulator of the operating theory.**

**Nuclear/chemical backdrop — the conventional battle is explicitly nested in the larger war.** The **gas-alarm scene** (Ch.10, "Gas! Gas! Gas!", pp.131–132) is a *false* alarm caused by white smoke shells, and it teaches both the NBC drill and its cost (respirators cut combat effectiveness; only the NBC-overpressured Leopards fight near-full capacity). Nuclear release governs tempo on *both* sides: Samsonov deliberately won't over-concentrate vehicles for fear NATO desperation triggers a nuclear strike (p.127); the book closes stating repeated withdrawal brings NATO nuclear use nearer (p.211). So "why is this battle conventional?" is answered as *mutual escalation management* — a living backdrop, not set dressing.

## 5. What the training-pub frame buys and costs (this half) + craft notes

**Buys:**
- License for the reference tables, timestamped maps, schematics and Cast to ride as first-class citizens.
- **Full-syllabus coverage**: chapter titles march through the doctrinal phases (deliberate attack · defence of a village · break-in · blocking/counter-penetration · sitrep · counter-attack planning & execution · withdrawal · covering-force action · ambush · EW). Comprehensive as a curriculum.
- Authority/realism from named cast and correct staff texture (SITREP percentages, H-Hour math, call-signs, radio net drill, FOO/BC mechanics).

**Costs:**
- The "Lessons Learned" leaves are **inert** — telling, not discovery; a syllabus stapled to a story.
- **No protagonist.** Characters are function-bearers (each exists to demonstrate one tactical point) with thin interiority; reader identification and any growth arc are absent. No one's understanding deepens — the fatal gap for a Goal transposition.
- **Visible didactic seams**: the narrator halts to admire a technique ("yet another example of the superiority of a stationary weapon system…").
- **Episodic, non-cumulative** — lessons never compound into a method; no equivalent of T/I/OE as a through-line.

**Directly craft-relevant for a corps-altitude Goal transposition (LANDJUT):**
1. **Steal the alternating friendly/enemy planning POV** as the dialectic engine — the adversary reasoning aloud is First Clash's strongest teaching and the nearest thing it has to Jonah. (NOTE for our project: red interiority is currently NOT budgeted at 55k — this finding is the strongest argument the counter-brief could make against that cut; but our G-2-mediated red is itself the book's argument. Tension flagged for Phase 1.)
2. **Steal the clock discipline and timestamped maps** — they make operational tempo legible cheaply; "First Light / H-Hour" hinges give the plot a spine.
3. **Steal the SITREP chapter as a structural beat** (Ch.12) — a pause that tallies the whole system in percentages is the "measurements review"; the book's most Goal-like device (state-of-the-system reckoning), could recur as periodic throughput checkpoints.
4. **Invert the "Lessons Learned" failure.** Offloading *facts* to tables/maps/Cast is proven safe and free; offloading *the teaching* to bullet leaves is proven dead. Keep the wargame-output annex and bibliography as **evidence/reference**, never as the lesson — discovery stays inside a protagonist's dramatized reasoning.
5. **Note the ending register**: culmination + heavy attrition + strategic retreat + escalation shadow. A sober, non-triumphal close is available and credible in this genre; The Goal's up-note victory is not the only option.
