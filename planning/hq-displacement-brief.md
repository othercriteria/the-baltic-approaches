# Design brief: the static headquarters (blind-panel design question #1)

*2026-07-25, session 52662a0d. DK asked: "What do our resources,
doctrine, plan, etc. say about this?" Shelf sweep run (Explore
agent over reference/, notes/, planning/); digest condensed here
with citations. Deliverable: assessment + options + a
recommendation AND its counter-brief. No drafting until DK rules.*

## 1. What the shelf actually holds

**On CP displacement doctrine: nearly nothing.** No extraction
covers main/alternate/jump-CP structure, displacement cadence, or
the signals cost of moving a corps HQ. The doctrine that would
settle it — HDv 100/100 (mid-80s) + Danish field regulations — is
already on the TO-ACQUIRE list (landjut-front.md:227). The
veteran-reviewer's "we planned to fight from displacing, dispersed
command posts" is testimony, not a sourced note.

**On the real arrangements:** LANDJUT HQ at Rendsburg peacetime,
FmBtl 610 + a Danish signal battalion (landjut-front.md:69, 214).
**No wartime displacement site, alternate CP, or war bunker for
LANDJUT is documented anywhere on the shelf.** The sourced war
bunkers are all one echelon up: BALTAP/COMAIRBALTAP at Karup
(Bunker 1137; Finderup from 1985; alternate at Vedbæk)
(air-apportionment.md:22–51, 106–108). Note: the draft's
"casemate cellars" under Rendsburg are OUR invention — plausible
for a fortress town, sourced nowhere.

**On the red threat to the HQ — the sweep's sharpest find:** our
own shelf documents WP doctrine tasking OMGs with "destruction of
rockets, command posts, reserves" (php-maritime-front.md:342,
Bange on SOYUZ-81, quote+page). Karup is a named nuclear DGZ in
the captured planning (php-maritime-front.md:202, 423). The Scud
brigade (5. RBr Demen) is documented. BUT: nothing on the shelf
maps any of it onto the corps HQ specifically; **Spetsnaz/
diversionary threat is absent from the shelf entirely** (a real
threat-picture-ledger hole regardless of this ruling); and the
wargame never modeled HQ targeting or survival on either side.

**What the plan pinned:** staticness is a design decision, not an
oversight — protagonist-echelon.md:198–201 argues the corps HQ
sits 95–110 km deep in a shallow theater, so "the war comes to
the headquarters"; the wall is the book's unity-of-place stage;
the ch. 13 harassment beat is built on the fixed address.

## 2. The honest tension

Our own sourced extraction says red doctrine wants command posts
destroyed; our draft's red conspicuously spares one for a month
("never the headquarters compound, whose location the enemy
certainly held" — ch. 13). The draft currently offers no reason.
That is the veteran's objection restated from our own shelf. And
one more internal fact: Rahn already SAYS the thesis a
displacement would dramatize — "the wall is plywood, Holt; the
institution is the discipline, not the board" (ch. 10) — and the
book never tests the sentence. The crate Aakjær carries into ch.
14, and ch. 20's "photographed twice before it left the cork,"
are both displacement-shaped artifacts already in the text.

## 3. Options, with blast radius

**A. Full displacement regime (the veteran's three moves).**
Realism ceiling, and it enacts Rahn's plywood line thrice. Blast
radius: LARGE — nearly every chapter stages in a fixed geography
(gallery, plexiglass, corridor to the intelligence cell, the
morale-and-welfare circuit, Rahn's visit, the joiner); doctrine
we don't hold would be load-bearing (invention risk until HDv
100/100 arrives); and the book's unity of place — its answer to
the seminar-in-prose hazard — is spent. NOT RECOMMENDED at this
draft's maturity.

**B. One displacement, at the ch. 13/14 seam (recommended).**
The harassment chapter ends with the decision; ch. 14 opens at
the dispersed war site with the wall re-pinned — Aakjær's crate
becomes a displacement artifact for free, and the
photograph-first discipline debuts here instead of ch. 20.
Rahn's plywood sentence gets its test: the institution survives
the plywood's dismantling. The counterstroke is then commanded
from a site red hasn't registered, which quietly answers "why
does the HQ survive the crest's fires." Blast radius: BOUNDED —
one new scene (~500–700 words, inside the remaining length
budget and the panel's "fund adds" reframe), one plant earlier
(the alternate site prepared, ch. 8 or 12, a line), site-texture
touches in chs. 14/16/17 (casemate references migrate), Rahn's
visit (ch. 13) untouched since it precedes the move. Shelf work
first: the Spetsnaz gap note + conventional-Scud accuracy check
(VERIFY tier), so the in-text reasoning stands on something.

**C. Keep fully static, argue it in-text (cheapest).** One
Aakjær beat naming why the building stands: red's instrument for
killing corps HQs is the OMG that never got past the delay
fight (ties HQ survival to the corps' own performance — sourced
to the Bange extraction); conventional Scuds are a poor trade
against a dispersed, hardened target being saved in the other
folder's arithmetic; the harassment pattern re-cast so the
sparing reads as economy, not courtesy. ~150 words, zero
structural risk. This much is probably owed EVEN IF B is
adopted.

## 4. Recommendation and counter-brief

**Recommendation: B + the in-text argument from C.** One move,
placed where the text already has the artifacts for it, paying
off a thesis line the book already carries; the veteran's
realism served without surrendering unity of place; cost fits
the remaining budget.

**Counter-brief (the case for C alone):** The unity-of-place
stage is the book's structural answer to its biggest diagnosed
hazard (the seminar); a displacement scene is a "war movie" beat
in a book whose discipline is refusing them; the panel's other
findings (voice, proportion) are higher-value uses of the same
revision capacity; and B builds on doctrine the shelf admits it
does not hold — the one thing this project has promised not to
do is invent where it could source. If HDv 100/100 arrives
later, B remains available at draft two.

DK rules.
