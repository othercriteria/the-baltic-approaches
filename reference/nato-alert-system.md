## The verdict up front

**Chapter 2's catalog is sound.** The real early-1980s NATO alert architecture
is a graduated, pre-agreed, nation-ratified menu of measures whose every
invocation was understood — at the time, in 1983 specifically — as a signal
read by the adversary. That is precisely the chapter's mechanism and precisely
its theme. Nothing to walk back. Flags are refinements, not corrections.

## The system's shape (ESTABLISHED unless noted)

The Cold War NATO alert system was **three parallel instruments**, not one
ladder — a fact the chapter can lean on if it ever names states:

1. **State of Military Vigilance** (Da. *Militær Vagttilstand*). Purely
   military precautionary measures for "a period of delicate international
   relations," sustainable for a long time without ill effect, easing a rapid
   transition to formal alert or counter-surprise if needed.
2. **Formal Alert System** — three ascending stages: **Simple Alert**,
   **Reinforced Alert**, **General Alert**. Political *and* military measures;
   the orderly peace-to-war transition under mounting tension.
3. **Counter-Surprise Military System** — purely military measures "to enable
   NATO forces to survive and retain the capability to carry out their
   missions" under attack with little or no warning. Two states: **ORANGE**
   and **SCARLET**. SACEUR-authorized. (Origins 1956; see docs below.)

**The catalog concept.** Within these, the system is a *pre-agreed menu of
discrete precautionary measures*, standardized in terminology and content
across the alliance so a measure means the same thing to every nation. Its
lineal descendant, the modern NATO Crisis Response System, still works this
way — a catalog of selectable **Crisis Response Measures (CRM)** drawn as
needed — which is the same architecture the chapter's "catalog... graduated
and numbered" describes (INFERENCE that the 1983 measures were literally
*numbered*; the *catalogued and discretely selectable* character is
ESTABLISHED).

**Who declares what.**
- **The North Atlantic Council** declares the formal alert stages; measures
  and terminology are pre-agreed alliance-wide (MC 67 series). Political
  control is absolute — no move without Council authority.
- **SACEUR** holds the counter-surprise alert authority (the survive-the-
  first-blow measures), the one instrument delegated to the military commander.
- **Nations retain autonomy.** Denmark explicitly resisted 1962 NATO pressure
  for "automatic solutions," reserving the right to assess and approve each
  measure per crisis. Nations run their *own* national readiness systems in
  parallel and can invoke national measures without NATO declaration — the
  structural basis for the chapter's "quiet list underneath the catalog."

## The 1983 record — the visibility problem, documented

This is the chapter's exact theme, and Able Archer 83 scholarship states it in
so many words (National Security Archive, Able Archer 83 Sourcebook,
nsarchive.gwu.edu):

- **Alert measures are signals — contemporary NATO said so.** Gen. Colin
  Powell's contemporaneous assessment warned a worldwide nuclear exercise
  "could be perceived as showing an intent for use of nuclear weapons" — the
  measure read as message. (ESTABLISHED)
- **The adversary can no longer tell exercise from mobilization.** Soviet
  Defense Minister Ustinov: "it becomes more and more difficult to distinguish
  [NATO exercises] from the real deployments of armed forces for aggression."
  This *is* "the enemy as the catalog's first reader," from the reader's side.
- **Escalation by precautionary response.** The Perroots memo warned that a
  precautionary US force-generation "against a Soviet alert in response to
  the Able Archer 83 NATO nuclear release exercise" risked cascading escalation
  — measures answering measures.
- Able Archer 83 (2–11 Nov 1983) "notionally moved forces from normal readiness
  through various alert phases to General Alert," using the real formal-alert
  vocabulary — so the chapter's General Alert / formal-alert-measure ladder is
  the correct period furniture, exercised that very fortnight.

The political sensitivity was acute: the exercise ran days before INF
(Pershing II / GLCM) deployment, amid the KAL 007 shoot-down aftermath and the
Bonn peace marches — the chapter's autumn (source: NSA sourcebook framing).

## Denmark / BALTAP specifics (koldkrig-online.dk, "Beredskabssystemet i
Danmark og NATO")

Denmark ran a **five-grade national readiness system**, mapped to NATO:

| Grade | Danish | NATO equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| I | Fredsberedskab | Peace readiness |
| II | Forstærket Fredsberedskab | State of Military Vigilance |
| III | Overgangsberedskab | Simple Alert |
| IV | Forstærket Overgangsberedskab | Reinforced Alert |
| V | Krigstilstand | General Alert / war status |

- **Express measures** (*Ekspresforanstaltninger*): **Alarm O** (damage
  expected within hours) and **Alarm S** (within minutes) — authorized
  *unilaterally by the Defense Command / Civil Defense without prior government
  approval*. This is the fast national channel beneath the NATO catalog.
- **Government-decision character.** Danish readiness changes are a *government*
  decision coordinated with NATO (Crisis Management Centre at the Foreign
  Ministry; Defense Command crisis centre at Vedbæk). Mine-laying in internal
  Danish waters (Søværnet), private-property requisition/expropriation, railway
  personnel recall (DSB), and civilian evacuation of anticipated combat zones
  (Zealand priority) are named national measures — matching the chapter's
  requisition, ferry, and mobilization texture. **Group IV had "soft" and
  "hard" variants** (soft = no premature demolitions clearing fields of fire) —
  a graduated, politically-priced choice exactly like the chapter's deferrals.
- **1952 Royal Directive**: any attack on Denmark itself is the mobilization
  order — direct jump to Group V. (Context for the ninety-hour clock beginning
  on the formal measure.)
- A wartime **Fuldmagtslov** (Full Powers Act) was pre-drafted for rapid
  Folketing passage — the legal backing for requisition/transport control.

## Verdict against drafts/02-handover.md — element by element

- **"a catalog... graduated and numbered, every measure agreed in advance by
  sixteen governments"** — **CONFIRMS.** Pre-agreed, standardized, alliance-wide
  menu (MC 67 series). "Sixteen governments" is *exactly right*: Spain became
  NATO's 16th member 30 May 1982, so Nov 1983 NATO had precisely 16 members.
  Keep the number; it is load-bearing and correct. (Soft edge: "numbered" is
  INFERENCE — the discretely-catalogued character is solid; literal numbering
  of 1983 measures unconfirmed. No change needed; "numbered" is defensible.)
- **"every numbered measure therefore a signal the moment it was invoked, with
  the enemy as the catalog's first reader"** — **CONFIRMS / EXTENDS.** This is
  the documented 1983 reality (Powell, Ustinov, Perroots). If a future line
  wants a real-world anchor, these three are it. The theme is not invented; it
  is the historiographical consensus of the war scare.
- **"The quiet list ran underneath the catalog"** (national measures priced in
  political noise, run without filable signals) — **CONFIRMS / EXTENDS.**
  Denmark's national system with unilaterally-authorized express measures below
  the NATO stages is the real structure. The chapter's "priced twice — readiness
  bought vs political noise made" is a fair dramatization of the genuine
  measure-as-signal cost and Danish reservation of national judgment.
- **"BALTAP WARNING ORDER" (6 Nov) preceding the formal alert measure** —
  **CONFIRMS shape.** A warning order from the joint command ahead of a
  Council-declared formal measure fits the graduated transition. No conflict.
- **"The alert measure the headquarters had rehearsed receiving for thirty
  years... 060955Z NOV 83"** — **CONFIRMS.** The formal alert system dates to
  the mid-1950s; ~30 years of rehearsal by 1983 is accurate, and WINTEX-CIMEX /
  Able Archer exercised exactly this receipt. Good.
- **Counter-surprise (held source's "Counter Surprise Alert" for Danish naval
  measures)** — **CONFIRMS with a naming refinement.** The real instrument is
  the **NATO Counter-Surprise Military System** (states ORANGE/SCARLET,
  SACEUR-authorized). Danish naval mine-laying sits as a national measure; if
  canon wants the alliance term, "counter-surprise" is correct, and
  ORANGE/SCARLET are the available state names.

**No CONTRADICTIONS at canon-breaking level.** Two soft edges (literal
