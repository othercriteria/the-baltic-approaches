## Q1 — BALTAP command architecture; COMLANDJUT nationality/rotation; COMBALTAP vs COMAIRBALTAP

**Verdict: CONFIRMS (strongly), with one precision flag on ch. 9's
"tired Danish airman."**

The founding structure is laid out in Ch. 9 ("The Creation of BALTAP"),
crowned by **Figure 9.3, "BALTAP organisation 1962"** (scrape renders the
chart as a run-together label string, but it is legible):

- **SACEUR → CINCNORTH → COMBALTAP**, then four co-equal sub-commands:
  **COMAIRBALTAP (Karup)**, **COMNAVBALTAP (Kiel-Holtenau)**,
  **COMLANDJUTLAND (Rendsborg = Rendsburg)**, **COMLANDZEALAND
  (Copenhagen)** — with the national forces hung beneath (Danish air/navy/
  army-Jutland/army-Zealand/army-Bornholm; West German air force in
  Schleswig-Holstein; West German navy; **German 6. PanserInf.Div**).
- **COMBALTAP was permanently Danish, with a German deputy and a Danish
  chief of staff** (stated twice: Ch. 9 final settlement, and Ch. 12
  "Organizing BALTAP"). CINCNORTH sat at Oslo/Kolsås; the ladder
  **COMLANDJUT → COMBALTAP (Karup) → CINCNORTH (Kolsås) → SACEUR** is
  exactly the request chain drafts/06 climbs. (The book does not print a
  nuclear-release SOP as such, but the command chain and the
  Danish-parliamentary-reservation-on-nuclear thread both match ch. 6's
  ladder.)
- **Rotation practice CONFIRMS canon.** The Oslo settlement (Ch. 9): three
  sub-commands "initially" Danish, but **only COMLANDZEALAND permanently
  Danish; the other three alternate German/Danish chiefs, their chiefs of
  staff preferably from the other country; if not, the senior staff member
  from the other country.** **COMAIRBALTAP was to be permanently Danish**;
  COMNAVBALTAP the German-leaning seat. So **COMLANDJUT is the alternating
  DA/GE army seat** — our October-1983 German→Danish change of command sits
  squarely inside the historical pattern. Ch. 6 restates it: COMLANDJUT
  "was located in Rendsburg... with alternating Danish-German top command,"
  and "the Danish dominance in military thinking was over."

**The COMBALTAP-vs-COMAIRBALTAP flag (ch. 9 "morning market").** These are
two different Danish three-stars, both at Karup:
- **COMBALTAP** is the joint theater commander — the man who actually
  *divides* scarce air among the three claimants (air defence / maritime /
  land). Historically he is a Danish **LTG or VADM** — an *army or navy*
  three-star. An **airman** COMBALTAP is not the attested pattern.
- **COMAIRBALTAP** is the (permanently Danish) air-component chief who
  advises and executes the air plan.

Ch. 9's "tired Danish voice... the theater commander... the air commander
for the Approaches" fuses the two offices into one airman. Dramatically
cheap and defensible (both Danish, both in the Karup bunker complex, and
`air-apportionment.md` already rules the apportionment act a
COMBALTAP-decision-advised-by-COMAIRBALTAP). But if we want it *technically*
clean, the officer who divides the fish is **COMBALTAP**, and giving him an
air-force cap is a deliberate compression, not an accuracy. Worth a
conscious ruling.

## Q2 — Air C2 / apportionment; "Operation HURRICANE," Figs 18.6–18.7

**Verdict: SILENT on the daily apportionment conference (confirms our
"inference-only" finding); CORRECTS the standing characterization of
HURRICANE.**

Two things to separate:

1. **The morning-market mechanism is not in this book.** There is no
   offensive-air bidding conference, no apportionment percentage, no ATOC/
   ASOC content. What the book *does* give (Ch. 18, "Danish naval tactics")
   is adjacent structure that confirms our three-customers frame from the
   side: air-recce tasking is COMAIRBALTAP's (in tension, **three routine
   plus three special daylight recce flights**, escalating by alert state —
   Ch. 14/15 recce table, cadence garbled); an **air-support priority
   ordering** — meet enemy air first, then hamper his forward movement,
   then direct support to ground and sea; the navy **requests air defence
   "if the air force had capacity"**; and reinforcement aircraft would
   "release some aircraft of the Danish Air Force for **TASMO**" (Ch. 15).
   So: air defence, maritime/TASMO, and the land battle really are three
   claimants on one thin air pool — but the book never stages the auction.
   This **confirms `air-apportionment.md`**: who-decides-the-daily-split
   stays inference. (It also re-confirms the Karup fighter command centre —
   **290 tracks / 50 simultaneous engagements**, C2 site at Vedbæk — Ch. 9.)

2. **HURRICANE is a naval missile ambush, not an air operation.** This
   corrects our shelf note, which framed "Op Hurricane" as an air/BAI
   contest over the MFG Tornados. Ch. 18 ("Operation HURRICANE"): it is the
   two navies' one meticulously **common-planned coordinated missile
   strike** against a major WAPA naval/invasion attack — Danes fire
   **HARPOON** (frigates, corvettes, FPBs), Germans **EXOCET** (FPBs) and
   **KORMORAN from TORNADO "if feasible."** All missiles timed to hit at
   once to shock the invasion force and break its momentum; Danish **MOBA
   OPS** sequences the shot (HARPOON first for range, then EXOCET); FPBs
   follow with torpedoes and guns. **Figs 18.6/18.7 are HURRICANE naval
   geometry diagrams, not air-allocation charts** — Fig 18.6 "Before
   HURRICANE" (minefields, defenders forward, red probing, invasion fleet
   far east, NATO subs recce at Bornholm); Fig 18.7 "HURRICANE initiated"
   (fleet closing but off the beaches; **Danish units hug the coast to spoof
   incoming missiles with coastal radar echoes**; after release, guns and
   torpedoes). Kormoran/Tornado is *one contributor "if feasible,"* not the
   plan's spine. **Consequence for us:** the MFG-Tornado "contested airframe"
   texture in `air-apportionment.md` is real but should not lean on
   HURRICANE as an *air*-apportionment case; HURRICANE is NAVBALTAP's
   fleet-killer, fired mostly from ships.

Period bonus (Ch. 18): the **nuclear alternative to HURRICANE — plan "SEP
BARD BELL (E)," SACEUR-authorized, executed outside Danish territorial
waters (around the Danish nuclear ban) — was TESTED against amphibious
units in September 1983**, weeks before our setting, with US/NAEW/German
Naval Air (Starfighters). Usable, and dated.

## Q3 — Strait barriers / minefield architecture (Fig 18.4)

**Verdict: CONFIRMS + EXTENDS richly.** (Ch. 18, "Danish naval tactics" →
Fig 18.4 "Mine fields around 1980.")

- **Whose mines, where.** **Danish *controlled* minefields at nine fixed
  places**: Middelgrundsfortet, Dragørfortet, Mandehoved (Stevns), Hårbølle
  Batteri (Grønsund), Østerhuse (northern Langeland), **Korsør**,
  Frederikshavn, Rønne, Neksø. Non-controlled highest-priority fields (two
  in the Sound, one each in Faxe and Køge Bays, Western Lolland, one–two at
  Omø); anti-invasion mines in Køge/Faxe Bays, at Møn, along Falster.
  **The Germans lay the southern fields**: east of Grønsund, **Fehmarn Belt
  and Fehmarn Sound**, and the **Bay of Lübeck**.
- **Laid when — the alert ladder.** Mine-laying is a **government
  decision**. Counter Surprise Alert: take mines aboard, go to waiting
  positions. Simple Alert: lay controlled fields (first Drogden and Stevns),
  uncontrolled at invasion beaches. Reinforced Alert: defensive mining in
  the Sound and **Storebælt** (and Lillebælt/Kattegat if needed).
- **Covered by what.** The **forts on Langeland and Stevns**, coastal
  batteries (Borgsted/Grønsund, Stevns), HAWK batteries, and escorting
  frigates/corvettes/FPBs; escorts also screen the layers from civilian
  infiltration.
- **CONFIRMS drafts/01's "Ships close straits; minefields close straits."**
  The corps holds the peninsula; the *sea* is closed by two navies' mines
  under fort/battery/air cover. Exactly Rahn's line.
- **ch. 15 raid geometry CONFIRMED.** The Great Belt approach is covered by
  the **Korsør controlled field + Østerhuse fort on N. Langeland + the
  Langeland/Stevns forts**, with Danish air defence (Starfighters high/low,
  Draken low) over the top — a fighter-screen-plus-island-batteries defence
  breaking a low raid in the Belt is authentic. Note too: **"the main Danish
  ferry routes" were explicitly out of bounds for emergency mining** — the
  straits stay minable *and* the ferry lift stays open, which is precisely
  ch. 15's situation.

## Q4 — Great Belt ferry fleet, Danish internal movement, UKMF strength

**Verdict: EXTENDS (mechanism) + RESOLVES the UKMF number; SILENT on the
discrete Belt-lift capacity figure.**

- **No bridge.** Ch. 5: "the strait of Storebælt divided the country in army
  terms; there was **no bridge or tunnel crossing until a long time after
  the Cold War**." Confirms ch. 15's "there had never been a bridge; there
  was a fleet."
- **The mechanism behind the lift.** Ch. 12: the army had no organic
  crossing capacity, so it **relied on ferries and merchant ships using
  ordinary harbours**; an American officer in the BALTAP staff wrote it up
  as **"Waterborne Movements," which became a standing NAVBALTAP order for
  moving troops from one part of Denmark to another.** That is the doctrinal
  home of our Zealand→Jutland lift. Mine-loading harbours named as Køge,
  Helsingør, **Korsør**; ferry routes kept clear of mining. **No discrete
  military throughput figure** (battalions/day, lane-metres) appears —
  consistent with `transport-1983.md` §9's open-tier negative. The book
  keeps the *concept*, not the number.
- **UKMF strength — the 29,000 / 15,000 / 10,000 tangle RESOLVES (Ch. 15,
  "Reinforcements in the 1980s"). They are different packages at different
  dates, not contradictions:**
  - **Full UKMF ≈ 29,000 men, 45 tanks**, land at **Esbjerg in 11 days**,
    **plus 52 aircraft to Karup after 4 days** — the whole force (division +
    air group + support). *(Numbers from running prose, not a table; still
    TO-VERIFY-PRINT.)*
  - **Land combat element = the 6th Field Force, 13,500 troops** — the pure
    manoeuvre slice (matches CIA-1984's "UK 1st Inf Bde 13,500"). Its move
    = 11,000 troops, 3,600 vehicles, 14,500 tons freight, 38 aircraft.
  - **Negotiated reduced-for-Zealand package = 10,000 men, 15 tanks, 36
    aircraft** (with pre-stocked depots).
  - **1987-planned "reduced UKMF" = 15,000 men**, supplies in depots by
    1988, **redeployed in 10 days by Danish ferry boats across the North
    Sea** (to Esbjerg). Separately, **US 9th Infantry Division = 27,000
    men**, "dedicated reinforcement," Esbjerg/Billund.
  - So: 29k = full force, 13.5k = fighting land element, 15k/10k = later
    reduced variants. All **TO-VERIFY-PRINT** (these live in scrape-garbled
    passages and one dropped table, Table 15.1).
- **Political texture worth mining:** British mistrust that Denmark used
  NATO reinforcement plans "as an excuse to reduce their national defences";
  the UKMF units at **7 days' notice (72 hrs in tension, 24 hrs for advance
  groups)** and largely **cadre/reservist logistics** ("a regular force...
  would be incapable of supporting itself in a sudden crisis"). Reinforcement
  is contingent and slow — reinforces our whole "Blue's divisions are
  promised, not booked" thread.

## Q5 — NEPS (North European Pipeline System)

**Verdict: CONFIRMS + EXTENDS (adds a wartime-vulnerability datapoint).**

- **The line (Ch. 6, Fig 6.3 "The NEPS pipeline for Danish airfields").**
  Initiated **1955**; **~140 miles**, linking the **Jutland airfields to
  fuel stocks**; carries several POL types (colour-coded on the figure);
  **later extended to the German airfields**. A **Skrydstrup→Danish-border**
  extension for **40,000 m³ of petrol** was a NATO investment. **Frederikshavn**
  (north Jutland) is the northern head/main harbour, with the big north-
  Jutland stores. This confirms and dates `transport-1983.md` §10
  (Frederikshavn head, Jutland airfields, southern extension to the German
  bases).
- **Wartime vulnerability — a new plant.** Ch. 13's exercise **DALGAS**:
  conventional air strikes destroy a power station, several oil depots, **and
  "the NATO-pipe line in Frederikshavn."** And a BALTAP nuclear/target list
  (Ch. 15/appendix) counts **"the NATO oil delivery system" plus POL depots
  at Plön, Heide, Brunsbüttelkoog** among the targets. **The pipeline is
  modeled as a struck target in NATO's own exercises.** This does not
  contradict canon — it *sharpens* it: since the line is obviously
  strikeable, RED's leaving the *in-reach pump stations* untouched
  (drafts/06 plant; drafts/14 payoff) reads as a **deliberate choice**, not
  an oversight. No ripple to the fuel picture.

## Q6 — German Territorial Army / Heimatschutz vs LANDJUT (corps OPCON of territorials in war)

**Verdict: EXTENDS / CONFIRMS-plausible.** The canon mechanism (a mid-war
order handing the corps its district companies) is exactly what the book's
sources say war *requires* — though Bogason doesn't quote a specific 1980s
transfer order.

- **Default state:** in the 1970 orbat (Ch. 12), the Schleswig-Holstein
  ground force is "the sixth armoured grenadier division, **and there was a
  local home guard brigade (not under NATO command)**." So the
  Heimatschutzbrigade sits *outside* the NATO/corps chain by default —
  matching `landjut-front.md`.
- **The war requirement:** the **HOLD FAST 1960** after-action (Ch. 6,
  COMLANDSCHLESWIG-HOLSTEIN's Erfahrungsbericht) states that **all forces in
  the area had to be under the tactical command of the joint HQ**, and
  specifically that **the German home guard (Territorialheer) had to be
  placed under the joint HQ — "as the Danish home guard was in Denmark" —
  and that HQ had to be the NATO command.** The stated reason is pure
  drafts-material: without it, the **Kiel-Canal crossings bottleneck and
  civilian-fugitive / military clashes** become unmanageable when the
  6th Division retires across the canal. That is the doctrinal warrant for
  our order. Bogason gives the *principle*, not an 1980s order text (that
  detail sits in the Dragoner/German-territorial sources already logged in
  `landjut-front.md`).

## Q7 — LANDJUT HQ at Rendsburg; wartime/alternate HQ practice

**Verdict: CONFIRMS Rendsburg; SILENT on any LANDJUT displacement to Hohn.**

- **Rendsburg CONFIRMED** twice: Fig 9.3 seats COMLANDJUTLAND at "Rendsborg";
  Ch. 6 states "the staff of the western army component, COMLANDJUT... was
  located in **Rendsburg**, south of the Danish border." (Also confirms the
  90-hour Danish-division relocation clock — Helms 1975 — and the "English
  working language / 60% peacetime manning to function from alert day"
  texture, Ch. 6.)
- **Hohn** appears **only as a Luftwaffe airfield** (Ch. 8): transport wing
  (18 aircraft) at Hohn, with a G-91 wing "forward-located to Hohn in times
  of crisis." **The book says nothing about LANDJUT displacing to a dispersed
  wartime HQ at Hohn** — that remains our invention, plausibly hung on a real
  nearby airfield (and the real "Hohn gap" terrain of drafts/01), but
  **unattested here.** The only war-HQ practice the book documents is at the
  *theater* level — BALTAP took an airfield building at **Karup** "with a
  promise to get war HQ in a bunker," plus a C2 site at **Vedbæk** (Ch. 12).
  Corps wartime-HQ practice: SILENT.
