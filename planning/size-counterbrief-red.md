## 1. The prosecution

### 1.1 The evidence you filed yourself, the same day you made the cut

The First Clash distillation (reference/first-clash.md) — commissioned by this project as its apparatus model — returned two findings the sizing brief did not metabolize:

- Ranked against The Goal's own didactic hierarchy, First Clash's **Soviet planning POV is that book's best teaching engine**: "adversarial articulation (strong, the book's best engine): the Soviet planning POV — Oblensky, Podduyev, Samsonov reasoning through echelon, momentum, bypass-vs-reduce — teaches doctrine by watching commanders decide *why*. The closest thing to a Socratic dialectic in the book."
- **Oblensky arguing Samsonov off the daylight attack is the one near-Socratic scene in 211 pages** — the single moment the genre's closest structural cousin reaches the middle of the hierarchy. It happens in the enemy's tent.

The back-half report even flagged the tension explicitly: "red interiority is currently NOT budgeted at 55k — this finding is the strongest argument the counter-brief could make against that cut." I am that counter-brief. Note also what the hierarchy says: in The Goal's own didactic ranking (goal-structure-map.md, Synthesis), **adversarial articulation is #2** — above Socratic dialogue with the mentor (#3). The sizing brief spends lavishly on engine #3 (6–8 Jonah contacts) and #1 (the exercise, the models), and cuts the #2 engine to zero on-page presence outside one late scene. That is a strange portfolio for a book whose whole bet is didactic efficiency per word.

### 1.2 Red is already a Goal character — the wargame built him and the plan won't seat him

This is not "add an enemy for texture." The project's own instruments have already written red as the second Goldratt protagonist, in the source text's exact idiom:

- **Red believes its plan** (setting-time.md, criterion 7): the 1983 offensive plan of record makes red "the overconfident throughput-forecaster — on-theme." That phrase is in canon-adjacent planning text. The overconfident throughput-forecaster is a *Goal* character type — he is Rogo-before-Jonah, or more precisely Hilton Smyth with a Front.
- **Red's staff keeps a misleading-metrics ledger daily.** The v18 instruments (notes/wargaming-findings.md) give red `plan_kmd` (the 10 km/d conventional-track norm from its own 1967 directive) and `plan_lag` — "days red stands behind its own timeline — the norm-shortfall its staff measures against the track it declined." A staff measuring itself daily against a baseline derived from the option it refused, while its plan written at 30–50 km/d meets ground that pays 3–10: **red's plan_lag column is Rogo's efficiency reports.** The findings log itself says a red plan written at 30 meeting ground that pays 10 "is a scene." The wargame has manufactured, at zero marginal cost, the one thing the metrics-engine ("the measurement system, played straight, is the book's only antagonist") cannot show from blue's side: what it looks like to go on believing.
- **The nbc-conceit already narrates red interiority in planning voice**: "red's staff measures its shortfall against the nuclear norm line it didn't take… The overconfident plant manager keeps declining the forbidden shortcut because taking it burns the plant down; that IS the Goal frame absorbing the bomb" (nbc-conceit.md §4). The design documents keep writing red scenes. The chapter plan refuses to.

### 1.3 The locked scene has no floor under it

The DK ruling of 2026-07-23 locked red's week-3 staff-argument — the strikes requested, the refusal or silence returned — as "red's half of the one earned scene," and re-anchored the envelope exit (day ~12–13, 30/30 runs) as the third act's opening bell. Prosecute the arithmetic of that scene as currently budgeted:

It is a ~2.5k scene, at roughly day 17+, set in a room the reader has never entered, among officers the reader has never met, arguing about a metric (plan_lag against the declined timeline) the reader has only encountered as G-2 paraphrase, whose stakes (the ОМП bundling, the limited-aims logic) were established once, in one blue seminar, some ten chapters and two weeks of story earlier. The book's most dangerous single scene — the one where the conceit either holds or reads as authorial luck — is being asked to cold-open its own POV, its own cast, its own doctrine-voice, *and* pay its dramatic payload, in one visit. First Clash's Oblensky scene works because chapters 2 and 4 built Samsonov's dread and Oblensky's competence first. A one-scene red is not a smaller version of that; it is a different and worse device — a cameo carrying an act break.

### 1.4 The anti-model vacancy

The Goal's metrics engine is "re-fought at every level," and one of its load-bearing fixtures is **Hilton Smyth, the sincere anti-model whose indicators improve while his bottom line sinks** — on-page repeatedly, so the reader watches belief-in-the-instruments as an ongoing living error, not a starting condition. Our blue staff cannot supply a Smyth: the protagonist is curing himself, and the ensemble converges on the method. The only sincere believer left standing in week 3 — the only character whose instruments keep telling him he's winning while his campaign culminates — is red. Cut red's interior and the book's second-half metrics engine has no anti-model, only a memory of one. (A blue careerist can carry cynicism; he cannot carry *sincere* instrument-faith without contradicting the ensemble's arc.)

## 2. The defense, steelmanned

The cut is not lazy; its best case is strong and mostly the project's own findings:

1. **The G-2-mediated red is itself the book's argument.** The v17 finding is the most original thing in the plan: "the G-2's architecture picture — not doctrine — decides the G-3's air question." The G-2 turn — reading the opponent's Herbie *by inference* — is a designated station and arguably the book's freshest payload. Give the reader direct red access and the G-2's epistemic drama deflates: the reader grades the G-2 against an answer key, and the "enemy's constraint" station degrades from discovery to confirmation. The book would be *showing* what it exists to teach you to *infer*.
2. **Reader-contract clause 2 wants the reader inside blue's confusion.** The misleading-metrics engine requires that the reader feel why a competent officer believes the instruments (reader-contract.md). The reader-ahead mechanism is calibrated in inches — the reader solves each problem *slightly* before the staff, from staged evidence. Omniscient red access is a different machine: it hands the reader miles for free. If the reader knows red's state, blue's instruments cannot mislead the reader, and the book's only antagonist is disarmed.
3. **The books that leaned on the enemy did so as compensation.** Red Army had no Jonah, no discovery arc, no metric inversion — adversarial articulation was the *only* engine it had (goal-likes.md: "didactic in effect, not in structure"). First Clash likewise: no mentor, no protagonist, so the Soviet tent was the only place reasoning could happen aloud. We have engines #1 and #3 fully manned. The precedents prove the enemy *can* teach, not that a book with a Jonah *needs* him to.
4. **The Goal's own POV discipline is single-vantage.** No chapter sits inside Hilton Smyth or division HQ without Alex. Smyth works as an anti-model *seen from outside* — his indicators arrive as reports and meetings. The form the project is transposing never bought interiority for its antagonist, and it is the best-selling didactic novel ever written.
5. **55k is tight and the fixed costs are real.** Ensemble establishment for one binational staff is already ~8–10k. A red room is a second ensemble: names, doctrine-voice, Soviet staff texture, a register that must not read as Clancy's Russians — new fixed cost, new failure surface (the Clancy-pastiche detector exists for a reason), superlinear critique load. §4a's undershoot warning cuts at the thread's throat: three "cheap" beats each quietly 15% thin plus their displacement effects and a 55k plan is bleeding.

## 3. The reconciliation space

The defense's points 1 and 2 are about **ground-truth access**, not interiority as such. That distinction is the whole reconciliation, and it can be held as a drafting rule:

**Rule: red scenes show red's reasoning, never blue's answer key — because red doesn't have one either.** What is blue confused about? Its own attrition state, its constraint, red's architecture and timing. A red beat written under the rule shows red's *plan*, red's *norms*, red's *self-report* — and red's staff is itself misinformed about its own forward state (period-honest: Soviet reporting culture ran on norm-compliance fiction; the same v17 physics that fooled the IC's two-phase timing read fools red's own headquarters slower and differently). The reader who visits red's room does not learn where the truth is; he watches a *second* staff misled by its own instruments. That does not deflate the misleading-metrics engine — it **doubles** it, and it upgrades the meta-station: the book's final epistemology ("whose consumption model is the argument standing on") lands harder when the reader has seen both consumption models fail their owners. The G-2 turn survives intact, because what the G-2 must infer — red's architecture, red's constraint — is exactly what red's own room is lying to itself about; the reader still has to do the G-2's work, now with the extra vertigo of having seen the enemy's dashboard and *still* not knowing the ground truth.

Supporting disciplines, all cheap:
- **Placement after commitment** (built into R3): each red beat sits after blue has acted on its wrong picture, so irony arrives as tragedy, never as spoiler. Beat 2 cannot warn the reader off a blue mistake that has already been made.
- **One room, no descent**: no conscripts, no Tursov's Nina, no rank-ladder camera (that is First Clash's device for a cast we can't afford). Two named officers and a map. This caps the second-ensemble fixed cost at the price of a Jonah contact, not a staff.
- **The apparatus carries red's data for free**: the wargame annex prints red's daily log with the plan_lag column; a captured operations-order facsimile is First Clash-proven carriage. Under the §0 ruling this costs zero narrative words. It cannot *replace* the beats (First Clash's other proven finding: doctrine-by-exhibit is inert) — but it means the beats never spend words on numbers.
- **Fallback short of interiority** (if the artifact test fails): Design R2 — red's voice reaches the reader only through mediated channels (COMINT fragments, a prisoner, the captured order), ~1–1.5k spread across Part II. Preserves clause 2 absolutely; buys voice but not the #2 engine. It is the floor, not the ask.

## 4. Recommendation — brief, not verdict

1. **Amend §5's cut language**, narrowly. Keep dead: any red POV *thread*, any red character arc, any red ground-truth disclosure, Red Army-scale mosaic. Replace "any red-interiority chapters" with: *red interiority is confined to three beats of one room (~4.5–5k net new, R3 design), written under the reasoning-not-reconnaissance rule, placed after blue's commitments; the week-3 scene is beat 3 of this strand, not a cold open.* Concession recorded: the G-2 remains the dominant red channel; the G-2-turn station is untouched; red never gets a discovery arc — red is the book's anti-model precisely because he *doesn't* learn in time.
2. **Priority ruling for the overflow direction**: if Phase-1 outlining opens the 55→60k door, the red beats stand ahead of the §4a interleaves in the queue — they buy the didactic hierarchy's #2 engine; the interleaves buy visibility.
3. **The cheap artifact demanded before any canon ruling**: write **beat 2 twice** — once as the red-interior scene under the rule, once as the identical content G-2-mediated (intercept fragments + the estimate brief). Give both to blind truncated readers (the instrument roster already names them) with two questions: *what did red's staff believe, and why were they wrong?* and *do you know anything about the true situation that the corps staff doesn't?* The cut survives only if the mediated version teaches as well AND the interior version leaks ground truth. My prediction is it fails the first test and passes the second — but that is what the artifact is for.
4. **Free regardless of ruling**: queue the wargame annex's red daily log (plan_lag column) and one captured-order facsimile as apparatus. Zero narrative cost under §0; do it in any variant.
