# The Polish Maritime/Coastal Front — the 1960s–70s primary-source layer

*Distilled 2026-07-22 from the WP-primary cluster held on the shelf
(`reference/pdf/php-*`, `wilson-bange-*`), closing the standing
"distillation owed — language" debt. This is the 1960s–70s document
layer UNDER the mid-80s architecture already established in
`reference/oob-verification.md` and `reference/zealand-landing.md`:
what the plans and directives themselves say, in Polish and Russian,
before the 1985 defensive turn reshaped the picture.*

**Discipline — read before using.**

- **These are RED's own operational documents.** Blue characters
  cannot know their contents. Every use here is for us (authorial
  continuity) and for red-side modelling only — a red staff would
  work from these; a blue G-2 would not have them. (The whole point
  of `oob-verification.md`'s "period-knowable vs archive-revealed"
  section: a 1980s LANDJUT G-2 could count red divisions, not read
  red directives.)
- **Two source registers, kept separate.** (a) *Document text* — the
  directives themselves (1961, 1967) and the ČSLA plan of 1964 —
  quoted or paraphrased with facsimile-page cites; these are period
  RED intent. (b) *Post-Cold-War scholarship* — Mastny, Luňák, Odom
  (1964 dossier) and Bange (SOYUZ/SHCHIT) — cited as commentary;
  their judgements are ours to use, never a character's.
- **Facsimile status.** The 1961 and 1967 directives are *exercise*
  operational directives (Ćwiczenie "Burza"; Ćwiczenia "Lato-67"),
  not the sealed war plan. Per Luňák's own caution on ČSLA exercise
  designs — they "cannot be considered an exact reflection of
  operational planning" but track it closely — treat them as the
  Coastal Front's plan rendered in exercise form. The 1964 ČSLA
  document is the one *actual* sharp plan text in the cluster (a
  different axis — see below).
- **Reading defeats are flagged, not guessed.** The scans are
  degraded photostats; German/Danish/Dutch place names are
  transliterated through Polish (1961) or Russian (1967) and many are
  warped. Uncertain readings carry [?].

## 1. The 1961 directive — Front Nadmorski, ex. "Burza" (Storm)

*Cites are to the facsimile as bound in `php-maritime-front-directive-1961.pdf`;
PDF p.1 is the archival cover, PDF p.2 = facsimile p.1, etc. Archive:
Archiwum Instytucji Centralnych MON w Modlinie, Główny Inspektorat
Szkolenia Bojowego, spis 18/91/30. Signed gen. dyw. Zygmunt
**Duszyński** (Front cdr), gen. bryg. Jan **Śliwiński** (CoS), gen.
bryg. Zdzisław **Bobecki** (ops chief) [PDF p.7].*

**Force composition.** Front Nadmorski = **50, 51, 52 Armies + 53
Air Army + 17 Airborne Division** [fac. p.1]. Two echelons: **50 A +
51 A** first, **52 A** second [fac. p.2–3]. Each army fielded ~5
divisions (e.g. 50 A = 19, 23 mech / 34 inf / 27, 31 tank; 51 A = 13,
22 mech / 15 inf / 16, 21 tank) [fac. p.3–4], each with an organic
rocket brigade (BROT), a heavy rocket brigade (RRBT) and an army
rocket-artillery regiment (ardp).

**Warning time (the opening premise).** "The beginning of aggressive
offensive actions by NATO armed forces is possible **within one to
two days** (*w ciągu jednej-dwóch dób*)" [fac. p.1]. The Front then
"crosses the Odra... and from the march moves to offensive actions" —
i.e. the plan is offensive-from-the-outset, the NATO attack a bare
trigger. (Directly relevant to `oob-verification.md`'s later
"defensive-first" contrast, and to the 90-hour-clock/warning-time
material in `landjut-front.md`.)

**Axes and objectives.**
- **Front main strike:** NEUSTRELITZ → PRITZWALK → WITTENBERGE →
  SOLTAU → ENSCHEDE → "MALIN" [Mechelen?] — i.e. across the north
  German plain toward the **Netherlands** [fac. p.1].
- **The Danish job is one army:** "with the forces of one army in
  cooperation with the Baltic Fleet, conducts an operation on the
  Jutland Peninsula and the Danish Islands **with the aim of
  completely eliminating Denmark from the war**" [fac. p.1]. That
  army is **50 A**, on the axis NEUBRANDENBURG → SCHWERIN →
  NEUMÜNSTER → FLENSBURG → Fredericia [fac. p.3]: crush NATO in
  **Schleswig-Holstein**, force the Elbe–Trave Canal bypassing
  Hamburg from the north, take **Lübeck and Kiel** with the Baltic
  Fleet, **seize the Kiel Canal crossings intact (locks not to be
  destroyed)**, breach the permanent fortifications on the
  German-Danish border, reach Aabenraa–Toftlund–Brede, then take Fyn
  ("Fiumen") and northern Jutland by D5–6.
- **Amphibious/air-landing on Zealand:** the **17th Airborne
  Division lands on D1 on Zealand in the region of Køge**, acting
  with the navy toward **Copenhagen**; the Baltic Fleet with 50 A
  and 17 DPD seizes Zealand, the Danish ports and the Straits,
  "ensuring the exit to the North Sea" [fac. p.2, p.4]. *This is the
  1961 ancestor of the whole Zealand-landing thread in
  `zealand-landing.md` — airborne div on D1 at Køge, exactly the
  bay-complex the 1977 plan of record later used.*

**Timeline norms.** D3 → Farge/Bremen line (Immediate task); D5–6 →
the North Sea coast with Wilhelmshaven, IJmuiden, Rotterdam and the
Antwerp–Leuven[?]–Tienen[?] line, "taking Holland out of the war"
(Further task) [fac. p.1–2]. Fast, single-tempo, nuclear-underwritten.

**Nuclear fire plan.** Front rocket troops allotted **93 missiles
for the operation** [fac. p.2 table], broken by type and yield:
tactical 3KT×10, 10KT×21, 20KT×12; operational 10KT×9, 20KT×9,
40KT×21; front 200KT×6, 500KT×5. Split **59 for the Immediate task,
34 for the Further task** [fac. p.3 table]. Main nuclear effort: kill
the enemy's own nuclear stocks and delivery means and the Northern
Army Group main body; suppress the German-Danish border
fortifications and the western-Baltic/North-Sea naval bases; enable
the air and sea landings [fac. p.2].

**Command relationship.** A **Polish** Front (Polish signatures
throughout), operating between neighbouring "Central Front" and
"Western Front" and with the Baltic Fleet; the Front controls its own
tactical/operational rocket troops but the architecture is a Soviet
coalition one (see §3 for how these directives are generated).

## 2. The 1967 directive — Coastal Front, ex. "Lato-67" (Summer-67)

*Cites to `php-maritime-front-directive-1967.pdf`; PDF p.1 = archival
cover (spis 18/91/227, "5 kart, język rosyjski"), PDF p.2 = fac. p.1,
etc. The document is a Russian-language extract. Signed gen. broni
Jerzy **Bordziłowski** (Coastal Front cdr), gen. dyw. Tadeusz
**Tuczapski** (CoS) [fac. p.5]. Same front, six years on, renamed
Приморский Фронт and with a wartime numbering.*

**Force composition — changed.** Coastal Front = **3, 5, 7, 12
Armies + 21st Tank Army** [fac. p.1, p.3]. Two echelons: **I echelon
3, 5, 12 A + 21 TA; II echelon 7 A; reserve 5 mech div** [fac. p.3].
Note the acquisition of a **tank army (21 TA)** and the wartime
army-numbering (3/5/7/12 vs the 1961 exercise's 50/51/52) — the same
formation set, re-dressed.

**A defensive preamble, then the counteroffensive.** The 1967
scenario opens with **NATO attacking first** (Central & Northern Army
Groups on the offensive from 05.00 31.5, WMD held back) and the
Coastal Front's forward armies (**12 A + 21 TA**) *defending*, halting
the enemy in their own zone; **at dawn 2.6 the Front commits 3 & 5 A
and goes over to the offensive** [fac. p.2–3]. This meet-then-counter
shape is a covering battle, not a strategic defensive posture — but
it is worth logging that a defence-first *exercise* preamble exists on
this axis as early as 1967, two decades before the 1985 strategic
defensive turn in `oob-verification.md`.

**Axes and objectives.**
- **Front main strike:** NEURUPPIN → HANNOVER → OSTEND [fac. p.3–4]
  — again toward the Low Countries, further south than 1961's
  Neustrelitz start.
- **Second strike (our axis):** SCHWERIN → LÜBECK → "**along the
  Jutland Peninsula**," tied to "**landing operations in the area of
  the Danish straits**" [fac. p.3]. "Striking with the forces of
  **three armies on the coastal direction and one army on the Jutland
  direction**" [fac. p.3] — the one-army-for-Jutland allocation is
  stable from 1961.
- **Named NATO defenders on the SH/Jutland axis:** the Front is to
  destroy "NATO troops in Schleswig-Holstein — **6th mech div and the
  Jutland inf div**" (i.e. 6. PzGrenDiv + the Danish Jutland Division)
  and the corps groupings I AK/FRG/, I AK/Br/, I AK/Belg/, I AK/Ger/
  [fac. p.3]. *The document names the exact blue formations
  `landjut-front.md` establishes for LANDJUT — the Bundeswehr div and
  the Danish div — as its objective, in 1967.*
- **Zealand:** seized "by the 10-11th day" [fac. p.4]; the whole of
  Holland, Belgium and Denmark by day 10-13 (with nuclear) / 14-18
  (without) [fac. p.4].

**Timeline norms — now dual (nuclear vs conventional).** The 1967
directive gives every phase line a *paired* deadline: seize northern
FRG + eastern Holland by **day 7-8 with WMD / day 10-11 without**;
Holland/Belgium/Denmark by **day 10-13 / 14-18** [fac. p.3–4]. This
two-track grammar — the same objective reached slower if nuclear
release is withheld — is exactly the "flexible response à la Warsaw
Pact" the 1964 dossier dates to c.1964 (see §3). It is *not* in the
1961 directive, which is single-tempo. **This is the clearest
document-level change 1961→1967.**

**Nuclear fire plan — two tiers.**
- **Strategic tier, Soviet-controlled:** "In case of the start of
  atomic war, the **Supreme Command of the Unified Armed Forces**
  delivers *in favour of* the Coastal Front **57 nuclear strikes by
  strategic means, total yield 3200 KT**" [fac. p.2]. Named target
  sets: communication junctions (Celle, Hannover, Nienburg, Minden,
  Verden, Münster, Wesel, Emmerich, Arnhem, Rotterdam, Brussels,
  Flensburg, Vejle, **Copenhagen**); ports (Holbæk, Eckernförde,
  Svendborg, Odense, Fredericia, Haderslev, Aalborg, Frederikshavn,
  Husum, Cuxhaven, Wesermünde, Wilhelmshaven, Emden, Den Helder, Den
  Haag, Vlissingen, Ostend); **airfields (Aalborg, Karup, Odense,
  Flensburg, Schleswig, Oldenburg, Osnabrück, ... Utrecht area)** —
  note **Karup**, i.e. BALTAP HQ, is a named nuclear DGZ; **Nike &
  Hawk SAM batteries** around Store Heddinge / Køge / Hillerød /
  Copenhagen (the Zealand air-defence belt); ammunition and WMD
  depots; and the **Lingen nuclear reactor** [fac. p.2].
- **Front/army tier:** one coastal-direction army (CP Karow; tank
  divisions **61, 63, 64, 65** [?]) is allotted **6 strategic strikes
  + 46 of its own nuclear strikes, total 2065 KT, + 30 chemical
  strikes; of these 17 nuclear strikes at 729 KT in the first strike**
  [fac. p.4]. *The exact attribution (front-wide vs this one army) is
  ambiguous in the degraded text — flag [?]; the order of magnitude
  is not in doubt.*

**Command relationship — sharper than 1961.** The Coastal Front is
"**subordinated until the moment of reaching the western border of
the GDR**," with a part of the 1st Air Army (Western Front) and the
**NVA GDR national air-defence troops temporarily attached** during
its transit of GDR territory [fac. p.2]. So: a Polish front marching
through Mecklenburg picks up GDR air defence + Soviet air support en
route, under a higher (Soviet-led Unified) command; **strategic
nuclear release sits at the Unified Command, not the front** — the
front *receives* 57 strikes "in its favour," it does not fire them.
(This is the 1967 ancestor of the mid-80s "Polish front deploying
through Mecklenburg" architecture in `oob-verification.md`.)

## 3. The 1964 dossier — the frame (a different axis, the same grammar)

*`php-warsaw-pact-war-plans-1964-dossier.pdf` has a clean text layer;
cites by PDF page. The plan itself is the **Czechoslovak Front** —
Prague→Nuremberg→Stuttgart→Dijon→**Lyon by ~D9**, the southern axis —
so it is **not** our front. Its use is as the only complete sharp
war-plan text in the cluster: it shows how a WP front directive is
built, and it carries the scholarly frame (plan vs exercise vs
aspiration).*

**The plan text (document register).** ČSLA Front = 1st + 4th Armies
(first echelon) + 10th Air Army + reserves, one-echelon structure
with divisions peeling into reserve as they mobilise [PDF pp.16–22].
Approved 14 Oct 1964, signed Lomský/Rytíř/Vitanovský; valid "until at
least 1968 and probably much later" [PDF p.14]. Nuclear arithmetic:
**131 warheads total (96 missiles + 35 bombs); first strike 41;
Immediate task 29; Subsequent task 49; 12 in reserve** [PDF p.17].
Release rule, stated in the plan itself: "**Use of special ammunition
— only with permission of the Supreme Commander of the Unified Armed
Forces**" [PDF pp.19–20]. Air army (57th) chops in from the Carpathian
MD on D1 [PDF p.18]. Phase lines D1/D2/D4, Rhine crossing, Langres–
Besançon by **D7–8**, then Lyon [PDF p.17].

**How the directive was generated (Štěpánek interview, register:
witness testimony).** Front plans "came about as a result of
directives from Moscow," were worked up locally, "took back to
Moscow, where they looked it over, endorsed it... Changes were made
right there on the spot" [PDF pp.13, 25]. The Warsaw Pact staff was
"30 officers and generals, exclusively Soviet; from the other armies
... only ever one single representative, a liaison officer" —
decisions "made... by the General Staff of the Soviet Army, whether
they liked it or not" [PDF p.26]. The objective (Dijon/Lyon) "did not
change... at least not until 1968"; plans were "updated," not
rewritten [PDF p.26]. **This is the command-relationship model the two
Polish directives sit inside**: nominally a Polish front, actually a
Soviet-authored, Soviet-approved, Soviet-nuclear-release instrument.

**Plan vs exercise vs aspiration (scholarship register — ours).**
- **Mastny:** "no reason to doubt that the plan was meant to be
  implemented in case of war"; but the design "presupposed that...
  Warsaw Pact troops [would march] unscathed through the wasteland" —
  "believing in fairy tales" [PDF pp.4, 6]. Serious *intent*,
  fantastical *physics*.
- **Luňák:** the Lyon-by-D9 ambition "did not appear overnight" —
  exercise phase-lines crept west and the tempo tightened across
  1958–64 [PDF pp.11–12]; "flexible response à la Warsaw Pact" (a
  conventional *option*, nuclear held back) entered ČSLA exercises in
  1964, but the sharp plan still "assumes the war will start with a
  massive nuclear strike by the West" [PDF p.12] — i.e. the dual
  conventional/nuclear timeline (which we then see live in the 1967
  Polish directive) was brand-new in 1964.
- **Odom (US LG, ret.):** the plan's boldness is credible — Soviet
  forces "would have acted according to it" — but flags two doubts:
  whether *non-Soviet* WP armies had the armour/trucks to execute the
  deep drive, and whether the early-60s nuclear inventory could even
  supply small-yield weapons (hence the 1961/64 plans lean on *large*
  yields, 20 KT+) [PDF pp.30–32]. Odom also notes recovered **East
  German** war plans "dictated surprisingly large numbers of nuclear
  weapons for use in the first few days... as many as 40 warheads to
  be dropped in the Hamburg vicinity" [PDF p.31] — a
  cross-confirmation of the density the Polish directives show on the
  SH axis.

## 4. Bange — SOYUZ-75, SOYUZ-81/83, SHCHIT-88 (carrying the axis to the late period)

*`wilson-bange-soyuz75-shchit88.pdf`, clean text; cites by PDF page.
Bange (MGFA) is post-Cold-War scholarship — our register, not a
character's. The Polish exercise documents he comments on come from
the IPN archive in Warsaw.*

**The continuity claim (the load-bearing one).** From 1955 the Polish
"Coastal Front" was "meant to attack Northern Germany and then move on
to Jutland... and from 1955 onwards... through Northern Germany towards
the Dutch border," its main axis running "just north and in parallel
to the 2nd Guards Tank Army of the Soviets (later the 5th Army made up
from East German troops)" — a "double-spearheaded scheme" [PDF p.1].
This "general idea... was regularly adopted to latest developments...
throughout the 1960s, 1970s and the early 1980s"; **"adaptation and
refinement rather than the drawing up of completely new plans"** to
the mid-80s [PDF p.1]. **The 1961 and 1967 directives we hold are two
snapshots of exactly this continuously-refined plan.** Deim's claim
(via Wenzke): Soviet planners deliberately posed Polish troops against
*West German* units and kept East German troops off the Bundeswehr
[PDF p.1] — consistent with the 1967 directive naming 6. PzGrenDiv +
the Jutland Division as the Polish Coastal Front's objective (§2).

**SOYUZ-75 (March 1975).** "Neither new nor unique" — it "continued
and further developed" the same scheme toward the Netherlands and
"perhaps across the Rhine" [PDF p.3]. New *details*: first heavy
attention to radio-electronic warfare; first computer-assisted SOYUZ;
and a **shocking density of nuclear use on both sides** — NATO ADMs
(atomic demolition munitions) hold up the WP second echelon, NATO
counter-attacks after five days, and "the further advance of the
Polish Front is prepared through a massive nuclear attack — paving its
way through the 1st [West German] Army Corps" [PDF pp.3–4]. Bange
places SOYUZ-75 as "representative — of the long-term development of
strategic-operational thought" [PDF p.4].

**SOYUZ-81 / SOYUZ-83 (the offensive climax).** SOYUZ-81 (Mar 1981):
first treatment of "selective and increasing use of nuclear weapons
... broken down by targets and delivery means"; first test of
Ogarkov's operational-manoeuvre-group concept (OMG task: "destruction
of rockets, command posts, reserves and taking air ports") [PDF p.4].
**SOYUZ-83 (30 May–6 Jun 1983)** = the peak: WP goes offensive "from
the beginning" on a "comprehensive scale"; result recorded as the
Coastal Front reaching **Enschede via Hannover** and a second thrust
Eisenach→Koblenz, a 240 km drive "in only nine days — more and
considerably quicker than in any other exercise before"; "the lines of
attack remained very much as described in the Polish 'plan' of 1969
and in SOYUZ-75" [PDF pp.5–6].

**The defensive turn (1984/85) and SHCHIT-88.** Lautsch — the 5th-Army
planning officer of `oob-verification.md` — "had to reverse his
utterly offensive plans from 1983 into a defensive scenario in
1984/85"; Akhromeev advised East Berlin toward defensive planning in
Dec 1984; the GDR refocused by Oct 1985, *ahead* of Gorbachev's public
turn [PDF pp.6–7]. But **Moszumański dates the Polish Coastal Front's
offensive rationale as "in principle unchanged" until summer 1986**,
and an **August 1986 Polish General Staff directive still detailed the
offensive Coastal-Front attack** in N Germany and Jutland [PDF pp.6–7].
SHCHIT-88 (June 1988) is the first fully defensive Polish scenario —
and folds Poland's domestic unrest into the war-game [PDF pp.2, 7].
Bange's four candidate explanations for the Polish lag (trust, doctrine
adherence, sharp-plan vs briefing-plan, allegiance-signalling) are on
PDF pp.7–8.

**Where the 1960s material is still the live architecture for the
setting.** The *shape* is stable across the whole span and is
identical to what `oob-verification.md` reconstructs for the mid-80s:
a **Polish front driving the north-German plain toward the Low
Countries, with one army peeling off Schwerin–Lübeck onto the
SH/Jutland axis, a Baltic-Fleet + airborne/naval landing taking
Zealand and the Straits, the whole underwritten by heavy nuclear
fires and running through GDR (Mecklenburg) territory under Soviet
coalition command.** That skeleton is in the 1961 directive and never
leaves.

**Where superseded — and the setting-year hinge.** Two things change
late and are *setting-year-coupled* (cf. `planning/setting-time.md`):
1. **The 1985 strategic defensive turn** (Lautsch/Akhromeev) —
   defence-first, restore-the-border. The 1967 directive's defensive
   *preamble* is only a covering battle, not this.
2. **The Zealand handover** to the Soviet 16th Army/UBF (1985–86),
   shrinking Poland's amphibious task to Bornholm.

Because both post-date 1984, **the held 1960s–70s directives are the
*directly live* plan for a November 1983 setting** (which the current
pin is): SOYUZ-83 — the offensive climax, lineal heir of "Burza" and
"Lato-67" — ran May–June 1983, months before the setting. A **1986**
setting sits *after* both turns, so for 1986 the same facsimiles are
the *superseded offensive predecessor* of a plan that grew a defensive
preamble and lost Zealand. **The 1983-vs-1986 choice therefore decides
whether these primary sources are the sharp plan or a historical
under-layer** — a genuine consequence of the setting-time decision,
not a cosmetic one.

## Consequences for the instrument

1. **Red's Jutland thrust is one army, not a front — and always has
   been.** From 1961 ("with the forces of one army") through 1967
   ("one army on the Jutland direction") to the mid-80s, the Danish
   mainland is a *supporting* task of a front whose weight goes toward
   the Netherlands. The book's LANDJUT fight is red's economy-of-force
   flank, underwritten by fleet and nuclear fires — not the main
   event. This is consistent across every document held.
2. **Nuclear use is planned, dense, early, and Soviet-released.**
   Karup (BALTAP HQ) and the Zealand SAM belt are named nuclear DGZs
   in 1967; Odom's recovered GDR plan puts ~40 warheads on Hamburg.
   For red-side modelling, nuclear release is *assumed available* and
   controlled *above* the Polish front (Supreme Command of the Unified
   Armed Forces) — a front commander requests/receives, he does not
   fire strategic weapons. A blue-side scenario that treats WP nuclear
   use as a last resort mis-models red intent (Mastny/Luňák: the WP
   plan *starts* nuclear; the conventional-only track is the slower
   *option*, not the default).
3. **The warning clock is short by design.** 1961: NATO attack "within
   one to two days," offensive from the march. This is the red mirror
   of the blue 90-hour covering-force clock in `landjut-front.md` — a
   red plan built to cross the Odra before blue closes its gap.
4. **Timings, for a 1983 setting (SOYUZ-83 grammar).** Fast: the
   Coastal Front made 240 km to Enschede in 9 days in the 1983
   exercise; the Jutland army takes SH by ~D7–8 (with nuclear) and
   Zealand by ~D10–11. If the setting holds at Nov 1983 these are the
   *live* norms; if it moves to 1986 they become the
