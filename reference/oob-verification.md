### Who actually attacks Schleswig-Holstein (the mid-80s answer)

The controlling source is **Siegfried Lautsch**, head of the operations
department, Kommando MB V, **1983-86** — i.e., the man who wrote the
5th Army's plan in exactly our window — published as:

- Lautsch, *Kriegsschauplatz Deutschland. Erfahrungen und Erkenntnisse
  eines NVA-Offiziers* (ZMSBw, Potsdam 2013, ISBN 978-3-941571-28-0).
- Lautsch, "Die NVA-Operationsplanung für Norddeutschland 1983-1988",
  in Bange/Lemke (eds.), *Wege zur Wiedervereinigung. Die beiden
  deutschen Staaten in ihren Bündnissen 1970 bis 1990* (Oldenbourg
  2013), pp. 265-286.
  [Identified via https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1524/9783486855388.265/html
  and https://www.sehepunkte.de/2013/03/20113.html, fetched 2026-07-22]

Findings (each flagged with its transmission path):

- **The 5. Armee's own attack axis was SOUTH of the Elbe**, against
  I (NL) Corps on NORTHAG's northern flank: 1983 plan — day 1 destroy
  the Dutch covering forces and advance 50-60 km (direction
  Evendorf-Uelzen); day 2 Soviet airborne west of Aller/Weser; day 3
  further mechanized divisions over the Weser near Nienburg; final
  objective the **German-Dutch border near Ahaus** at 270-350 km
  depth in 5-7 days; **up to 32 nuclear strikes** in the army's
  initial nuclear salvo. [Lautsch interview, aixpaix.de,
  https://www.aixpaix.de/europa/lautsch-interview-20170408.html,
  fetched 2026-07-22 — interview with the planner himself, strong]
- **The Schleswig-Holstein/Jutland axis belonged to the KÜSTENFRONT
  (Coastal Front — the wartime Polish Front): three Polish armies
  plus one air army.** Its mission: **take Schleswig-Holstein and the
  Danish mainland, bypass Hamburg, advance to the German-Dutch
  border.** [Lautsch, *Kriegsschauplatz Deutschland*, as quoted in a
  Bundeswehrforum thread citing pp. 121, 124
  (https://bundeswehrforum.de/forum/index.php?topic=50905.35),
  fetched 2026-07-22 — **forum-transmitted; UPGRADED/PARTIALLY CORRECTED by Addendum 2 (ZZB p. 8/13): three armies +2 days toward Jutland CONFIRMED; the air army NOT confirmed**]
- **The covering phase**: on the assumption NATO attacks first, **two
  divisions — the 8. MSD and the Soviet 94. Gds MSD — hold the
  Schleswig-Holstein border sector for about two days (to X+2)**
  while the Coastal Front completes its approach march from Poland
  and deploys through Mecklenburg; the 8. MSD then reverts to 5th
  Army second echelon (Nienburg direction). [same forum-transmitted
  Lautsch cite — **CORRECTED IN SHAPE by Addendum 2: the covering grouping is the Soviet 94. GMSD (ZZB p. 8 fn 18), NOT 8. MSD, which fights in the attack corridor**]

### Revision of the standing assumption

The landjut-front.md standing assumption ("GDR + Polish first echelon
on the SH axis, Soviet follow-on") is **wrong in shape**. The mid-80s
architecture is:

1. **Covering force (X to ~X+2): one GDR division + one Soviet
   division** (8. MSD + 94. GdMSD) holding the border — thin, and
   deliberately so;
2. **Striking echelon (from ~X+2): a Polish FRONT** — three armies
   plus an air army — passing through Mecklenburg onto the
   SH/Jutland axis;
3. **Soviet weight on the axis**: the attached 94. GdMSD, the
   (post-1985) Zealand landing via 16th Army/UBF, and the Baltic
   Fleet — not a ground follow-on echelon behind the Poles;
4. The NVA 5th Army's main body fights a **different battle** south
   of the Elbe against I (NL) Corps.
