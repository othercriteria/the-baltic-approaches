# Air apportionment in BALTAP — who owns the deep/close split

*Researched 2026-07-21 (session 71ede904), raised to load-bearing by
the echelon hostile review (planning/echelon-hostile-review.md,
attack 2). Two-track pass: shelf read (Young, FM 100-5 82/86,
FM 71-100 78, NATO activation docs, TRADOC 525-5) + web (Danish
sources, NATO archives index, Central Region comparators) + FM
100-26 from /bulk. Evidence classes marked: ESTABLISHED /
INFERENCE (from the NATO-wide model) / UNKNOWN.*

## The answer

**COMLANDJUT did not decide the deep-vs-close air split. He was a
requester and a nominator; the apportionment decision was made at
Karup, at the joint BALTAP level.** The corps' G-3 controlled
requests, BAI target nominations, priority arguments, and the
sub-allocation of whatever CAS/BAI sorties came back down — never
the split itself.

## Established

1. **Structure (1962-93).** BALTAP was a *joint* PSC under AFNORTH:
   COMBALTAP (Danish LTG/VADM, German deputy, HQ Karup — from 1985
   the Finderup bunker) over four co-equal components: LANDJUT,
   LANDZEALAND, NAVBALTAP, AIRBALTAP. AIRBALTAP was the former
   Tactical Air Force Denmark, a component beside LANDJUT **from
   activation day** (NATO SGM-0694-62, on the shelf; Young pp. 9,
   63-64: LANDJUT is "a land component of Commander BALTAP").
2. **LANDJUT owned no fixed-wing air.** AIRBALTAP "commanded all
   flying units based within its sector and all reinforcements
   flying into its sector" — including the planned RAF (1x Harrier,
   2x Jaguar) and USAF (1x F-15, 3x F-16, 1x A-10) squadrons for
   Denmark. [Wikipedia BALTAP; Nørby, Balloons to Drones 2020]
3. **The doctrinal grammar (US/NATO-standard).** Apportionment —
   the percentage split across counter-air / AI / offensive air
   support — is the *joint force commander's* decision on his
   component commanders' recommendations, "usually confirmed
   daily"; the land side *recommends*, receives an allocation, and
   sub-allocates (FM 100-26, 1978, paras 3-3/3-4 — /bulk, Vietnam
   War Library dir). FM 100-5 1982 (pp. 7-10/7-11): BAI targets are
   "nominated by the ground commander"; corps commanders "should
   request BAI emphasis early in the planning process." FM 100-5
   1986 (p. 48): "BAI is executed by the air component commander."
   The corps' cell in this machinery is an ASOC (request flow up,
   tasking done at the air command's ATOC-equivalent) [JAPCC;
   FM 71-100 1978 pp. 3-14, 7-17 for the TACP/G-3 request roles].
4. **The Danish dual-hat.** COMAIRBALTAP = chief of Flyvertaktisk
   Kommando, one officer, Bunker 1137 at Karup; in war the whole
   RDAF passed to AIRBALTAP. The joint commander and the air
   commander shared a bunker complex. [forsvaret.dk; da.wiki FTK;
   koldkrig-online.dk]
5. **The air-defence anomaly.** Counter-air/air defence over
   Schleswig-Holstein and Hamburg belonged to **2 ATAF (RAF
   Germany, Mönchengladbach)** until 1990 — a different theater's
   air commander owned the sky over LANDJUT's own German sector.
   [Wikipedia BALTAP + dewiki]
6. **The deep-strike competitor.** "Operation Hurricane" — the
   layered anti-invasion-fleet strike (MFG F-104/Tornado with
   Kormoran + Danish/German missile craft) — was a BALTAP
   joint-level pre-plan. The MFG Tornados were simultaneously
   NAVBALTAP's fleet-killer and the nearest BAI-capable air on the
   Baltic axis: LANDJUT's deep fight and the navy's had a standing
   claim on the same airframes, adjudicated at Karup.
   [koldkrig-online.dk; hod.dk Bogason review]
7. **Central Region comparator, documented:** "COMAAFCE does the
   apportionment of air assets and allows the ATAFs to allocate
   their apportioned forces as they see fit" (Air War College,
   ADA217946, 1989). BALTAP replicated this one echelon down, with
   the simplification that its joint HQ was already air-co-located.

## Inference (strong, unnamed in open documents)

- The apportionment act itself was a **COMBALTAP decision advised
  by COMAIRBALTAP** (and contested by NAVBALTAP's maritime-strike
  claims). No open document says "COMBALTAP apportions" in those
  words; every structural fact above requires it.
- LANDJUT's request cell was ASOC-pattern; AIRBALTAP's offensive
  tasking lived inside the fused AIRBALTAP/FTK staff at Karup. The
  Cold War label "ATOC Karup" is NOT attested in reachable sources
  (the SOC air-defence side of Bunker 1137 is well documented; the
  offensive-tasking side's designation is not).
- A Kolsås layer above (Air Force Commander Northern Europe, USAF,
  assuming command of AFNORTH air in war) is attested-but-thin;
  geography made the practical decision Karup's regardless.

## Bogason pass (2026-07-22; scrape in holdings, tables unusable)

The book is navy-centric: **the who-decides-the-daily-split
question stays inference-only** (no apportionment/ATOC/ASOC/TACP
content at all). But it hardens the command skeleton:
- The 2 ATAF carve-out, precisely bounded [Ch 8]: 2 ATAF held
  "operational control of aircraft in Schleswig-Holstein... even
  after the BALTAP command was established; COMAIRBALTAP only had
  operational command of air forces north of the Danish-German
  border" — though tactical command within BALTAP. The functional
  split [Ch 9]: AIRBALTAP offensive operations to the Elbe; 2 ATAF
  air defence of all Schleswig-Holstein (with the HAWK/NIKE belts).
- **The deconfliction machinery is named**: a 1978 compromise
  created an "Air Space Control Authority" to coordinate the two
  commands [Ch 9] — the interface our note listed as unknown.
- Lineage: pre-1962 the same split ran COMTAFDEN (Karup, under
  AFNORTH) vs 2 ATAF, and exercise HOLD FAST 1960 documented the
  friction ("COMTAFDEN got little information about what happened
  in the air") [Ch 6] — the arrangement AIRBALTAP was built to
  fix, and textbook material for the book's air-argument scenes.
- Karup fighter command centre capacity: 290 tracks / 50
  simultaneous engagements, alternate at Vedbæk [Ch 6]; nuclear
  releasing authority delegated to Karup under Code GREEN [Ch 9].

## Consequences

**For the echelon decision (hostile review, attack 2): the attack's
factual premise is CONFIRMED.** The instrument's `deep_fraction`
lever is a COMBALTAP-altitude decision; the Rendsburg G-3 holds a
recommendation, nominations, and sub-allocation of the returned
sorties. Whether this kills the corps choice or feeds it is a DK
ruling, but note what the research adds to *both* sides:

- *Against corps:* the flagship lever is two headquarters away, and
  even the sky over the corps' own sector belongs to a third (2
  ATAF). The G-3's experiments in deep battle are memos to Karup.
- *For corps (unexpected texture):* the apportionment is confirmed
  **daily** (FM 100-26) — the daily iterated decision unsettle #2
  wanted exists, but it is a daily *argument*, made by liaison
  into a Danish-air-force bunker, against a navy with a claim on
  the same Tornados and an absent air-defence owner. That is
  alliance friction, metrics-vs-purpose, and the two staged
  questions (what is the goal / what does the enemy think it is)
  compounded by a third: *what does the air force think the goal
  is?* Nothing about that texture is reachable from a division or
  brigade TOC — a division book loses the Karup fight entirely.

**For the instrument:** `deep_fraction` must be re-read as "the
theater's apportionment outcome," not "the protagonist's choice."
The G-3-grade levers the model actually contains are the
recognition lag (v4) and reserve/CA decisions; the air lever is
COMBALTAP's. Campaign 2 should consider modeling the apportionment
as *partially responsive* to corps advocacy (a lag or a discount
between requested and received emphasis — the persuasion tax made
mechanical). Logged in notes/wargaming-findings.md.
