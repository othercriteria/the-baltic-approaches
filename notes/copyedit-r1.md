## Chapter 1 — ENDEX

- **"took the curls of acetate away" / "Nine days of chinagraph came away
  in curls" (razoring the map, lines 16–25)** vs Ch.2 line 292 "razored
  the exercise off the **plexiglass**" and the recurring
  chinagraph-written-directly-on-plexiglass throughout (Ch.2 l.294, Ch.5
  l.40, etc.) → The map-marking surface is described as **acetate** here
  (razor produces "curls of acetate") but as **plexiglass** everywhere
  after — and chinagraph (grease pencil) wiped off plexiglass would not
  razor into acetate curls. The same captain performing the same action
  is rendered with two different materials/physics. → Pick one surface
  model (acetate overlay razored off, *or* chinagraph on plexiglass
  wiped) and make Ch.1 agree with the rest. → **QUERY** (material
  continuity).

## Chapter 8 — The Crossing

- **"Two hundred and ninety vehicles per working hour. At half spacing,
  estimated four hundred ten." (lines 180–181)** → Number style breaks
  within a single speech: "two hundred **and** ninety" then "four hundred
  ten" (no *and*). The manuscript-wide norm is "X hundred and Y" (12+
  instances, incl. "four hundred and ten kilometers" in Ch.4 and Ch.20).
  → "four hundred **and** ten." → **FIX**.

## Chapter 12 — The Narrows

- **"Two and the armor. The third stands." (line 221) + "the corps that
  finishes today owning one fresh battalion group…" (lines 228–230)** vs
  **"The reserve: three battalion groups, none fresh." (line 340)** →
  Hvidt commits two of three reserve groups plus the armor and explicitly
  keeps the third *fresh*, stating the day's aim as finishing "owning one
  fresh battalion group." The end-of-day price posts the reserve as
  "three battalion groups, **none fresh**," which contradicts a retained
  fresh group. May be intentional (his stated intent overtaken by the
  day), but as written it reads as a numeric inconsistency. → Confirm the
  third group's fate, or signal that the intent failed. → **QUERY**
  (OOB/continuity).

- **"convoys the marshalling yards were not promising" (line 340)** →
  "marshalling" (British double-l) here and in Ch.10 l.199 ("marshalling
  yard") vs the verb "marshaling / marshaled / marshals" (American
  single-l) in Ch.7, 9, 11, 15. Spelling drift. Arguably defensible if
  "marshalling yard" is treated as a fixed rail term, but it clashes with
  the manuscript's otherwise consistent American orthography. → Normalize
  (single-l to match the verb, unless the rail-term exception is
  deliberate). → **QUERY**. *(See also Ch.10.)*

## Chapter 15 — Sequencing

- **"Six hundred forty on the twenty-fifth." (line 309)** → Same number-
  style issue as Ch.8: norm is "hundred **and** forty." (The adjacent
  "eleven hundred" / "nine hundred" correctly take no *and*; only this one
  needs it.) In a staccato list, so slightly softer than the Ch.8 case. →
  "Six hundred **and** forty." → **QUERY** (leaning FIX).

- **"cancelled" (line 288)** → British double-l against the dominant
  American spelling (compare color/colored, organize, gray, maneuver,
  plexiglass throughout). → "canceled." → **QUERY** (orthography
  consistency). *(Groups with Ch.14/Ch.20 below.)*

## Chapter 14 — The Cathedral, Rebuilt

- **"cataloguing" (line 216)** → British -ue spelling vs dominant
  American orthography; pairs with "catalogue" in Ch.20 l.150. →
  "cataloging" (and "catalog" in Ch.20). → **QUERY** (orthography
  consistency).

## Chapter 17 — The Refusal

- **"it is the thing I am proudest of, of everything" (line 199)** →
  Possible unintended doubled "of." Reads as an appositive intensifier
  ("of everything, the thing I am proudest of") and may be deliberate
  Rahn cadence, but it trips. → Consider "the thing I am proudest of, of
  everything I have done" or reorder. → **QUERY**.

- **"call-sign lists" (line 163)** → Only hyphenated instance of
  "call sign"; the term is open elsewhere (Ch.4 l.141, Ch.9 l.224, Ch.10
  l.40, Ch.12 l.239). Attributive hyphenation here is grammatically
  defensible, so lowest priority. → Optionally open to "call sign lists"
  for uniformity, or leave. → **QUERY** (minor).

## Chapter 20 — December

- **"catalogue" (line 150)** → see Ch.14 orthography note. → **QUERY**.

- **Minor, register-level:** date-time groups appear as "060955Z NOV 83"
  (Ch.2) and "090600Z NOVEMBER" (Ch.4) with Zulu suffix, but the
  cease-fire time is rendered "062400 December" (line 19) with no Z and a
  spelled month, and internal log DTGs use bare ddhhmm (072240, 130700,
  292000). The variance is plausibly contextual (formal signal vs.
  narrative vs. internal log), so probably intentional — flagged only for
  a conscious ruling. → **QUERY** (very minor).

second-draft manuscript of ~49k words. The single unambiguous mechanical

American spelling drift on a handful of words, and a couple of low-stakes
style-consistency notes. Diacritics, ranks, proper nouns, compound
hyphenation, foreign-language snippets, number registers, caps-block
internal consistency, and the date spine are all in order. No comma-splice
or fragment "corrections" are proposed — the deliberate voice was
respected throughout.
