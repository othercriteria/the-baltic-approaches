## ASSISTANT

Confirmed current: $125 for one, $295 for ten. Here's the trade-off picture:

**Free KDP ISBN**
- $0, assigned instantly at title setup; fully eligible for KDP's Expanded Distribution.
- The catch is **lock-in and identity**: that ISBN is valid only on KDP — you can't take it to IngramSpark or any other printer, ever. If you later want wider print channels, the paperback gets a *new* ISBN there, so the "same book" exists under two identifiers, which is mildly ugly for a project whose whole ethos is a clean unit of record.
- Publisher of record in the metadata reads **"Independently published"** — you don't get an imprint name. The notices page can still print whatever imprint you like, but the catalog metadata won't match it, and the `imprint TK` on our notices page suggests you may care about that line.

**Own ISBN (Bowker/myidentifiers — the only US agency)**
- $125 single / $295 for ten ($29.50 each). The single is deliberately priced to push you to the 10-pack.
- **You are the publisher of record**, under an imprint name you register — the metadata, the notices page, and Books in Print all agree.
- **Portable**: the same ISBN works across KDP and IngramSpark simultaneously (that combination *requires* your own ISBN), and survives any future printer change. One identity for the edition, wherever it's printed.
- Small admin overhead: register the imprint, fill the title metadata at Bowker yourself.
- One ISBN covers only one format — a hardcover or a distinct paperback edition later would each consume another from the pack. (A Kindle ebook needs none; Amazon uses ASINs.)

**The decision hinges on two questions**, both yours: Is this book plausibly not the last one? This one is more "legal chassis vs. brand" than publishing arcana. The two are separable, and most of the leverage is in keeping them separate.

**What an imprint actually is.** Just a trade name a publisher issues books under. There's no imprint registry — it exists in three places: the Bowker record attached to your ISBNs, the publisher/imprint field at KDP (or IngramSpark), and the printed page. The only hard consistency rule: when you bring your own ISBN, KDP expects the publisher/imprint you enter to match what the ISBN is registered to at Bowker — mismatches cause title-setup friction. So decide the name *before* filling in the Bowker metadata.

**The LLC's role.** If you want book business through the LLC, the clean structure is: the **LLC buys the ISBN block and is the publisher of record**, the KDP account runs on the LLC's EIN and bank account (royalties, 1099s, deductible expenses — ISBNs, proofs, any ads — all land in the business), and the **imprint is whatever trade name the LLC publishes under**. Copyright is unaffected: the notices page keeps "© 2026 Daniel Klein" personally, which is normal and desirable — the LLC publishes, the author owns.

**Should the imprint be the LLC's name?** Only if the name already reads like a press and you want it on spines indefinitely. The trade-offs:

- *LLC name as imprint*: zero extra paperwork — the entity's legal name needs no DBA. But "…, LLC" is clunky on a title page (you can usually display it without the suffix), and if the LLC is named for consulting or some other line of business, the name will look odd in book catalogs and set the tone for every future book in the campaign.
- *Distinct imprint name*: the usual choice — pick a press-like name, register it at Bowker under the LLC as publisher. The one legal wrinkle: an imprint used commercially that isn't the LLC's legal name is a fictitious/assumed name in most states, so the LLC files a cheap DBA registration. That's typically the entire cost of doing it properly.

**Choosing the name, constraints worth respecting:** don't collide with an existing publisher (search Books in Print, USPTO trademarks, and Amazon before committing — Amazon will flag names that impersonate known houses); avoid anything implying affiliation; prefer genre-neutral if the "larger writing campaign" ranges wider than Cold War fiction; and check the domain if the imprint might ever want a web presence. The name is nearly permanent per book — the ISBN metadata carries it forever.

**One project-specific note:** Bowker's registration is public — anyone can trace the imprint to the LLC, and the LLC to you. For most self-publishers that's a shrug; for this book it's actually on-theme. The provenance apparatus insists the ledger, not the byline, is the honest unit of account — a named imprint transparently registered to your entity is the same posture applied to the legal layer.

**Bottom line:** use the LLC as the chassis regardless (ISBNs, KDP tax identity, money). Use its literal name as the imprint only if it was named like a press; otherwise spend the DBA filing fee on a name you'd be happy to see on book ten. Usual caveat: DBA mechanics and tax details vary by state — worth ten minutes with whoever does the LLC's filings before the Bowker purchase.

If you settle the imprint name, the remaining TKs collapse: notices line, Bowker metadata, KDP publisher field, all same string — and the wrap needs nothing (barcode zone already handles the ISBN's physical side).
