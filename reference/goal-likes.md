# The Goal-likes — a survey of the didactic-fiction genre

*Researched 2026-07-23 (session fa9b03ec) by an Opus web-research
subagent; report filed verbatim. Two lineages: the business/ops
didactic novel (Goldratt's) and military didactic fiction
(Swinton's). Headline findings: (1) the operational-level Goldratt
slot is EMPTY — nobody has written The Goal for operational art;
(2) the genre's seven iteration engines are cataloged in §2, and the
operational-phase-cadence engine is the strongest honest answer to
standing unsettle #2 (where iteration comes from in a three-week
war). Companion docs: reference/goal-structure-map.md,
reference/phoenix-transposition.md. URLs preserved; purchase
candidates for the shelf flagged in-line (First Clash, Hill 781,
Red Army already genre-neighbor material).*

---


Two lineages converge on this project: the **business/ops didactic novel** (Goldratt's genre) and **military didactic fiction** (Swinton's genre). The military operational-level slot is the high-value target, and — spoiler for the gap analysis — it is essentially **empty**.

---

## 1. Annotated Catalog

### A. The business / operations didactic novel

**The Goal** — Goldratt & Jeff Cox, 1984. *Teaches:* Theory of Constraints; throughput/inventory/operating-expense as the true metrics vs. local-efficiency cost accounting; identify-exploit-subordinate-elevate. *Devices:* the archetype set — a Socratic mentor (Jonah, a physicist who **only ever asks questions and refuses to give answers**), a hard **deadline clock** (90 days to save the plant), a **misleading-metrics inversion** as the central epiphany, guided discovery by an in-over-his-head protagonist (plant manager Alex Rogo), a domestic subplot mirroring the work problem, and an **embedded parable** (the Boy-Scout hike with "Herbie" the slow kid = the bottleneck) that teaches the abstract lesson in miniature. *Reception:* 7-10M copies; the founding text of "business fiction." *Steal:* the mentor-who-withholds; the metric inversion as the spine; the miniature-parable device (a small vignette that models the whole thesis). *Must avoid:* Goldratt's flat prose and cardboard domestic scenes. [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/113934.The_Goal) · [narrative analysis (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373864886_A_Narrative_Analysis_of_The_Goal_as_Corporate_Fiction_with_a_focus_on_the_nature_of_conflict)

**It's Not Luck** — Goldratt, 1994. *Teaches:* the TOC "Thinking Processes" (evaporating-cloud conflict resolution, current/future-reality trees), applied to marketing and holding-company strategy. *Devices:* same Jonah-less structure with Alex now the mentor; problems solved by explicit logic-tree diagrams on the page. *Assessment:* the diagrams-in-text are didactically honest but narratively inert; a cautionary example of doctrine overwhelming story. *Steal:* nothing structural. *Avoid:* letting the framework's diagram apparatus surface as diagrams. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_Not_Luck)

**Critical Chain** — Goldratt, 1997. *Teaches:* TOC for project management — buffer management, the "student syndrome," aggregating safety time. *Devices:* dual frame — an MBA classroom (professor + students as the Socratic engine) interleaved with a company case. *Steal:* the **classroom frame as a sanctioned iteration engine** — students bring successive cases, the instructor Socratically dismantles each. Maps cleanly onto a mentor running a staff officer through map problems. [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Critical-Chain-A-Business-Novel/Goldratt/p/book/9780566080388)

**Necessary But Not Sufficient** — Goldratt, Schragenheim & Ptak, 2000. *Teaches:* why ERP software delivers value only when paired with TOC process change. *Assessment:* narrowest, most dated; skip. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_But_Not_Sufficient_(novel))

**Isn't It Obvious?** — Goldratt, Eshkoli & Brown, 2009. *Teaches:* TOC replenishment applied to retail/distribution inventory. *Assessment:* minor. [tocinstitute.org](https://www.tocinstitute.org/eliyahu-goldratt.html)

**The Race** — Goldratt & Robert Fox, 1986. **NOT a novel** — an illustrated primer teaching Drum-Buffer-Rope scheduling. Flagged because it shows Goldratt's *other* register: when he wanted pure doctrine transfer he abandoned fiction entirely. Instructive boundary case for "how much story does the payload actually need." [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/1032536.The_Race)

**Velocity** — Dee Jacob, Suzan Bergland & Jeff Cox (AGI-Goldratt Institute), 2009. *Teaches:* integration of TOC + Lean + Six Sigma. *Of note:* the "velocity" method was reportedly adopted by the **US Navy and Marine Corps** for supply-chain work — a real thread from this genre into military logistics. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Velocity-Combining-Constraints-Breakthrough-Performance/dp/1439158924)

**The Phoenix Project** — Kim, Behr & Spafford, 2013. *Teaches:* DevOps / IT flow — the "Three Ways", WIP limits, plant-floor TOC applied to IT. *Devices:* explicit decade-in-the-making homage to The Goal — mentor Erik as Jonah; failing deployment under a hard release deadline; misleading metrics; the **incident/deployment cycle** as the recurring beat. *Steal:* how Kim modernized the Jonah figure (cryptic, irritating, earns it); the crisis-cadence (each outage a fresh iteration of the same lesson at higher resolution). *Avoid:* Erik's mysticism tips toward smugness. [itrevolution.com](https://itrevolution.com/product/the-phoenix-project/) · [Techtarget on the homage](https://www.techtarget.com/searchitoperations/feature/Gene-Kim-explores-DevOps-transformation-in-The-Unicorn-Project)

**The Unicorn Project** — Gene Kim, 2019. *Teaches:* same transformation from the **developer's** POV — the "Five Ideals." *Devices:* **parallel timeline** to Phoenix, same company, different vantage. *Steal:* the multi-vantage-on-one-event idea — G3/G2/G4 vantages on the same battle; the same doctrinal lesson looks different from each staff cell. [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/44333183-the-unicorn-project)

**The Deadline** — Tom DeMarco, 1997. *Teaches:* software project management — team size, pressure, estimation, the futility of overtime. *Devices:* two worth stealing. (1) A **controlled-experiment frame**: Tompkins runs the same project as many parallel teams of different sizes/methods — **spatial rather than temporal iteration**. (2) **"Mr. Tompkins' Journal"** — each chapter ends with the protagonist's bulleted lesson-capture, an in-diegesis annotation apparatus. *Steal:* the journal-at-chapter-end in the character's own voice — a staff officer's notebook is period-perfect. *Avoid:* the whimsical abduction premise. [tomdemarco.com](https://www.tomdemarco.com/Books/deadline.html)

**The Five Dysfunctions of a Team** (and Lencioni's "leadership fable" line) — Lencioni, 2002. *Teaches:* five-layer team-pathology pyramid. *Devices:* tight single-narrative fable with the **model bolted on as an explicit appendix afterward**. *Steal:* the discipline of a small, memorable, ordered framework. *Avoid:* the model-appendix confession that the story was scaffolding — the opposite of "payload earned in-scene." [Wiley](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Five+Dysfunctions+of+a+Team:+A+Leadership+Fable,+20th+Anniversary+Edition-p-9780470893869)

**The Gold Mine / The Lean Manager / Lead With Respect** (Gold Mine trilogy) — Freddy & Michael Ballé, 2005-2014. *Teaches:* lean/TPS as a *way of seeing* (go-and-see, respect-for-people, kaizen). *Devices:* a genuine **sensei mentor** (Bob Woods, retired Toyota-trained) walking a skeptic through the *gemba*; practitioner + cognitive-sociologist authorship makes the *learning process itself*, mistakes included, convincing. *Steal:* the sensei who teaches by making the student *go look at the actual thing* — directly transposable to a mentor forcing a staff officer to the map, the road, the traffic-control point. Best-rendered mentor relationship in the business branch. [Lean Enterprise Institute](https://www.lean.org/store/book/the-gold-mine/)

**Rolling Rocks Downhill** — Clarke Ching, 2014. *Teaches:* Agile reframed through TOC. *Assessment:* fast, funny, minor; evidence the form is a recognized template writers deliberately re-run. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Rolling-Rocks-Downhill-Business-mentions-ebook/dp/B00PJ8HBW8)

*(Adjacent: Jeff Cox franchised the form across* Zapp!, Selling the Wheel, The Venture, The Cure*. Confirms a repeatable factory of doctrine-novels around a practitioner-plus-novelist pairing.)*

### B. The military didactic-fiction lineage

**The Defence of Duffer's Drift** — Ernest Swinton ("Lt. Backsight Forethought"), 1904. *Teaches:* small-unit tactics — 50-man detachment defending a river drift against Boers; 22 concrete lessons. *Devices:* the **canonical iteration engine** — six sequential **dreams**; scenario resets identically; BF forgets the terrain but *retains the lessons*, applying them cumulatively until he wins. **No mentor** — the dream-respawn *is* the teacher. *Reception:* required reading for a century. *Steal:* iteration-with-retained-lessons is the genre's purest teaching machine. *Must confront:* it works because the scenario is a *repeatable set-piece* — our three-week war does not naturally repeat (project open question #2). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Defence_of_Duffer%27s_Drift) · [Army PDF](https://www.benning.army.mil/infantry/199th/ocs/content/pdf/The%20Defence%20of%20Duffers%20Drift.pdf)

**The Defense of Hill 781** — James R. McDonough, 1988. *Teaches:* mechanized combined-arms combat at task-force/battalion level, drawn from the NTC. *Devices:* Duffer's engine relocated to **purgatory** — Lt. Col. A. Tack Always must win six NTC missions to earn his way out; a spectral mentor debriefs between attempts (**respawn-with-explanation**). *Assessment:* proof the device scales from platoon to task force — but stops *below* the operational level, and the purgatory conceit is what our "play it straight" criterion forbids. *Steal:* the between-iterations debrief. [Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/111266/the-defense-of-hill-781-by-james-r-mcdonough/9780891414759/)

**The Defense of Jisr al-Doreaa** — Burgoyne & Marckwardt (foreword Nagl), 2009. *Teaches:* COIN at platoon level in Iraq. *Devices:* explicit Duffer's homage (dream iteration), **published bound together with Swinton's original**. *Steal:* the model of openly acknowledging your ancestor text. [U. Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/D/bo6695659.html)

**First Clash: Combat Close-up in World War Three** — Kenneth Macksey, 1985. *Teaches:* battle procedure and the combined-arms fight of a Canadian mechanized battlegroup (3 RCR BG, 4 CMBG) in the first 72 hours of a WWIII Central-Front defensive. *Devices:* **commissioned by the Canadian DND as a training aid**; straight tactical narrative with **bullet-point lesson summaries at each chapter's end** — annotation apparatus instead of mentor or iteration; wargame-grade OOB detail. *The single closest structural cousin to our project's ambition* — officially-sanctioned fiction-as-doctrine on the exact Cold-War terrain. *Where it misses:* its doctrine is battle procedure and drills, not a unifying operational-art *thesis* — no Jonah, no discovery, no metric inversion; the reader is told the lessons, not led to them. [Cold War Gamer review](https://coldwargamer.blogspot.com/2012/03/review-books-first-clash-k-mackesy.html) · [Fuldapocalypse review](https://fuldapocalypsefiction.com/2020/01/28/review-first-clash/)

**Red Army** — Ralph Peters, 1989. *Teaches:* effectively a primer on Soviet operational doctrine — echelonment, OMG, tempo-and-reserves logic — **entirely from the Soviet side, and the Soviets win.** *Devices:* multi-POV mosaic from conscript to Front commander; **no mentor, no iteration, no explicit lessons** — didactic in effect, not in structure. *Assessment:* the best existing evocation of operational-level war as *felt experience*; a direct tonal/period reference for our enemy side. *Steal:* how Peters makes operational tempo legible through the men executing it; the enemy-as-competent-professionals stance. *Why not "The Goal for operational art":* no protagonist who discovers a framework; no teaching engine. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_(novel)) · [Russia in Fiction](https://russiainfiction.com/red-army)

**The Third World War: August 1985** — Hackett et al., 1978 (rev. 1982). *Teaches:* NATO-WP general war at strategic/theater level; partly defense-spending advocacy. *Devices:* **retrospective "future history"** — documentary voice, committee-authored, maps and OOBs. *Why it misses:* no protagonist discovery, no learning-in-real-time — the war is over when you open it. (Coyle's **Team Yankee**, 1987, took Hackett's scenario *down* to a company team — engaging, but tactical and non-didactic; the lineage runs *away* from operational teaching.) [War Room, Army War College](https://warroom.armywarcollege.edu/articles/hackett-third-world-war/)

**Ghost Fleet** — Singer & Cole, 2015. *Teaches:* near-future great-power war tech/strategy. *Devices:* founding **FICINT** text — **hundreds of footnotes**; the annotation apparatus is the didactic engine. *Steal:* footnote-as-teaching-channel keeps doctrine out of characters' mouths — but footnotes fight immersion; a period-1983 novel can't footnote itself without breaking spell. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_Fleet_(novel))

**2034** — Ackerman & Stavridis, 2021. More literary, fewer footnotes, cautionary rather than instructional; FICINT drifting back toward technothriller. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2034:_A_Novel_of_the_Next_World_War)

**White Sun War: The Campaign for Taiwan** — Maj. Gen. Mick Ryan, 2023. *Teaches:* near-future operational/campaign-level war — autonomy, human-machine teaming. *Devices:* multi-POV FICINT campaign narrative. *Assessment:* **the strongest operational-level didactic-fiction candidate in print** — but it teaches emerging tech/concepts through a distributed campaign, **not one protagonist's Socratic discovery of a doctrinal framework**; no mentor, no iteration engine. Proves the slot can be occupied — not the Goldratt method. [Modern War Institute](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/how-the-war-for-taiwan-will-be-fought-and-won/)

**The 2020 Commission Report** — Jeffrey Lewis, 2018. *Devices:* the entire novel is a retrospective official document with citations. *Steal:* the institutional after-action-report voice — a corps HQ would natively produce it. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_2020_Commission_Report_on_the_North_Korean_Nuclear_Attacks_Against_the_United_States)

**Didactic memoir:** Rommel, *Infantry Attacks* (1937); von Schell, *Battle Leadership* (1933) — vignette-then-extracted-lesson in autobiographical form; the non-fiction root of the "experience → explicit lesson" beat. The voice — a professional soldier drawing the lesson from his own action — is period-authentic and avoids a mystical mentor.

**Scholarship/the form itself:** "business novel" as a named genre with criticism ([narrative analysis of The Goal](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373864886_A_Narrative_Analysis_of_The_Goal_as_Corporate_Fiction_with_a_focus_on_the_nature_of_conflict)); "useful fiction"/FICINT as an organized movement — Singer & Cole's [TRADOC Mad Scientist essays](https://madsciblog.tradoc.army.mil/332-realer-than-real-useful-fiction-with-p-w-singer-and-august-cole/), [FICINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FicInt), and RAND's ["Dominating Duffer's Domain"](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RR1166z1.html) — which re-runs Swinton's dream device to teach Information Operations: the Duffer's engine still deliberately reused as doctrine-delivery in PME.

## 2. Synthesis — the design space of iteration engines

Every work in this genre needs a mechanism that lets a protagonist **fail, learn, and re-approach** without the story either ending or cheating. Seven engines:

| Engine | Exemplar | How honest iteration is manufactured |
|---|---|---|
| **Real-time operational cadence** | Goldratt (plant shifts/crises); Kim (incident & deployment cycle) | The workplace *naturally recurs* — every shift/outage is a fresh instance of the same problem at higher resolution. No contrivance needed. |
| **Literal respawn (silent)** | Swinton — six dreams | Scenario resets identically; protagonist keeps only the lessons. Purest teaching machine; requires a repeatable set-piece. |
| **Respawn-with-debrief** | McDonough — NTC purgatory | Same, plus a mentor explains between attempts. More teaching bandwidth; requires a fantastical frame. |
| **Parallel variants (spatial iteration)** | DeMarco — many teams at once | Compare simultaneous variants instead of re-running one — iteration in *space*, not time. |
| **Sanctioned rehearsal (map-exercise/wargame)** | Critical Chain's classroom; TEWTs/CPXs implicitly | The mentor runs the protagonist through *training* problems that don't cost reality; native to any HQ. |
| **Annotation apparatus (no in-story iteration)** | Macksey (bullets), Ghost Fleet (footnotes), Lewis (report format) | The teaching happens *outside* the diegesis; the story runs once, the apparatus carries the doctrine. |
| **Retrospective after-action** | Hackett; von Schell/Rommel memoirs | The whole thing is already over; the reader iterates by comparing decisions to known outcomes. |

**For a three-week war (open question #2),** the temporal-respawn engines are the most effective teachers but irreconcilable with "play it straight." The realistic options:

1. **Operational-phase cadence (the Goldratt move, mapped up an echelon):** successive WP pushes — first-echelon assault, commitment of follow-on forces, the counterattack window — become the "shifts." Each is *the same operational problem* (where is the throughput constraint on tempo — mine, or the enemy's?) re-encountered at higher resolution every 48-72 hours, exactly how Goldratt's plant recurs every shift. **Most defensible engine; closest to the source text.**
2. **Map-exercise / CPX rehearsal (the classroom move):** the mentor pre-plays problems on the map before reality delivers them — period-authentic (TEWTs, map exercises, wargaming the enemy course of action); launders iteration without breaking realism.
3. **Notebook/journal capture (the DeMarco/Macksey move):** the staff officer's own notebook carries the extracted lesson in-voice — didactically honest and period-authentic.

Avoid: literal respawn; footnotes/annotations (immersion-breaking in a 1983 novel unless framed as an in-world after-action report); the Lencioni "appendix the model" confession.

## 3. Gap analysis — has anyone written *The Goal* for operational-level war?

**No.** The intersection is genuinely vacant, and the vacancy is structural, not accidental. Sort the field on two axes — **echelon taught** and **teaching method**:

- **Right method, wrong echelon.** Swinton, McDonough, Burgoyne/Marckwardt use the true didactic engine (iteration, cumulative lessons, fail-and-learn protagonist) — but top out at small-unit-to-battalion tactics. McDonough (task-force, NTC) is the highest the discovery-engine has ever reached: still two echelons below a corps and squarely tactical.
- **Right echelon, wrong method.** Red Army, Hackett, White Sun War render the operational/theater level convincingly — but none teaches by guided discovery. No mentor, no metric inversion, no iteration.
- **Closest single cousin overall:** Macksey's *First Clash* (1985) — commissioned fiction-as-doctrine on the exact terrain, explicit lesson-capture — but battle-procedure doctrine by annotation, no discovery arc, no operational thesis. It proves the *institutional appetite*; it does not do the thing.

**The specific unclaimed slot:** a novel in which a single staff officer, ignorant at the start, is led by a Socratic mentor to *discover operational art itself — tempo as throughput, the culminating point as the constraint, the follow-on echelon as the enemy's "Herbie"* — the way Rogo discovers TOC. The conceptual bridge is sound: operational-art literature already frames tempo and logistics bottlenecks in throughput terms ([MWI on congested logistics](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/wargames-keep-warning-us-about-congested-logistics-its-time-to-take-action/); [Proceedings, "Maneuver Warfare Is Just Operational Art"](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/2023/november/maneuver-warfare-just-operational-art)). The analogy has been stated in essays; **no one has dramatized it as guided discovery.** That is the white space.

**The one caution the gap implies:** the tactical didactic-fictions all lean on a *repeatable set-piece* (a drift, a hill, an NTC rotation) to justify iteration. Operational art doesn't offer one — which is *why* the slot is empty and *why* open question #2 is the load-bearing design problem. The operational-phase-cadence engine is the project's best answer, and the most faithful transposition of what Goldratt actually did.
