## 1. The 30k attractor (the headline, now cross-model)

The 55k plan, drafted at natural stride with counter-pressure
applied from ch. 4, produced 29,936 words — within 4% of White
Buffalo's 31k. **DK notes the same attractor produced WB's initial
Opus 4.5 draft** — so this is a cross-model LLM-drafting attractor,
not a Fable idiosyncrasy: the model drives each unit to its payload
and stops; "done" = argument landed, not material exhausted. The
missing mass is specifically DIALOGUE-SCENE: The Goal runs ~80%
talk at the speed of speech; the specimen is chronicle-narration
with dialogue islands — a novella register (WB's register).

**DK ruling-in-effect: size up from 30k, for good reasons** — the
30k is "a very cozy length" but the increment is the project. The
expansion engines, DK-ratified:

1. **Dialogue conversion** — matches the pace of reasoning to the
   reader (the reader-ahead mechanism lives at talking speed) AND
   provides the contrastive/adversarial instrument for
   voice differentiation (fixing failure §4.3).
2. **Failure injection** — grade the competence ramp (fixing §4.1);
   the specimen's Part III lacks its ch.-39 moment (success
   creating the new crisis); the staff must stay behind the reader
   longer and pay for its lessons later into the book.
3. **65k-tier items back in scope, selectively** — the interleaf
   material (division/Karup), more red-room beats, second
   iteration cycles per station.
4. **B-plot thickening** — the friction variant proved cheap
   (~4% of words, Paige-thin); grow it rather than replace it.

Implication for the allocation sheet: rebuild from the specimen's
ACTUALS (natural chapter 1,200–1,800 words; ~1 iteration cycle per
station per 30k), not from estimates. A 52–56k target = the
specimen's 20-chapter skeleton + ~8–10 added/expanded scene-units,
each with a named engine from the list above.

## 2. DK's orthogonality observation (payload identity resolved)

DK, on reading: *the book built an engine that lands orthogonal to
textbook/field-manual AirLand Battle — but would likely have
recovered the textbook if red had presented according to the
textbook.* This is the deep result of the whole campaign-and-draft
sequence, and it resolves premise.md's original claim ("the
didactic payload is AirLand Battle doctrine") into its mature
form: **the payload is the METHOD (constraint-reading,
two-calendar accounting, pricing your own ignorance), and the
doctrine is a contingent output of the method** — deep battle
falls out of it exactly when the enemy's architecture matches the
doctrine's assumptions (the Central Front geometry), and something
else falls out when he doesn't (the SH axis; wargame v17). The
draft teaches the thing that GENERATES doctrine, which is why the
wargame's META-claim ("no ranking survives the next calibration
layer; the argument is the deliverable") could become a closing
beat instead of a nihilism. This framing should govern Phase-1
outlining: stations teach method-moves; doctrine appears as the
method's output under stated assumptions, never as content to be
transmitted.

1. **Competence ramp too clean** — confusion clause dead by
   ch. 14; Part III's staff never wrong again; no
   success-creates-crisis beat. The single biggest design risk

   model errs late AND the protagonists pay for trusting it; keep
   the staff behind the reader deeper into Part III.

   Zawadzki are four flavors of the drafting voice; ensemble
   differentiation held by role, not voice. Fix via engine 1;
   test in the matrix re-renders.
3. **B-plot at ~4%** — cheap but Paige-thin; Marei two scenes;
   Aakjær/Korsør one beat. Engine 4.
4. **Windows thin** — Loheide got one strong scene; Karup stayed
   a voice on a line. The 65k interleaf question NOT answered by
   the specimen; engine 3 decides it in outline.
5. **All numbers are vibes** — expenditure, counts, rates

   verify); the counter-battery-countdown device needs a CAL
   sanity check; war-origin gestural (by scope); cease-fire
   arrives thin (one more beat of the war ending AROUND the
