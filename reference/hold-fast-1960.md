# HOLD FAST 1960 — the Spiegel witness

*Source: "Zehn Köpfe," DER SPIEGEL 41/1960 (4 Oct 1960), spiegel.de
archive a-eca701d7-0002-0001-0000-000043066968. Surfaced and
supplied by DK 2026-07-27 (full text read in-session; distilled
here — the article is Spiegel's copyright, free-access archive).
Contemporary reporting on the NATO maneuver HOLD FAST, 20–24 Sept
1960, Schleswig-Holstein — the exercise whose after-action report
is our Territorialheer-OPCON warrant (bogason-answers.md Q6).
Session e3137278@2.*

## The exercise

45,000 German/Danish/British/Canadian/Belgian troops, 284 heavy
tanks, ~12,000 vehicles; ordered and directed by AFNORTH (Gen Sir
Horatius Murray) with Generalmajor Hax heading the integrated
direction staff. Scenario: ORANGE (not "Red" — NATO convention,
the article mocks the integrated-slang pronunciation "Orränsch")
attacks westward across an assumed Iron Curtain drawn on the
Hamburg–Lübeck autobahn, objective "into Jutland." The
after-action report (source of Bogason's Q6 passage) was expected
to take three months and stay classified.

## What it confirms or enriches, by canon seam

1. **The corps' direct ancestor, at period-press grade.** The 3rd
   Danish Division marched south from Jutland to the Kiel Canal —
   the article says "as in 1864" — and united with the German 6th
   Panzergrenadier Division as the **Allied Corps
   Schleswig-Holstein**, commanded (in English, with 87 Danish, 51
   German, 5 British staff assistants) by German **Konteradmiral
   Bernhard Rogge** — a sailor commanding the land corps, two
   years before LANDJUT existed. Binational corps + Danish
   division + 6. PzGren + English working language = the book's
   structure, attested 1960. (The 1864 invocation also shows the
   Danevirke-year resonance was period-available, not our
   imposition.)
2. **Ch. 1's scripted-enemy critique has a 1960 journalistic
   witness.** Spiegel's central complaint IS Brauer's wash-up: the
   attacker was "unrealistically weak" — two British-Canadian
   divisions against the corps, the second echelon and reserves
   present only on the map and never played, so "the attacker
   stayed as weak as he will never be in war." The umpires braked
   ORANGE "with plausible tactical arguments." Our opening
   chapter's whole epistemology — the exercise grades you against
   an enemy of your own scripting — was already the press verdict
   on the real 1960 exercise.
3. **The alert ladder, in use, with German period names.** Blue
   ordered *einfacher Alarm* (18 Sept afternoon) then *erhöhter
   Alarm* (20 Sept midday) as ORANGE massed — the graduated
   alert states operating exactly as nato-alert-system.md
   reconstructs, vocabulary attested in-period press.
4. **Ch. 6's release friction, in the corps commander's own 1960
   words.** Rogge, asked why he hadn't nuked ORANGE's second
   wave: restraint until the enemy's Schwerpunkte are certain —
   and the warheads must be requested from and collected from the
   Americans, which "costs a lot of time"; handover "officer to
   officer, without paperwork." The custody/release friction our
   locked-door chapter runs on, stated by the actual commander.
   ORANGE, realistically, carried its own dummy warheads in its
   own train — the asymmetry ch. 6's tree walks (his weapons
   closer to his fingers) visible in 1960 exercise design.
5. **Kjeldsen's "every exercise scenario of ten years had it by
   D+3" is conservative.** HOLD FAST played 16 nuclear strikes in
   four days (max ten per side, each strike applied for
   individually with target/purpose/yield and approved by the
   direction staff — release-request procedure INSIDE the
   exercise). ORANGE nuked Kiel, Neumünster, and the **Rendsburg
   road bridge (8 kT)**: road bridge shattered by blast, **the
   rail bridge beside it intact**, brick buildings down within
   1.5 km, approaches blocked by rubble, bridgeheads "contaminated"
   3.5 hours — while Blue crossed on the bypass and **by ferry**,
   and engineers replaced the bridge after the decay window. A
   nuclear strike on the book's HQ town, adjudicated by table, in
   1960. (Also: a strike on a located Blue division CP missed by
   2 km and spent itself in forest — CP-survivability texture.)
6. **Nuclear-dispersed attack.** ORANGE attacked WITHOUT
   Schwerpunkte "because of the nuclear situation" — armor
   probing company-wise, never massing, denying Blue a nuclear
   target. The German armor school's "Klotzen, nicht kleckern"
   instinct waited in vain for the massed breakthrough. Period
   texture for how the nuclear shadow deformed conventional
   tactics — background to the book's norm-vs-nuclear framing.
7. **Home-guard gap, sardonically noted.** With the mobile Danish
   forces sent south, Jutland's defense against airborne/naval
   landing was "left to the reservists and the Volkssturm" —
   Spiegel's mocking word for the home guard. The press could see
   the territorial-forces seam the after-action report then
   formalized (Q6's recommendation). Strengthens the
   known-problem-unfixed reading.
8. **Texture bank:** maneuver damage under 100 DM settled in cash
   on the spot; a Bundeswehr captain's envy of the British
   commander's craftsman calm ("we will never get there —
   enviable, to be like that") against the *innere Führung*
   soul-searching; the SPD press counting ten atomic strikes where
   the umpires logged sixteen.

## Verdict for the Q6 warrant

The article does not itself carry the OPCON recommendation (that
stays Bogason's after-action passage), but it hardens the frame:
the 1960 exercise was real, corps-scale, binational, and
English-commanded; the press could already see both the scripted
enemy and the home-guard gap; and DK's 23-year caveat keeps its
inverted force — the seam was visible in 1960, recommended-on in
the after-action, and still default-open in the 1970 orbat, which
is exactly the institutional non-fix canon depicts Witzel closing
ad hoc in week two.
