## 0. What this volume is (and isn't) for us

DIIS Bind 3 is a history of **threat *perception* and security
politics**, not a Danish order-of-battle. Danish force structure,
stocks, and mobilization clocks stay with the CIA papers and Davies
(already on the shelf). What Bind 3 uniquely supplies:

- **ch. 77** `FE's vurderinger af østlige kapabiliteter og intentioner`
  (pp. 425-467): the fullest available reconstruction of what FE told
  ~200 recipients (EOs) and ~80 (Truslen mod Danmark) about the Eastern
  threat, year by year — the blue G-2 record. **This is the Target-1
  core.**
- **ch. 82** `Vestlige vurderinger af dansk sikkerhedspolitik`
  (pp. 578-585 for defense): the allied (mostly US) verdict on Danish
  readiness and its **direct link to reinforcement commitments** —
  Target 3 + 4, and material the CIA papers don't give in the ally's
  own voice.
- **ch. 59, 62-72**: the 1983 crisis and fodnotepolitik — Target 2.
- **ch. 79** (Operation RJAN/Able Archer today-assessment) and **ch. 80**
  (near-area today-assessment) are for-us backfill; ch. 80 was already
  mined into `zealand-landing.md` (KK3 pp. 443-466, 537-554).

## 1. FE's conflict models — the warning-time treatment (Target 1)

The instrument treats warning time **W** as a first-class parameter.
Bind 3 gives FE's own preparation-time band, which is the vol-3
treatment of W (note: I did **not** find the "political warning
48h-8 days / tactical worst-case none" phrasing verbatim in vol 3 —
that band, cited in `zealand-landing.md`, is from KK2 / synthesis; what
vol 3 actually quantifies is *WP preparation time*, below).

**The Kystfront (Coastal Front) picture, 1980s** [F, DIIS ch. 77
p. 462, quoting *Truslen mod Danmark* 1984 pp. 127f/134 & 1988 p. 160]:
- **19-25 Eastern divisions** after mobilization, in **four armies**:
  5th GDR Army, 1st and 3rd Polish Armies, 11th Soviet Guards Army (from
  the Baltic MD). Split into two "operative groups" — one against
  N. Germany/Holland, one against Denmark. Plus ~1,100 aircraft and
  ~500 helicopters for the opening air offensive and support.
- **Priority:** the attack on N. Germany/Holland has priority over the
  Schleswig-Holstein/Jutland thrust — but the latter is still pressed
  hard enough to establish "temporary security." (Matches the
  Lautsch/oob-verification.md finding that our axis was the secondary
  effort; DIIS confirms it from the *blue* side.)

**The preparation-time scenarios (= FE's W)** [F, DIIS ch. 77
pp. 462-463]:
- Two archetypes: a **fast attack after ~1 week's preparation**, and a
  **later attack after 11-14 days' preparation**.
- **From 1985 FE judged 11-12 days' preparation the most likely** — "to
  achieve optimal balance between superiority and surprise"; DIIS notes
  FE never said what the shift rested on [*Truslen mod Danmark* 1985
  s. 141]. → **This is the money quote for W: the enemy's own optimum
  is ~11-12 days, not a bolt-from-the-blue.** (Post-setting for a Nov-83
  character; the pre-1985 picture is the 1-week/11-14-day pair.)
- Force-mix per scenario (character-relevant furniture for the LANDJUT
  axis; all *Truslen* 1984/1988, so for-us):
  - **1984 fast variant:** 5th GDR Army (+2 Polish divisions) drives to
    the **Kiel Canal**; 1-2 Soviet airborne regiments seize the Canal
    crossings; **2-3 fresh Polish divisions** continue up into Jutland.
  - Alt fast variant: 1st Polish Army (4 div) to the Canal, continuation
    by 2-3 GDR divisions.
  - **14-day variant:** main thrust by the 11th Soviet Guards Army moved
    from the Baltic MD — DIIS flags (FE did not) that most Baltic-MD
    divisions were **category C**: assemble and train up first [p. 463].
  - **1988:** a *pure Polish* attack was not excluded — 1st Polish Army
    initial, 3rd Polish Army follow-on, 5th GDR Army peeled off to
    N. Germany/Holland. **Only one scenario had the 5th GDR Army
    continuing up into Jutland.**
- GSST (Soviet forces in the GDR): **20 divisions ~350,000 men, able to
  go into combat "with short warning" (kort varsel)** [F, p. 440].
- 5th GDR Army (1988): **1 panzer + 4 motorized-infantry divisions**,
  within an 11-division/2-army GDR ground force (3 more divisions than
  in the 1970s). GDR **mobilization capacity larger than previously
  reckoned** — four NCO schools each raise a mobilization division +
  a fifth; **mobilization in 72 hours** [F, p. 442, *Truslen* 1988
  s. 44].

**FE paper Nr. 526/87 of 26 May 1987** to the Defense Ministry
(`Sammenfatning vedrørende "Truslen mod Danmark"`, **unclassified**)
[F, DIIS ch. 77 pp. 463-464 — full text quoted]. A darker picture: WP's
ability to execute its strategy "greatly increased over the past
decade"; the forces attacking S-H/Jutland "must be expected to have
greater offensive potential than before." Crucially it argued WP had
shifted the **overall strategy to put *more* weight on the north-German
front sector — and thereby Denmark**. The quoted logic is the
**North-Sea-corridor thesis**: *"it will be operational logic to place
the weight in those front sectors where the defense is expected to be
weak or where the distance to the strategic objective is shortest ...
the north-German sector will be exposed. If WAPA forces a breach and
reaches the German North Sea coast ... the north-south coherence of
NATO's defense will be broken. A corridor will have opened through
which especially air operations can be carried out in NATO's
rear."* DIIS ties this to US analyst **Phillip Petersen** and to NATO's
MC-131, and notes it drove Danish worst-case planning into the 1990s.
(This is the 526/87 paper flagged in `zealand-landing.md` — here is its
full provenance and text. **Period-dated 26 May 1987**, post-setting,
but the *reasoning* is exactly our v14 corridor premise stated in
period voice.)

**Blue's own dispositions, as DIIS states them** [F, ch. 77 p. 464,
from an April-2004 interview with the acting chief of the Danish
Division, Col. Vagn Holm Nielsen — a today-source describing period
doctrine, so [I] on the period detail]: the **Jutland Division's**
wartime task was a **rapid forward displacement to the inner-German
border at Lübeck** and, per NATO's forward strategy, a **strong
place-bound defense — "reminiscent of trench warfare" — in and west of
Lübeck**, on a carefully prepared system of defensive lines. The
**6th German Division** fought its defensive battle immediately to the
south. The Danish side **did not expect a main attack on the Danish
sector** and therefore **did not foresee fighting a strongly
numerically superior enemy**. → Squares with the 90-hour clock
(landjut-front.md) and the secondary-axis finding; note the *blue
self-image* was "we are not the Schwerpunkt," which the 526/87 paper
then tried to overturn.

**FE's standing probability judgment** [F, ch. 77 pp. 455-456, 466-467]:
through the 1980s FE judged the probability of a military conflict in
Europe **low** (superpowers won't risk escalation to nuclear war). From
the **1984** edition FE declared an **isolated WP attack on Denmark
alone "excluded" ("meget ringe")** — a reversal worth noting, since the
early-80s editions still carried the limited-war/political-pressure
case. The big offensive war was kept only as a **theoretical worst case
for NATO force-planning** — "we could not decisively change Danish
defense without an intelligence basis to do so" was the signal FE still
sent as late as 1990/91. → The documented **worst-case ratchet** from
`zealand-landing.md §"asymmetry"` is visible here in the aggregate: FE
never let the crisis-signs in the East restructure the planning
scenario.

## 2. The 1983 window (Target 2)

**FE's contemporaneous read of Able Archer was "routine."** [F, ch. 77
pp. 443-444, EO **November 1983**] FE's *only* periodic-material mention
of the Soviet reaction during Able Archer ran under the heading
"Increased electronic surveillance in connection with the NATO exercise
'Able Archer'" and consisted of a list of the Soviet/Polish/GDR/Czech
reconnaissance units deployed, plus the verdict: *"The scope of the
surveillance corresponded broadly to what is normally recognized in
connection with exercises, incl. the WINTEX series."* → **For a Nov-83
setting this is the single most useful item: the Danish service, in the
month itself, scored the Soviet posture as ordinary, WINTEX-level.** The
war-scare was invisible from Karup. (DIIS's 2005 verdict that Moscow was
genuinely frightened — ch. 59 pp. 16-18, ch. 79 — is for-us backfill;
the *gap between the two* is the story.)

**The three-event autumn 1983** [DIIS ch. 59 pp. 16-18, for-us]:
(1) the KAL 007 shoot-down (269 dead, 1 Sept 1983); (2) Soviet fear of
a Western first strike around Able Archer coinciding with the first INF
deployments; (3) the Soviet walkout from the INF and START talks. Marshal
Ogarkov's press conference cast the shoot-down as the answer to a
deliberate US provocation; Gromyko: Soviet borders "are sacred." The US
only grasped the depth of Moscow's alarm in **spring 1984**, via a
British (Gordievsky) assessment; Thatcher then worked to convince Reagan
the fear was real.

**Fodnotepolitik — the tally and the defense link** [F, ch. 70-71]:
- **9 footnotes, 10 reservations, 3 remarks**, all on the **double
  decision (INF) and SDI**; **3 footnotes + 3 remarks fell by end-1983**,
  the remaining 6 footnotes + 10 reservations in 1984-86; the last
  ("technically a reservation") in Dec 1986 [p. 232]. For scale: Greece
  footnoted more often; France/Spain/Norway one each 1983-86.
- The politics ran through a **"security-policy majority"** in the
  Folketing (S, RV, SF, VS) against the Schlüter four-leaf-clover
  government from 1982-88 — the government "chose to live with the
  footnotes" because economic policy and the Radical-Liberal cooperation
  took priority [summary ¶21].
- **The load-bearing link for our furniture:** the 1984 "omnibus"
  agenda demanded Denmark stay nuclear-free "in peace, crisis **and
  war**," and the four-year debate that followed turned precisely on
  *whether a no to nuclear weapons on Danish soil in peace/crisis/war
  could be reconciled with Denmark's NATO membership and **the
  reinforcement agreements** Denmark had signed* [F, ch. 71 pp. 261-263].
  DIIS states the nuclear-territory stance "would touch the precondition
  for the agreements on the unified command and reinforcements"
  [p. 273]. → **Footnote politics is not just diplomatic noise; it
  bears directly on whether nuclear-capable reinforcements can arrive —
  an Andon-cord-shaped input, same family as the mine-release decision.**
- Net consequence [summary ¶22]: footnotes had **no demonstrable impact**
  on international developments (Denmark lacked the NATO weight); some
  negative effect on Danish reputation, but no demonstrable security
  consequence. Defense consensus held: the traditional defense-agreement
  parties stayed united on effectiveness/infrastructure [summary ¶20].

## 3. NATO-ally verdict on Danish readiness + reinforcement (Targets 3, 4)

Chapter 82 (pp. 578-585) is the ally's own voice on Danish weakness —
the mirror of the CIA readiness caveats, and it names the reinforcement
consequences.

**The two-track duality made explicit** [F, ch. 82 p. 579]: ever-closer
integration (command structure, reinforcement agreements, infrastructure)
**vs.** the base/nuclear reservations *plus* chronically **funding
defense below what the main partners thought reasonable**.

**Pre-positioning / reinforcement furniture** [F, p. 579]:
- **COB agreements 1976 & 1979**: a total of **5 squadrons of US
  fighters** planned to deploy to **Jutland airfields in wartime**.
- **LOC agreement 1979**: storage of **ammunition and spare parts** for
  those US aircraft in Denmark.
  (Complements CIA-1989 App C's "~100 F-15/F-16 to three Danish airfields
  within 6 days" already in landjut-front.md — DIIS gives the *treaty*
  layer under it.)

**The 1980 Brown-Søgaard letters — reinforcement used as leverage**
[F, pp. 580-581, **period-dated Jan-May 1980**, made public in *Dansk
Udenrigspolitisk Årbog 1980*]: US SecDef Harold Brown's personal "Dear
Poul" letter (Jan 1980) criticized the Danish effort; a sharper second
letter **directly threatened that US reinforcements and materiel storage
would be held back** unless Denmark could be seen to be able to carry
out its wartime tasks. An April-1980 NSC memo to Brzezinski: the Danes
"are doing miserably on defense"; a no-real-growth program would mean
**eliminating a Zealand brigade, a fighter squadron, 12 warships and
4,000 military personnel** — "given Denmark's strategic position and
their already weak military forces, these cuts would be serious."

**Reinforcement actually withdrawn, 1981** [F, p. 582]: summer 1981 a
new draft of NATO's **"Rapid Reinforcement Plan" no longer allocated US
Army forces to the Northern Region**; per the head of the Pentagon's
NATO office George Bader, this was tied to the poor Danish effort —
"the grounds for earmarking forces and placing depots in Denmark were
not present." → **A concrete, period-dated reinforcement loss traceable
to Danish underfunding** — directly relevant to the US 9th ID / Northern
Region commitment question in landjut-front.md.

**The Sept 1985 Schlüter-Washington briefing note** [F, pp. 582-583,
period-dated Aug-Sept 1985] listing the allied indictment, verbatim
bullets:
- decline in strength across nearly all areas;
- "Danish defense suffers from **significant deficiencies that will
  limit our ability to withstand a Warsaw Pact attack and thereby hamper
  the arrival of reinforcements**";
- "external reinforcements will have **uncertain arrival conditions due
  to insufficient air defense and lack of minesweeping capacity**";
- a decade of insufficient resource allocation pushed the problems
  forward / left essential needs uncovered;
- lack of solidarity even where it costs nothing (the footnotes, the
  self-imposed Baltic restrictions).
  Reagan told Schlüter directly the US "was disturbed by developments in
  Denmark regarding defense." The pejorative **"danmarkisering"
  / "Denmarkization"** dates from the early 1980s [p. 583 n.13].

**The British angle (Target 4)** [F, p. 583 n.13, period-dated July
1984]: British statements to a senior Danish official — sharp criticism
of the Danish defense agreement and that **Denmark was not prepared to
create the necessary preconditions for *British* reinforcements**. →
Bears on the UKMF (UK 1st Inf Bde) arrival the OOB ledger tracks: the
host-nation-support preconditions were, in London's view, not being
laid. After 1985 the criticism faded as détente resumed [p. 584].

**The minesweeping gap** named by the allies (above) corroborates the
CIA-1984 "hollow minelaying capacity" finding and Bogason's
"political-will, not capability" split already triangulated in
`zealand-landing.md` — now a *third* headquarters (the allies briefing
Schlüter) says the reinforcement clock itself is hostage to the
air-defense + minesweeping shortfalls.

## 4. Extensions to the amphib/near-area threads (Target 5)

Mostly already in `zealand-landing.md` (ch. 80, pp. 443-466, 537-554).
New/sharper period-dated items from ch. 77:

- **U-137 → the nuclear revision** [F, p. 449-451, EO **Nov 1981**]: the
  grounded Whiskey almost certainly carried a nuclear-armed torpedo. FE
  revised its standing view: Soviet units on **readiness patrol in the
  Baltic carried nuclear weapons as standard**, and nuclear weapons would
  be "more widespread on Soviet units in the Baltic in a tension or war
  situation than previously assumed." → A pre-Nov-83 datum: by 1982 the
  Baltic maritime threat picture already had a nuclear layer.
- **Warning degraded by a stroke of the pen** [F, p. 448, EO **Jan
  1985**]: the GDR extended its territorial sea from 3 to 12 nm on
  1 Jan 1985, which FE said reduced Danish naval/air collection "to a
  significant degree" and **"degraded the warning possibilities"
  (varslingsmulighederne forringet)** — a rare explicit W-shrinking
  event, red-controlled and costless.
- **Air threats reaching Danish soil** [F, pp. 453-455, EOs 1985-88]:
  MiG-29 Fulcrum under an SA-5 umbrella (~250 km) could engage over
  southern Denmark at all altitudes ("Danish air operations will thereby
  be significantly hampered"); Su-27 Flanker escorts stood up in Poland;
  **Fitter-K jetbombers based at Laage (SE of Rostock, end 1985)** —
  FE: "a significant increase in the GDR navy's offensive potential" and
  "increased possibility of air support for amphibious operations in
  BALTAP's area"; **SA-10b Grumble SAM (5-130 km) reaching the southern
  edge of Copenhagen** (EO 12/88); Foxbat-F with 250 km missiles able to
  hit Danish radar stations from WP territory.
- **Ogarkov's lightning-war doctrine** [F, pp. 435-436] with helicopter
  forces at division level and air-assault units for depth operations —
  FE expected these against the **Kiel Canal, "in the rear of the
  Danish-German defense."** TVD command layer inserted early-80s;
  **Legnica (Poland)** = the operational-strategic HQ (TVD-West)
  coordinating fronts and the air offensive; FE expected BALTAP
  operations might be led from there [p. 436].
- **FE was "on the wrong track" on OMGs** [F, pp. 436-437]: 1983 heavy
  skepticism ("few indications"), 1988 "still developing" — yet GDR
  exercise material from the early 80s shows the OMG concept was live.
  A documented blue miss.
- **The mid-80s defensive inversion, seen but not internalized** [F,
  p. 452, *Truslen* 1988]: from 1988 FE assessed the Soviets were moving
  *away* from offensive maritime operations in the Baltic at the tactical
  level (uncertainty over whether Sweden/Finland could be kept from
  aiding NATO) — "a rather surprising assessment," given four decades of
  the offensive picture; advanced "without further proof." Another
  down-datum the worst-case machinery declined to act on.
- **Stockholm CSBM 1986** [F, ch. 81 pp. 569-570]: binding notification
  of large exercises, **up to ~2 years' notice for major maneuvers**,
  plus observers — "made it harder to carry out a surprise attack under
  cover of exercise activity" and reinforced the normal-picture baseline.
  → A W-lengthening institution, red *and* blue bound by it, dating to
  the back half of the setting decade.

## Consequences for the instrument

1. **W is bimodal in the sources, and the enemy's own optimum is
   long.** FE's preparation-time band is not a smear from 0 to N; it is
   two archetypes (a ~1-week fast attack, an 11-14-day deliberate one),
   and **from 1985 FE named 11-12 days as most likely — the WP's own
   "optimal balance of superiority and surprise"** (ch. 77 p. 462). If
   the instrument's W distribution is meant to reflect the *blue G-2
   prior*, it should be **bimodal with mass on a long (~11-12 day)
   deliberate mode**, not a short-warning default. The short-warning
   worst case is real but is the *tail*, not the center — and blue knew
   it. This is a direct, sourced calibration for the 90-hour-clock
   scenario parameter.
2. **W is a red-manipulable and treaty-bound quantity, both directions.**
   The GDR 12-nm extension (Jan 1985) *shortened* blue warning at no
   cost to red; the Stockholm CSBM (1986) *lengthened* it and bound both
   sides. If W is a scenario dial, these are two documented,
   period-dated levers for setting it — one a cheap red signal, one a
   structural floor. Consistent with the graded-credibility design in
   `zealand-landing.md §4`.
3. **The corridor premise has a period-voiced blue statement.** FE
   526/87's North-Sea-corridor logic (ch. 77 p. 463-464) is our v14
   corridor thesis in a contemporaneous, *unclassified* Danish document —
   usable as in-world furniture (a staff can hold this paper) and as
   external validation that the Jutland axis's *point* was the corridor,
   not ground seizure. But note it is **May 1987** and explicitly a
   *raised* worst case "from debatable premises" — for a Nov-83 setting
   the corridor thesis is not yet the house view; the house view is
   still "we are the secondary sector" (Holm Nielsen, ch. 77 p. 464).
4. **Reinforcement arrival is coupled to blue's own funding and to
   footnote politics — a second Andon-cord-shaped input.** Ch. 82 gives
   three sourced couplings: (a) the 1981 Rapid Reinforcement Plan
   *actually dropped* US Army forces from the Northern Region over
   Danish underfunding (p. 582); (b) the 1985 allied brief says
   reinforcement *arrival conditions* are hostage to Danish air-defense
   and minesweeping shortfalls (p. 583); (c) the no-nuclear-in-war
   footnote directly interrogated the reinforcement agreements' premise
   (ch. 71 p. 273). → If the model has a reinforcement clock (UKMF, US
   9th ID), its *availability* is not a fixed input; it is downstream of
   political-military decisions blue controls. This is the same shape as
   mine-release: a blue-owned prior on a red-facing outcome.
5. **The worst-case ratchet is confirmed at the aggregate.** FE saw
   every down-datum (GDR amphib training stopped, the 1988 defensive
   naval turn, the crisis-signs across the East) and let none restructure
   the planning scenario (ch. 77 pp. 452, 441, 466-467). This is not
   cynicism to model; it is the FE record, and it is what makes a red
   feint cheap — reaffirmed from a fresh chapter.

## Book-texture finds

- **The service's own voice.** FE published `Truslen mod Danmark`
  irregularly (~80 recipients) and monthly `Efterretningsoversigter`
  (~200 recipients); in Feb 1991 the flagship dropped the word "trussel"
  and became `Det efterretningsmæssige planlægningsgrundlag`. A staff
  officer's desk has *this month's EO* on it.
- **FE quoting the enemy admiral.** In 1989 FE reprinted Baltic Fleet
  commander Adm. Vitalij Ivanov (to *Marinerundschau*) conceding his own
  amphib forces were "**insufficient for effective** [offensive]
  **defense** ... military professionals must grasp that an offensive
  landing operation would require far stronger amphibious forces than our
  fleets possess" — and FE printed it "without real comment," then held
  its worst case anyway (ch. 77 p. 447). The enemy tells you he can't,
  and your G-2 files it and doesn't move.
- **"Danmarkisering."** A live period slur for the low-profile defense
  posture — usable in an allied officer's mouth (ch. 82 p. 583).
- **The GDR read Danish railway timetables.** GDR military intelligence
  based its Danish infrastructure assessments on **DSB's own
  publications** ("DSB 2000 — Moderne transport på skinner," 1988) and
  the planned Great Belt bridge (ch. 81 pp. 569-570). And a 1988 GDR
  observer (Oberst Teschner, Bereich Aufklärung) filed an "undramatic"
  report on the Danish exercise **BOLD GROUSE**, meeting FE officers "in
  a positive, détente-spirited atmosphere" — legalized mutual spying.
- **The "Dear Poul" letter.** A US SecDef writing a first-name personal
  letter that then escalates to a printed public threat to withhold
  reinforcements (ch. 82 pp. 580-581) — the texture of alliance pressure
  applied to a small ally, on the record.
- **Ivan Rogov leaves.** Nov 1986 the big landing ship departs the
  Baltic for the Pacific "too big and deep-drafted for the Baltic"; FE
  scored −15% Soviet Baltic amphib lift (ch. 77 p. 446) — already in
  `zealand-landing.md`, here confirmed to the page.
