### Who actually attacks Schleswig-Holstein (the mid-80s answer)

The controlling source is **Siegfried Lautsch**, head of the operations
department, Kommando MB V, **1983-86** — i.e., the man who wrote the
5th Army's plan in exactly our window — published as:

- Lautsch, *Kriegsschauplatz Deutschland. Erfahrungen und Erkenntnisse
  eines NVA-Offiziers* (ZMSBw, Potsdam 2013, ISBN 978-3-941571-28-0).
- Lautsch, "Die NVA-Operationsplanung für Norddeutschland 1983-1988",
  in Bange/Lemke (eds.), *Wege zur Wiedervereinigung. Die beiden
  deutschen Staaten in ihren Bündnissen 1970 bis 1990* (Oldenbourg
  2013), pp. 265-286.
  [Identified via https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1524/9783486855388.265/html
  and https://www.sehepunkte.de/2013/03/20113.html, fetched 2026-07-22]

Findings (each flagged with its transmission path):

- **The 5. Armee's own attack axis was SOUTH of the Elbe**, against
  I (NL) Corps on NORTHAG's northern flank: 1983 plan — day 1 destroy
  the Dutch covering forces and advance 50-60 km (direction
  Evendorf-Uelzen); day 2 Soviet airborne west of Aller/Weser; day 3
  further mechanized divisions over the Weser near Nienburg; final
  objective the **German-Dutch border near Ahaus** at 270-350 km
  depth in 5-7 days; **up to 32 nuclear strikes** in the army's
  initial nuclear salvo. [Lautsch interview, aixpaix.de,
  https://www.aixpaix.de/europa/lautsch-interview-20170408.html,
  fetched 2026-07-22 — interview with the planner himself, strong]
- **The Schleswig-Holstein/Jutland axis belonged to the KÜSTENFRONT
  (Coastal Front — the wartime Polish Front): three Polish armies
  plus one air army.** Its mission: **take Schleswig-Holstein and the
  Danish mainland, bypass Hamburg, advance to the German-Dutch
  border.** [Lautsch, *Kriegsschauplatz Deutschland*, as quoted in a
  Bundeswehrforum thread citing pp. 121, 124
  (https://bundeswehrforum.de/forum/index.php?topic=50905.35),
  fetched 2026-07-22 — **forum-transmitted, TO-VERIFY against the
  book itself; purchase candidate**]
- **The covering phase**: on the assumption NATO attacks first, **two
  divisions — the 8. MSD and the Soviet 94. Gds MSD — hold the
  Schleswig-Holstein border sector for about two days (to X+2)**
  while the Coastal Front completes its approach march from Poland
  and deploys through Mecklenburg; the 8. MSD then reverts to 5th
  Army second echelon (Nienburg direction). [same forum-transmitted
  Lautsch cite — TO-VERIFY, but consistent with everything else held]
- **Plan revisions**: 1983 (offensive as above) → **1985: defensive
  prioritization** — hold a Dassow-Lenzen line, objective the
  restoration of the status quo ante at the inner-German border,
  offensive action reduced to counter-strokes → **1988: strictly
  defensive** inside GDR territory, fortified positions, no border
  crossing. [German Wikipedia 5. Armee article + aixpaix interview +
  sehepunkte review of Wenzke (ed.), all fetched 2026-07-22]
  **A November-1986 setting sits on the 1985 plan of record** — the
  defense-first, counterattack-second shape, not the 1983 romp. This
  is now a setting-year-coupled fact, same class as the Zealand
  criterion (planning/setting-time.md criterion 6).
- **Polish Front building blocks** (wiki-grade): 1. Armia from the
  Pomeranian MD (Bydgoszcz, readiness A, 91,000 wartime), 2. Armia
  from the Silesian MD (Wrocław, B, 89,500), 4. Armia from the Warsaw
  MD (C, 64,700). [English Wikipedia, "List of Polish People's Army
  units", fetched 2026-07-22 — which armies stood in which echelon on
  the Jutland axis: open; Moszumański's chapter in the Wenzke volume
  is the named source to get]
- **Amphibious/Zealand piece** (cross-ref, already held): mid-80s the
  Polish landing task shrinks to Bornholm; Zealand passes to the
  Soviet 16th Army/Unified Baltic Fleet (May 1985/Nov 1986) — full
  distillation in reference/zealand-landing.md. The Moszumański and
  Szafran chapters in the Wenzke volume cover the Polish side of this
  same story. [sehepunkte review, fetched 2026-07-22]

### Revision of the standing assumption

The landjut-front.md standing assumption ("GDR + Polish first echelon
on the SH axis, Soviet follow-on") is **wrong in shape**. The mid-80s
architecture is:

1. **Covering force (X to ~X+2): one GDR division + one Soviet
   division** (8. MSD + 94. GdMSD) holding the border — thin, and
   deliberately so;
2. **Striking echelon (from ~X+2): a Polish FRONT** — three armies
   plus an air army — passing through Mecklenburg onto the
   SH/Jutland axis;
3. **Soviet weight on the axis**: the attached 94. GdMSD, the
   (post-1985) Zealand landing via 16th Army/UBF, and the Baltic
   Fleet — not a ground follow-on echelon behind the Poles;
4. The NVA 5th Army's main body fights a **different battle** south
   of the Elbe against I (NL) Corps.

### Period-knowable vs archive-revealed (book material)

- What a 1986 LANDJUT G-2 could know: the existence and garrisons of
  MB V's divisions, the Polish Pomeranian MD, GSFG's northern army —
  building blocks, not the plan. The held **CIA 1989** paper estimates
  "four divisions probably would attack the Jutland Peninsula and Fyn"
  [CIA reading room, https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/1989-04-01.pdf,
  on the shelf] — right order of magnitude for the first days,
  **structurally wrong**: it misses that the axis's real weight was a
  Polish front arriving from day 2-3, behind a two-division screen.
- Paired with the already-logged red mirror error (Soviets crediting
  BALTAP with a phantom British corps, CIA 1989 p.4): **both G-2s
  count the other side's divisions and miss the other side's plan.**
  That symmetry is the book's to use.
- Continuity check against the held 1960s material (1964 dossier,
  ex. "Storm" 1961, "Summer 67"): the Polish-front-on-the-Jutland-axis
  architecture is stable from the 1960s; what the mid-80s adds is the
  **defensive first phase** (1985) and the **Soviet takeover of
  Zealand** — the 1960s facsimiles are not simply obsolete, they are
  the offensive phase of a plan that grew a defensive preamble.
